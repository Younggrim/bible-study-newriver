1Now Aaron’s sons Nadab and Abihu took their censers, put fire in them and added incense, and offered unauthorized fire before the LORD, contrary to His command.

2So fire came out from the presence of the LORD and consumed them, and they died in the presence of the LORD.

3Then Moses said to Aaron, “This is what the LORD meant when He said: ‘To those who come near Me I will show My holiness, and in the sight of all the people I will reveal My glory.’” But Aaron remained silent.

4Moses summoned Mishael and Elzaphan, sons of Aaron’s uncle Uzziel, and said to them, “Come here; carry the bodies of your cousins outside the camp, away from the front of the sanctuary.”

5So they came forward and carried them, still in their tunics, outside the camp, as Moses had directed.

6Then Moses said to Aaron and his sons Eleazar and Ithamar, “Do not let your hair become disheveled and do not tear your garments, or else you will die, and the LORD will be angry with the whole congregation. But your brothers, the whole house of Israel, may mourn on account of the fire that the LORD has ignited.

7You shall not go outside the entrance to the Tent of Meeting, or you will die, for the LORD’s anointing oil is on you.” So they did as Moses instructed.

8Then the LORD said to Aaron,

9“You and your sons are not to drink wine or strong drink when you enter the Tent of Meeting, or else you will die; this is a permanent statute for the generations to come.

10You must distinguish between the holy and the common, between the clean and the unclean,

11so that you may teach the Israelites all the statutes that the LORD has given them through Moses.”

12And Moses said to Aaron and his remaining sons, Eleazar and Ithamar, “Take the grain offering that remains from the food offerings to the LORD and eat it without leaven beside the altar, because it is most holy.

13You shall eat it in a holy place, because it is your share and your sons’ share of the food offerings to the LORD; for this is what I have been commanded.

14And you and your sons and daughters may eat the breast of the wave offering and the thigh of the contribution in a ceremonially clean place, because these portions have been assigned to you and your children from the peace offerings of the sons of Israel.

15They are to bring the thigh of the contribution and the breast of the wave offering, together with the fat portions of the food offerings, to wave as a wave offering before the LORD. It will belong permanently to you and your children, as the LORD has commanded.”

16Later, Moses searched carefully for the goat of the sin offering, and behold, it had been burned up. He was angry with Eleazar and Ithamar, Aaron’s remaining sons, and asked,

17“Why didn’t you eat the sin offering in the holy place? For it is most holy; it was given to you to take away the guilt of the congregation by making atonement for them before the LORD.

18Since its blood was not brought inside the holy place, you should have eaten it in the sanctuary area, as I commanded.”

19But Aaron replied to Moses, “Behold, this very day they presented their sin offering and their burnt offering before the LORD. Since these things have happened to me, if I had eaten the sin offering today, would it have been acceptable in the sight of the LORD?”

20And when Moses heard this explanation, he was satisfied.

Summary

Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu offer unauthorized fire before the LORD that he had not commanded, and fire comes out from the LORD and kills them on the spot (vv. 1-2). Moses tells Aaron that this shows the LORD will be shown holy by those who come near him, and Aaron remains silent; Moses then has the bodies carried outside the camp and forbids Aaron and his remaining sons from any mourning rites, on pain of death, lest the LORD's anger fall on the whole congregation (vv. 3-7). The LORD tells Aaron that priests must never drink wine or strong drink when entering the Tent of Meeting, so that they can distinguish holy from common and clean from unclean, and can teach Israel the statutes (vv. 8-11). Moses instructs Aaron and his remaining sons on eating the remaining grain offering and the priests' portions of the peace offerings in a clean place (vv. 12-15). Moses is angry to learn that the goat of the people's sin offering was burned rather than eaten as commanded, but accepts Aaron's explanation that eating it that day, after the deaths of his sons, would not have pleased the LORD (vv. 16-20).

Authorship & Background

Author: Moses. Leviticus is the third book of the Pentateuch, written during the wilderness period (approximately 1445-1405 BC). Hebrew title: "Vayikra" — "And He called." God speaks from the newly completed Tabernacle, giving Israel instructions for worship, sacrifice, and holy living.
Historical Context: Chapter 10 is the immediate and devastating sequel to chapter 9. The fire that came from God to consume the offering in divine acceptance (9:24) now comes from God to consume two priests in divine judgment (10:2). The same fire that blesses obedience destroys presumption. Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's two eldest sons — newly consecrated priests — offer "strange fire" (unauthorized fire) before the LORD. They are killed instantly. This chapter establishes a principle that runs through all of Scripture: God is holy, and those who approach Him must do so on his terms, not their own. The chapter also reveals the cost of priestly service: Aaron is forbidden to mourn publicly, God speaks directly to Aaron (the only time in Leviticus outside of chapter 16) about sobriety, and the chapter ends with a dispute about proper handling of the sin offering — resolved by Aaron's grief-stricken but theologically sound reasoning.
The Sin of Nadab and Abihu (vv.1-3): Nadab and Abihu each take his censer, put fire in it, lay incense on it, and offer "strange fire" (KJV) / "unauthorized fire" (ESV) before the LORD — "which he commanded them not" (v.1). Fire comes out from before the LORD and devours them; they die before the LORD (v.2). Moses speaks to Aaron: "This is it that the LORD spake, saying, I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me, and before all the people I will be glorified" (v.3). Aaron's response: silence. "Aaron held his peace" (v.3). The nature of their sin: they offered what God had not commanded. The issue is not that they did something explicitly forbidden — it is that they innovated. They approached God on their own terms. The fire that consumed the offering (9:24) now consumes the offerers (10:2). Same source, opposite result. Obedience brings blessing; presumption brings death.
Carried Out in Their Coats (vv.4-7): Two cousins are called to carry the bodies out of the camp, and the detail the text keeps is that they were carried in their coats, still wearing the priestly tunics they died in. Then the hardest instruction in the book is given to their father: uncover not your heads, neither rend your clothes, lest ye die. Aaron and his surviving sons are forbidden the ordinary signs of grief, and the reason is that the anointing oil of the LORD is upon you. The mourning is delegated, but let your brethren, the whole house of Israel, bewail the burning. Israel may weep for them. Their father may not.
No Wine, and the Duty to Distinguish (vv.8-11): Do not drink wine nor strong drink, thou, nor thy sons with thee, when ye go into the tabernacle. The prohibition arrives immediately after the deaths, which has led readers for centuries to suspect what Nadab and Abihu had been doing. Then the reason, and it is the job description of a priest in two clauses: that ye may put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean, and that ye may teach the children of Israel all the statutes. Judgment and instruction, and neither survives drink.
Instructions for the Remaining Priests (vv.12-15): Moses instructs Aaron, Eleazar, and Ithamar regarding their priestly portions: the grain offering eaten unleavened beside the altar — "it is most holy" (v.12). Eaten in the holy place — their due (v.13). The wave breast and heave thigh eaten in a clean place — by the priests, their sons, and daughters (v.14). These are their perpetual due from the peace offerings (v.15). Even in the midst of tragedy, the priestly duties continue. God's service does not stop for personal grief.
The Dispute over the Sin Offering (vv.16-20): Moses searches for the goat of the sin offering and finds it has been burned up entirely (v.16). He is angry with Eleazar and Ithamar (v.16). His charge: "Why have ye not eaten the sin offering in the holy place?" — it was most holy, given to them to "bear the iniquity of the congregation, to make atonement for them" (v.17). The blood was not brought inside the sanctuary, so they should have eaten it (v.18). Aaron answers: they offered their sin offering and burnt offering today, "and such things have befallen me" — if he had eaten the sin offering today, "should it have been accepted in the sight of the LORD?" (v.19). Moses hears this and is satisfied (v.20). Aaron's reasoning: in his state of grief and defilement (his sons' sin and death), eating the holy offering would not have honored God. This is not disobedience — it is pastoral wisdom. Moses accepts it.

Commentary

  • Enduring Word (David Guzik): enduringword.com Guzik emphasizes that Nadab and Abihu's sin was offering what God had NOT commanded — the principle of worship by divine prescription, not human innovation. He notes the possible connection to intoxication (vv.8-9 placed immediately after their death). He highlights Aaron's silence as one of the most powerful moments in the Old Testament — a father who submits to God's justice even in the loss of his sons.
  • Jamieson-Fausset-Brown (1871): blueletterbible.org A critical and explanatory commentary on the whole Bible, more concise than Matthew Henry and closer to the Hebrew and Greek, with attention to historical and geographical detail. Public domain.
  • Matthew Henry's Exposition (1710): blueletterbible.org A devotional verse-by-verse commentary in the Puritan tradition, still among the most widely read three centuries on. Public domain.

Reflection

  • Unauthorized Worship (v.1) — Nadab and Abihu offered fire God had not commanded, not something explicitly forbidden but something they invented on their own. Are you approaching God on His terms, or bringing Him whatever feels right to you in the moment?
  • Greater Access Means Greater Accountability (v.3) — God declared He would be regarded as holy by those who come near Him, and the two priests closest to His presence paid the highest price for presuming otherwise. Does your reverence for God match how close He's let you come?
  • Sobriety and Discernment (v.9) — Immediately after this tragedy, God commanded the priests not to drink before entering His presence, so they could distinguish holy from common. What in your life dulls your ability to tell right from wrong when it matters most?
  • Wisdom in the Middle of Grief (v.19) — Aaron, in the aftermath of losing two sons, reasoned that eating the sin offering that day would not have honored God, and Moses accepted his judgment. Do you trust that God cares about the heart behind your obedience, especially in seasons of grief, more than a rigid checklist?
