1Soon afterward, Jesus traveled from one town and village to another, preaching and proclaiming the good news of the kingdom of God. The Twelve were with Him,

2as well as some women who had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities: Mary called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out,

3Joanna the wife of Herod’s household manager Chuza, Susanna, and many others. These women were ministering to them out of their own means.

4While a large crowd was gathering and people were coming to Jesus from town after town, He told them this parable:

5“A farmer went out to sow his seed. And as he was sowing, some seed fell along the path, where it was trampled, and the birds of the air devoured it.

6Some fell on rocky ground, and when it came up, the seedlings withered because they had no moisture.

7Other seed fell among thorns, which grew up with it and choked the seedlings.

8Still other seed fell on good soil, where it sprang up and produced a crop—a hundredfold.” As Jesus said this, He called out, “He who has ears to hear, let him hear.”

9Then His disciples asked Him what this parable meant.

10He replied, “The knowledge of the mysteries of the kingdom of God has been given to you, but to others I speak in parables, so that, ‘though seeing, they may not see; though hearing, they may not understand.’

11Now this is the meaning of the parable: The seed is the word of God.

12The seeds along the path are those who hear, but the devil comes and takes away the word from their hearts, so that they may not believe and be saved.

13The seeds on rocky ground are those who hear the word and receive it with joy, but they have no root. They believe for a season, but in the time of testing, they fall away.

14The seeds that fell among the thorns are those who hear, but as they go on their way, they are choked by the worries, riches, and pleasures of this life, and their fruit does not mature.

15But the seeds on good soil are those with a noble and good heart, who hear the word, cling to it, and by persevering produce a crop.

16No one lights a lamp and covers it with a jar or puts it under a bed. Instead, he sets it on a stand, so those who enter can see the light.

17For there is nothing hidden that will not be disclosed, and nothing concealed that will not be made known and brought to light.

18Pay attention, therefore, to how you listen. Whoever has will be given more, but whoever does not have, even what he thinks he has will be taken away from him.”

19Then Jesus’ mother and brothers came to see Him, but they were unable to reach Him because of the crowd.

20He was told, “Your mother and brothers are standing outside, wanting to see You.”

21But He replied, “My mother and brothers are those who hear the word of God and carry it out.”

22One day Jesus said to His disciples, “Let us cross to the other side of the lake.” So He got into a boat with them and set out.

23As they sailed, He fell asleep, and a windstorm came down on the lake, so that the boat was being swamped, and they were in great danger.

24The disciples went and woke Him, saying, “Master, Master, we are perishing!” Then Jesus got up and rebuked the wind and the raging waters, and they subsided, and all was calm.

25“Where is your faith?” He asked. Frightened and amazed, they asked one another, “Who is this? He commands even the winds and the water, and they obey Him!”

26Then they sailed to the region of the Gerasenes, across the lake from Galilee.

27When Jesus stepped ashore, He was met by a demon-possessed man from the town. For a long time this man had not worn clothing or lived in a house, but he stayed in the tombs.

28When the man saw Jesus, he cried out and fell down before Him, shouting in a loud voice, “What do You want with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I beg You not to torture me!”

29For Jesus had commanded the unclean spirit to come out of the man. Many times it had seized him, and though he was bound with chains and shackles, he had broken the chains and been driven by the demon into solitary places.

30“What is your name?” Jesus asked. “Legion,” he replied, because many demons had gone into him.

31And the demons kept begging Jesus not to order them to go into the Abyss.

32There on the hillside a large herd of pigs was feeding. So the demons begged Jesus to let them enter the pigs, and He gave them permission.

33Then the demons came out of the man and went into the pigs, and the herd rushed down the steep bank into the lake and was drowned.

34When those tending the pigs saw what had happened, they ran off and reported this in the town and countryside.

35So the people went out to see what had happened. They came to Jesus and found the man whom the demons had left, sitting at Jesus’ feet, clothed and in his right mind; and they were afraid.

36Meanwhile, those who had seen it reported how the demon-possessed man had been healed.

37Then all the people of the region of the Gerasenes asked Jesus to depart from them, because great fear had taken hold of them. So He got into the boat and started back.

38The man whom the demons had left begged to go with Jesus. But He sent him away, saying,

39“Return home and describe how much God has done for you.” So the man went away and proclaimed all over the town how much Jesus had done for him.

40When Jesus returned, the crowd welcomed Him, for they had all been waiting for Him.

41Just then a synagogue leader named Jairus came and fell at Jesus’ feet. He begged Him to come to his house,

42because his only daughter, who was about twelve, was dying. As Jesus went with him, the crowds pressed around Him,

43including a woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years. She had spent all her money on physicians, but no one was able to heal her.

44She came up behind Jesus and touched the fringe of His cloak, and immediately her bleeding stopped.

45“Who touched Me?” Jesus asked. But they all denied it. “Master,” said Peter, “the people are crowding and pressing against You.”

46But Jesus declared, “Someone touched Me, for I know that power has gone out from Me.”

47Then the woman, seeing that she could not escape notice, came trembling and fell down before Him. In the presence of all the people, she explained why she had touched Him and how she had immediately been healed.

48“Daughter,” said Jesus, “your faith has healed you. Go in peace.”

49While He was still speaking, someone arrived from the house of the synagogue leader. “Your daughter is dead,” he told Jairus. “Do not bother the Teacher anymore.”

50But Jesus overheard them and said to Jairus, “Do not be afraid; just believe, and she will be healed.”

51When He entered the house, He did not allow anyone to go in with Him except Peter, John, James, and the child’s father and mother.

52Meanwhile, everyone was weeping and mourning for her. But Jesus said, “Stop weeping; she is not dead but asleep.”

53And they laughed at Him, knowing that she was dead.

54But Jesus took her by the hand and called out, “Child, get up!”

55Her spirit returned, and at once she got up. And He directed that she be given something to eat.

56Her parents were astounded, but Jesus ordered them not to tell anyone what had happened.

1And it came to pass afterward, that he went throughout every city and village, preaching and shewing the glad tidings of the kingdom of God: and the twelve were with him,

2And certain women, which had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils,

3And Joanna the wife of Chuza Herod’s steward, and Susanna, and many others, which ministered unto him of their substance.

4And when much people were gathered together, and were come to him out of every city, he spake by a parable:

5A sower went out to sow his seed: and as he sowed, some fell by the way side; and it was trodden down, and the fowls of the air devoured it.

6And some fell upon a rock; and as soon as it was sprung up, it withered away, because it lacked moisture.

7And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprang up with it, and choked it.

8And other fell on good ground, and sprang up, and bare fruit an hundredfold. And when he had said these things, he cried, He that hath ears to hear, let him hear.

9And his disciples asked him, saying, What might this parable be?

10And he said, Unto you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of God: but to others in parables; that seeing they might not see, and hearing they might not understand.

11Now the parable is this: The seed is the word of God.

12Those by the way side are they that hear; then cometh the devil, and taketh away the word out of their hearts, lest they should believe and be saved.

13They on the rock are they, which, when they hear, receive the word with joy; and these have no root, which for a while believe, and in time of temptation fall away.

14And that which fell among thorns are they, which, when they have heard, go forth, and are choked with cares and riches and pleasures of this life, and bring no fruit to perfection.

15But that on the good ground are they, which in an honest and good heart, having heard the word, keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience.

16No man, when he hath lighted a candle, covereth it with a vessel, or putteth it under a bed; but setteth it on a candlestick, that they which enter in may see the light.

17For nothing is secret, that shall not be made manifest; neither any thing hid, that shall not be known and come abroad.

18Take heed therefore how ye hear: for whosoever hath, to him shall be given; and whosoever hath not, from him shall be taken even that which he seemeth to have.

19Then came to him his mother and his brethren, and could not come at him for the press.

20And it was told him by certain which said, Thy mother and thy brethren stand without, desiring to see thee.

21And he answered and said unto them, My mother and my brethren are these which hear the word of God, and do it.

22Now it came to pass on a certain day, that he went into a ship with his disciples: and he said unto them, Let us go over unto the other side of the lake. And they launched forth.

23But as they sailed he fell asleep: and there came down a storm of wind on the lake; and they were filled with water, and were in jeopardy.

24And they came to him, and awoke him, saying, Master, master, we perish. Then he arose, and rebuked the wind and the raging of the water: and they ceased, and there was a calm.

25And he said unto them, Where is your faith? And they being afraid wondered, saying one to another, What manner of man is this! for he commandeth even the winds and water, and they obey him.

26And they arrived at the country of the Gadarenes, which is over against Galilee.

27And when he went forth to land, there met him out of the city a certain man, which had devils long time, and ware no clothes, neither abode in any house, but in the tombs.

28When he saw Jesus, he cried out, and fell down before him, and with a loud voice said, What have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God most high? I beseech thee, torment me not.

29(For he had commanded the unclean spirit to come out of the man. For oftentimes it had caught him: and he was kept bound with chains and in fetters; and he brake the bands, and was driven of the devil into the wilderness.)

30And Jesus asked him, saying, What is thy name? And he said, Legion: because many devils were entered into him.

31And they besought him that he would not command them to go out into the deep.

32And there was there an herd of many swine feeding on the mountain: and they besought him that he would suffer them to enter into them. And he suffered them.

33Then went the devils out of the man, and entered into the swine: and the herd ran violently down a steep place into the lake, and were choked.

34When they that fed them saw what was done, they fled, and went and told it in the city and in the country.

35Then they went out to see what was done; and came to Jesus, and found the man, out of whom the devils were departed, sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind: and they were afraid.

36They also which saw it told them by what means he that was possessed of the devils was healed.

37Then the whole multitude of the country of the Gadarenes round about besought him to depart from them; for they were taken with great fear: and he went up into the ship, and returned back again.

38Now the man out of whom the devils were departed besought him that he might be with him: but Jesus sent him away, saying,

39Return to thine own house, and shew how great things God hath done unto thee. And he went his way, and published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him.

40And it came to pass, that, when Jesus was returned, the people gladly received him: for they were all waiting for him.

41And, behold, there came a man named Jairus, and he was a ruler of the synagogue: and he fell down at Jesus’ feet, and besought him that he would come into his house:

42For he had one only daughter, about twelve years of age, and she lay a dying. But as he went the people thronged him.

43And a woman having an issue of blood twelve years, which had spent all her living upon physicians, neither could be healed of any,

44Came behind him, and touched the border of his garment: and immediately her issue of blood stanched.

45And Jesus said, Who touched me? When all denied, Peter and they that were with him said, Master, the multitude throng thee and press thee, and sayest thou, Who touched me?

46And Jesus said, Somebody hath touched me: for I perceive that virtue is gone out of me.

47And when the woman saw that she was not hid, she came trembling, and falling down before him, she declared unto him before all the people for what cause she had touched him and how she was healed immediately.

48And he said unto her, Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace.

49While he yet spake, there cometh one from the ruler of the synagogue’s house, saying to him, Thy daughter is dead; trouble not the Master.

50But when Jesus heard it, he answered him, saying, Fear not: believe only, and she shall be made whole.

51And when he came into the house, he suffered no man to go in, save Peter, and James, and John, and the father and the mother of the maiden.

52And all wept, and bewailed her: but he said, Weep not; she is not dead, but sleepeth.

53And they laughed him to scorn, knowing that she was dead.

54And he put them all out, and took her by the hand, and called, saying, Maid, arise.

55And her spirit came again, and she arose straightway: and he commanded to give her meat.

56And her parents were astonished: but he charged them that they should tell no man what was done.

Summary

Jesus tells the parable of the sower, explaining that the seed is God's word and the soils represent different responses to it, and teaches that revealed truth is meant to be shared, not hidden (vv. 1-18). When his mother and brothers seek him, he redefines family as those who hear and do God's word (vv. 19-21). Crossing the lake, he calms a violent storm with a word, astonishing the disciples (vv. 22-25), then delivers a man possessed by a legion of demons, sending them into a herd of pigs that rushes into the water (vv. 26-39). Returning, he heals a woman who touches his cloak after twelve years of bleeding, commending her faith (vv. 40-48), and raises Jairus' twelve-year-old daughter from death, telling her parents she is only sleeping before taking her by the hand (vv. 49-56) — together showing his authority over nature, demons, sickness, and death.

Authorship & Background

Author: Luke (see Chapter 1 notes for full authorship details).
Historical Context: Chapter 8 demonstrates Jesus' authority over every domain: His word (the Parable of the Sower, vv.4-15), nature (calming the storm, vv.22-25), demons (the Gadarene demoniac, vv.26-39), disease (the woman with the issue of blood, vv.43-48), and death (raising Jairus' daughter, vv.49-56). Nothing is beyond His power.
Classification: Gospel — Narrative and Discourse
Key Themes: women named outright as financial supporters of the ministry, Mary Magdalene, Joanna and Susanna, a sower parable interpreted soil by soil in terms of what happens to the word rather than to the ground, a family unable to reach Jesus through the crowd answered with a redefinition of family itself, my mother and my brethren are these which hear the word of God, and do it, a storm calmed by two words followed by a question harder than the storm itself, where is your faith, a demon-possessed man named Legion healed and found sitting clothed and in his right mind while the townspeople ask Jesus to leave rather than stay, and two healings interleaved, a woman healed by a touch she cannot hide and a girl raised after a delay that seemed, for a moment, to have cost her life
The Women Who Followed (vv.1-3): Unique to Luke — he names women who supported Jesus' ministry financially: Mary Magdalene, Joanna (wife of Herod's steward), and Susanna. Luke consistently highlights women — marginalized in that culture — as full participants in Jesus' mission.
The Parable of the Sower (vv.4-15): The parable is told to a great crowd and explained only to the disciples, and Luke keeps the hard saying about why, unto others in parables, that seeing they might not see. The four soils are interpreted one by one, and Luke's version of each is about what happens to the word rather than to the ground: taken away by the devil, received with joy and no root, choked with cares and riches and pleasures of this life. The good ground is described with two words the others do not use, an honest and good heart, and one that is Luke's own emphasis, bring forth fruit with patience.
Nothing Hid That Shall Not Be Known (vv.16-21): A candle covered with a vessel or put under a bed is a picture of concealment that cannot last, for nothing is secret, that shall not be made manifest. Then a warning about hearing, take heed therefore how ye hear, and the measure that follows it. The paragraph ends with the family arriving and being unable to reach him for the crowd, and the answer given, my mother and my brethren are these which hear the word of God, and do it. Luke places it here so that hearing and doing is the thread joining all three parts.
Peace, Be Still (vv.22-25): He is asleep in the boat when the storm comes, and Luke says the boat was filling, they were in jeopardy. The disciples' cry is Master, master, we perish, and he rebukes the wind and the raging of the water. Then the question that is harder than the storm, where is your faith? and the reaction, which Luke records as fear rather than relief, they being afraid wondered, saying one to another, What manner of man is this! for he commandeth even the winds and water, and they obey him.
Legion, and the Swine (vv.26-39): The man is described before the exorcism in detail Luke does not spare: no clothes, no house, living in the tombs, kept bound with chains and fetters and breaking them. The name given is Legion, because many devils were entered into him. What they ask for is not to be sent into the deep, and what they get is the herd. Then the scene afterwards, and it is the reason the story is told: the townspeople find the man sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind, and they were afraid, and asked him to leave. The healed man asks to come with him and is refused, and given an errand instead, return to thine own house, and shew how great things God hath done unto thee.
The Woman and the Ruler's Daughter (vv.40-56): Two stories interleaved, and Luke the physician keeps the medical details. The woman had spent all her living upon physicians, neither could be healed of any, and the touch is of the border of his garment. The exchange that follows is about disclosure rather than power, somebody hath touched me, for I perceive that virtue is gone out of me, and she comes trembling and tells all the people why. The delay is what kills the girl, and the message arrives mid-conversation, thy daughter is dead, trouble not the Master. The reply is fear not, believe only. Then the room cleared, two words, Maid, arise, and the flattest possible follow-up instruction, he commanded to give her meat.
The Parable of the Sower: The seed is the Word of God (v.11). Four types of soil represent four responses to the Word: (1) hard path — the devil snatches it away, (2) rocky ground — initial joy but no root, (3) thorns — choked by cares, riches, and pleasures, (4) good soil — hearing, keeping, and bearing fruit with patience.
The Gadarene Demoniac: A man possessed by a "legion" of demons — naked, living among tombs, uncontrollable by chains. Jesus freed him completely. The man was found "sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind" (v.35). The townspeople asked Jesus to leave — they preferred their pigs to His power.

Map & Geography

  • The Sea of Galilee: Jesus and the disciples crossed the lake and encountered the storm (vv.22-25). They landed on the eastern shore in Gentile territory.
  • The country of the Gadarenes (v.26): The eastern shore of the Sea of Galilee, in the Decapolis — Gentile territory where the demoniac was healed.
  • Jairus' house (v.41): Back on the western shore, likely in Capernaum.

Deep Dive

Setting: About AD 29, in Galilee and across the lake. Chapter 7 ended at a Pharisee's dinner with a forgiven woman and the question "Who is this, who even forgives sins?" (7:49). Chapter 8 opens with Jesus on the road again, now with the Twelve and a group of women who support the work. The first half is about hearing: the parable of the sower, the lamp, and Jesus' true family, "those who hear the word of God and do it." The second half shows the power behind that word in four mighty works: over a storm, over a legion of demons, over twelve years of sickness and over death itself. Fear and faith run through every scene.

Women Accompanying Jesus (vv1–3)

  • "Through cities and villages, proclaiming and bringing the good news" (v1). This is a second tour of Galilee, now with the Twelve.
  • "Some women who had been healed" (v2). Rabbis did not normally travel with women disciples. Luke names women more than any other Gospel writer, and here they are part of Jesus' traveling company. Each had a story of healing or deliverance behind her.
  • "Mary, called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out" (v2). "Magdalene" means "from Magdala," a fishing town on the lake's west shore north of Tiberias, where a first-century synagogue was excavated in 2009. "Seven" suggests a severe bondage. Nothing in the text says she was a prostitute. That idea came centuries later from wrongly merging her with the woman of 7:37. She stays faithful to the end: at the cross, at the tomb and as the first witness of the risen Lord (24:10; John 20:1–18).
  • "Joanna, the wife of Chuza, Herod's household manager" (v3). Chuza managed the estates or household of Herod Antipas, the ruler who imprisoned John. So the gospel had reached into Herod's court, and a woman of standing chose to travel with a wandering teacher. Joanna is also at the empty tomb (24:10).
  • "Who provided for them out of their means" (v3). The Son of God, who fed thousands, lived on gifts from people he had helped. "Provided" is diakoneō, "to serve," the root of "deacon." Women were not on the edges of Jesus' ministry. They funded it.
  • Commentators: Henry: "The greatest of sinners must not despair of pardon; and the worse any have been before their conversion the more they should study to do for Christ after." JFB calls Jesus "the Prince of itinerant preachers," marvels that he lived on his people's gifts, and says "it is a great wrong to this honored woman" to identify Mary Magdalene with the sinner of 7:37. Guzik also warns against assuming her past was sexual sin, and the Tyndale notes say there is no basis for it.

The Parable of the Sower (vv4–8)

  • "A sower went out to sow his seed" (v5). Farmers scattered seed by hand as they walked, often before plowing it in. So some seed landed where no one meant it to go.
  • "Along the path… trampled underfoot" (v5). Footpaths ran through the fields. The soil there was packed hard, and the seed lay on top for birds to take.
  • "On the rock… because it had no moisture" (v6). Much of Galilee has a limestone shelf a few inches under the surface. Seed there sprouts quickly in the warm, thin soil, but its roots hit rock and it dries out.
  • "Among thorns" (v7). Thorn roots stay in the ground after the surface is cleared and grow back faster than the grain, crowding it out.
  • "Yielded a hundredfold" (v8). Matthew and Mark list thirtyfold, sixtyfold and a hundredfold. Luke gives only the highest.
  • Commentators: Henry draws from the path that "where the word of God is heard carelessly there is commonly a contempt put upon it too." Guzik prefers calling it the parable of the soils, since the seed and the sower never change. Only the ground differs. JFB refers the reader to its notes on Mark 4 and adds nothing here.

The Purpose of the Parables (vv9–10)

  • "To you it has been given to know the secrets of the kingdom of God" (v10). "Secrets" is mystēria, "mysteries": not riddles but truths that can be known only when God reveals them. "Has been given" is a gift. Disciples do not earn understanding by cleverness.
  • "So that 'seeing they may not see, and hearing they may not understand'" (v10). This is from Isaiah 6:9–10, God's commission to Isaiah to preach to a people who would not listen, a word of judgment. Parables work like a door. Those who come asking, as the disciples do here (v9), are let in. Those who are not interested hear a story and walk away unchanged, and their hardness is confirmed. The New Testament uses this text repeatedly to explain why many rejected Jesus (John 12:40; Acts 28:26–27). Luke's quotation is shorter than Mark's and leaves out the line about turning and being forgiven.
  • Commentators: Guzik describes parables as doorways that invite the interested to step through, and defines a mystery as truth known only by revelation.

The Parable of the Sower Explained (vv11–15)

  • "The seed is the word of God" (v11). The seed is always good, and the sower always sows. Every difference lies in how the word is heard. In Acts, the church grows as "the word of God increased" (Acts 6:7; 12:24).
  • "Then the devil comes and takes away the word… so that they may not believe and be saved" (v12). Only Luke adds "so that they may not believe and be saved." The enemy's goal is to stop faith before it starts. Hearing without receiving leaves the word lying on the surface.
  • "Receive it with joy. But these have no root" (v13). Joy is good, but emotion is not root. Luke says "in time of testing" where Mark has "tribulation or persecution." The word for testing, peirasmos, can mean trial or temptation. Under pressure, a faith with no depth falls away.
  • "Choked by the cares and riches and pleasures of life" (v14). Only Luke includes "pleasures." Cares are the worries of the poor, riches the snare of the wealthy, and pleasures a danger for anyone. None of these is dramatic sin. They simply crowd out the word over time, so "their fruit does not mature."
  • "Hold it fast in an honest and good heart, and bear fruit with patience" (v15). "Honest and good" (kalos kai agathos) was a Greek phrase for a person of fine character. Here it describes a heart opened by the word. "Hold it fast" means to keep, retain. "Patience" is hypomonē, steady endurance under pressure. Fruit takes time. Luke's sower ends with perseverance.
  • Which soil am I? Interpreters differ on whether the rocky and thorny ground pictures people never truly saved or believers who fall away. Reformed readers usually take the first view, pointing to the fact that only the good soil bears fruit (Matt 7:16–20; 1 John 2:19). Wesleyan and some other readers take the second, as a warning that faith can be lost. Both agree the parable calls hearers to examine themselves and keep the word.
  • Commentators: Guzik observes that the devil is always on time when the word is preached, and he reads the lack of moisture as a lack of the Spirit's work. He rejects a fatalistic reading. Hearts can be cultivated by turning from what hardens them. Henry stresses the need for "patience" and perseverance, since fruit does not come at once. JFB again refers to its notes on Mark.

A Lamp Under a Jar (vv16–18)

  • "No one after lighting a lamp covers it with a jar" (v16). A household lamp was a small clay dish of olive oil with a wick, set on a stand or in a wall niche to light the room. The secrets just given to the disciples (v10) are not meant to stay secret. They are a light meant to shine "so that those who enter may see."
  • "Nothing is hidden that will not be made manifest" (v17). What is now told privately will one day be known by all. This is a promise that the gospel will spread and a warning that every heart will be shown for what it is.
  • "Take care then how you hear" (v18). Matthew and Mark say "what you hear." Luke stresses "how." The way the word is received decides what happens next.
  • "To the one who has, more will be given" (v18). This is a rule of spiritual growth. Those who take in the word and hold it receive more. Those who do not lose even "what he thinks that he has," a religious knowledge that was never really theirs.
  • Commentators: Henry: "Ministers and Christians are to be lights in the world, holding forth the word of life." JFB calls v18 "very weighty" and explains the last clause as what a person has "hanging loosely on him, and not appropriated." Guzik makes a similar point, that what someone seems to have can be an illusion, and stresses that receiving truth brings the duty to share it.

Jesus' Mother and Brothers (vv19–21)

  • "His mother and his brothers came to him" (v19). Mark places this scene just after his family tried to "seize him" because people said he was out of his mind (Mark 3:21, 31–35). Luke tells it more gently.
  • "His brothers." The Gospels name four brothers, James, Joses, Judas and Simon, and mention sisters (Mark 6:3). Most Protestants take them as younger children of Joseph and Mary. Eastern Orthodox tradition holds they were Joseph's children from an earlier marriage. Roman Catholic tradition, following Jerome, holds they were cousins. Both of the latter views protect a belief in Mary's lifelong virginity. The Greek word adelphos normally means a full or half brother but can stretch to close relatives.
  • "My mother and my brothers are those who hear the word of God and do it" (v21). Jesus is not rejecting his family. Mary herself is Luke's model of one who hears and keeps God's word (1:38; 2:19, 51). He is saying that the closest bond with him comes through hearing and doing, and that door is open to anyone. Two of these brothers, James and Jude, later believed and wrote New Testament letters (Acts 1:14; 1 Cor 15:7).
  • Commentators: Guzik reads the brothers as literal siblings, arguing against perpetual virginity. The Tyndale notes favor the same view while giving the Orthodox alternative. Henry stresses the honor Christ gives to those who obey the word, calling them his nearest relations.

Jesus Calms a Storm (vv22–25)

  • "Let us go across to the other side of the lake" (v22). Jesus gave them a destination. That promise matters later in the story.
  • "As they sailed he fell asleep" (v23). This is the only time the Gospels say Jesus slept. He was truly human and truly tired. His sleep in the storm is also the picture of trust from Psalm 4:8: "In peace I will both lie down and sleep."
  • "A windstorm came down on the lake" (v23). The lake lies about 700 feet below sea level, ringed by hills. Cool air from the heights can pour down the ravines and whip the water into violent waves in minutes. "Came down" fits that exactly. "They were filling with water" is literally "they were being swamped." These were experienced fishermen, and they knew they could die.
  • "Master, Master, we are perishing!" (v24). The doubled name shows panic.
  • "He awoke and rebuked the wind and the raging waves" (v24). Jesus "rebukes" the storm the way he rebuked a demon (4:35) and a fever (4:39). In the Old Testament, stilling the sea belongs to God (Ps 89:9; 107:25–30, "he made the storm be still"). Jonah slept in a storm because he was running from God. Jesus sleeps in one as Lord of it.
  • "Where is your faith?" (v25). Luke's version is gentler than Mark's "Have you still no faith?" They did have faith, enough to wake him. But they had forgotten his word: they were going to the other side.
  • "Who then is this, that he commands even winds and water, and they obey him?" (v25). Their fear moves from the storm to the one in the boat.
  • Commentators: Henry: "Many that have true faith have it to seek when they have occasion to use it." Guzik notes that the disciples were in the storm because they obeyed, unlike Jonah, and suggests Jesus' question pointed to his promise to reach the other side. JFB notes only that "filling" means the boat was taking on water.

Jesus Heals a Man with a Demon (vv26–39)

  • "The country of the Gerasenes, which is opposite Galilee" (v26). This is the east shore, Gentile land in the Decapolis, the "Ten Cities" with Greek culture. Manuscripts differ among "Gerasenes," "Gadarenes" and "Gergesenes," and the Gospels vary too. Gerasa was some 30 miles from the lake and Gadara about 6, so the names probably describe the wider district. Many place the scene near modern Kursi, where steep slopes come down close to the water.
  • "For a long time he had worn no clothes… not lived in a house but among the tombs" (v27). Tombs were cut into the hillsides. To a Jew they were unclean, the haunt of the dead. Naked, homeless, among the dead, he was as cut off as a person could be.
  • "What have you to do with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God?" (v28). The demons know exactly who Jesus is. "Most High God" was a title Gentiles often used for Israel's God (Gen 14:18–20; Dan 3:26). Their knowledge does not save them (James 2:19).
  • "Bound with chains and shackles, but he would break the bonds" (v29). People had tried to control him. No one could. Only Jesus sets him free.
  • "What is your name?… 'Legion'" (v30). A Roman legion held up to 6,000 soldiers. The name may be a boast meant to intimidate, or it may express how many were tormenting him.
  • "Not to command them to depart into the abyss" (v31). The abyss (abyssos) is the prison of evil spirits (Rev 9:1–2; 20:1–3). They know a day of judgment is coming (Matt 8:29, "before the time").
  • "A large herd of pigs… rushed down the steep bank into the lake and drowned" (vv32–33). Pigs were unclean under the law, which marks this as Gentile territory. Why did Jesus allow it? The text does not say. Many see a visible proof that the demons had really left, and a picture of what they intended for the man: destruction. A man is worth more than a herd of pigs.
  • "Sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed and in his right mind" (v35). Every detail reverses verse 27. "At the feet" is the place of a disciple (10:39). "In his right mind" is sōphroneō, to be of sound mind, self-controlled.
  • "They were afraid… asked him to depart" (vv35, 37). They were not afraid of the madman, whom they had lived with for years. They were afraid of the one who set him free. They also counted the cost of the lost pigs. They choose their old life over the power of God among them.
  • "Return to your home, and declare how much God has done for you" (v39). He begs to go with Jesus. Jesus says no, and makes him the first missionary to the Decapolis. Notice the wording: Jesus says "how much God has done," and the man tells "how much Jesus had done." Luke lets the two stand side by side.
  • Commentators: Henry: "Those lose their Saviour, and their hopes in him, that love their swine better," and on v35, "If God has possession of us, he preserves to us the government and enjoyment of ourselves." Guzik reads "Legion" as an attempt to intimidate Jesus, notes that the demons' correct theology is not saving faith, and suggests that sending the man home taught him to trust Jesus without his physical presence. JFB only refers to Matthew and Mark.

Jesus Heals a Woman and Jairus's Daughter (vv40–56)

  • "The crowd welcomed him, for they were all waiting for him" (v40). One shore sent him away. This one welcomes him.
  • "A man named Jairus, who was a ruler of the synagogue" (v41). A synagogue ruler was a respected lay leader who arranged services and looked after the building. For him to fall at Jesus' feet in public cost him something, since some synagogue leaders were already opposed to Jesus.
  • "An only daughter, about twelve years of age" (v42). At twelve a Jewish girl was on the edge of womanhood, nearly old enough to be betrothed. Luke links this story to the next with the number twelve: a girl who had lived twelve years, and a woman who had suffered twelve years.
  • "A discharge of blood for twelve years" (v43). Probably a chronic menstrual bleeding. Under Leviticus 15:25–30 it made her ritually unclean, and anyone she touched was unclean too. For twelve years she had been excluded from worship and kept at arm's length by everyone around her.
  • "Though she had spent all her living on physicians" (v43). Some important early manuscripts lack this phrase, and the ESV footnotes it. Mark 5:26 says bluntly that she "was no better but rather grew worse." If Luke wrote it, the doctor tells the story more kindly to his profession.
  • "Touched the fringe of his garment" (v44). The "fringe" is probably one of the tassels (kraspedon) that the law told Israelite men to wear on the corners of their cloak as a reminder of God's commands (Num 15:38–40). Her touch should have made him unclean. Instead his cleanness flows into her.
  • "Who was it that touched me?" (v45). Peter thinks it a strange question, with crowds crushing him on every side.
  • "Power has gone out from me" (v46). This healing was not a magic trick drawn from his clothing. Jesus knew, and he willed it.
  • "She came trembling… declared in the presence of all the people" (v47). Why make her speak? She would have gone away healed but hiding, perhaps thinking she had stolen a cure. Now her healing is public, so her community knows she is clean. And she meets Jesus face to face.
  • "Daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace" (v48). This is the only time in the Gospels Jesus calls someone "daughter." "Made you well" is sōzō, the same word as "saved" in 7:50. It covers both the body's healing and the soul's rescue.
  • "Your daughter is dead; do not trouble the Teacher any more" (v49). While Jesus stopped for one woman, another life ran out. The delay that healed one seemed to cost the other.
  • "Do not fear; only believe, and she will be well" (v50). "Only believe" is the shortest sermon in Luke. Fear and faith are set against each other, as in the storm.
  • "Peter and John and James, and the father and mother" (v51). These three form an inner circle, present again at the Transfiguration (9:28) and in Gethsemane (Mark 14:33).
  • "She is not dead but sleeping… they laughed at him" (vv52–53). Jesus does not deny she died. Luke says her spirit returned (v55). He means that for him, death is like sleep, something from which he wakes people. The New Testament often speaks of believers' death as sleep (John 11:11–14; 1 Thess 4:13–14). The mourners, likely including professional wailers, laugh.
  • "Taking her by the hand he called, saying, 'Child, arise'" (v54). Jesus touches a corpse, which by law should defile him, and gives life instead.
  • "He directed that something should be given her to eat" (v55). She is truly alive and hungry.
  • Commentators: JFB notes that among the throng, the question is "rather ask who touched Thee not." It says "the voluntary, living contact of faith" is what alone draws healing from him, and that making her speak was a "great trial to the shrinking modesty of the believing woman," yet he meant it for her good. Henry: "There may be trembling where yet there is saving faith," and on v50, "our faith in Christ should be bold and daring." On v40 JFB adds that "the tide of His popularity was now fast rising." Guzik contrasts the crowd's casual contact with a touch of faith, and lists reasons Jesus called her forward, including assurance for her and encouragement for Jairus as he waited.

Pointing to Christ

Luke 8 shows the word of God and the Lord who speaks it. The seed is the word, and everything turns on how it is heard. Then the one who sows that word commands the storm, as only God does in the Psalms. He walks into the tombs to free a man no chains could hold. He lets an unclean woman touch him and calls her "daughter." He takes a dead girl's hand and says "Child, arise." Unclean, demonized, sick and dead, every barrier gives way before him. The chapter keeps asking "Who then is this?" The answer is the Son of the Most High God, who will one day go into death himself and come out again, so that everyone who trusts him can hear "Do not fear; only believe."

Reflection

  • What Soil Is Your Heart? (vv.5-15) — The Parable of the Sower names four responses to God's Word: indifference, shallow enthusiasm, competing priorities, and a heart that hears, keeps, and bears fruit. Which soil most honestly describes your heart right now, especially when it comes to what crowds out the Word?
  • Take Heed How You Hear (v.18) — Jesus warns that the posture behind our hearing matters as much as the hearing itself. Do you tend to receive God's Word with an open, obedient heart, or do you filter it for whatever is comfortable?
  • Where Is Your Faith? (v.25) — Jesus asked this question in a boat during a real, dangerous storm, not as a rebuke of fear itself but as a call to trust Him in the middle of it. What storm are you in right now, and where is your faith actually anchored?
  • No One Too Far Gone (vv.35-39) — The man once called Legion was found clothed and in his right mind, then sent home to tell everyone what God had done for him. Who have you written off as beyond hope, and is there a story of what God has done in you that someone needs to hear?
  • Desperate Enough to Reach (vv.43-48) — After twelve years and every resource exhausted, the woman with the issue of blood finally reached for Jesus. Has God ever let you run out of other options so you'd finally turn fully to Him?
  • Fear Not, Believe Only (vv.49-50) — When Jairus was told his situation was hopeless, Jesus told him to keep believing instead of giving up. In what current circumstance are you being tempted to stop believing, and what would it look like to hold on anyway?
