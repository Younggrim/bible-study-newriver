1These were the divisions of the descendants of Aaron. The sons of Aaron were Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.

2But Nadab and Abihu died before their father did, and they had no sons; so Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests.

3With the help of Eleazar’s descendant Zadok and Ithamar’s descendant Ahimelech, David divided them according to the offices of their service.

4Since more leaders were found among Eleazar’s descendants than those of Ithamar, they were divided accordingly. There were sixteen heads of families from the descendants of Eleazar and eight from the descendants of Ithamar.

Summary

Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu die without children before their father, leaving Eleazar and Ithamar to serve as priests (vv. 1-2). David, together with Zadok from Eleazar's line and Ahimelech from Ithamar's, organizes the priesthood into twenty-four courses for their appointed service; because more leading men are found among Eleazar's descendants, sixteen heads of families are drawn from his line and eight from Ithamar's, with the order determined by casting lots and recorded by Shemaiah the scribe (vv. 3-6). The twenty-four lots are then listed by name, beginning with Jehoiarib and Jedaiah and continuing through Harim, Malchijah, Abijah, Jeshua, Immer, and the rest, establishing the fixed rotation by which each family enters the house of the LORD to serve (vv. 7-19). The chapter closes with a further list of the remaining Levites - descendants of Kohath and Merari - who likewise cast lots for their duties in the presence of David, Zadok, Ahimelech, and the heads of the priestly and Levitical families, just as their kinsmen the sons of Aaron had done (vv. 20-31).

Authorship & Background

Author: Traditionally attributed to Ezra the scribe. Originally one book with 2 Chronicles. Written post-exile (approximately 450-400 BC) for the returned remnant. The Chronicler retells Israel's history from a priestly/worship perspective, emphasizing the Davidic covenant, Temple worship, and God's faithfulness.
Historical Context: Chapter 24 records David's organization of the Aaronic priesthood into 24 rotating divisions (or "courses") for service in the future Temple. This was a crucial administrative achievement — with the number of priests having grown substantially since Aaron's time, a system was needed to ensure orderly, continuous worship without overwhelming the sanctuary or leaving priests idle. David worked with Zadok (from the line of Eleazar) and Ahimelech (from the line of Ithamar) to establish these divisions by sacred lot, ensuring God's sovereignty determined the order rather than human preference or politics. The system endured for centuries — it was still functioning in the New Testament era (Luke 1:5 identifies Zechariah, father of John the Baptist, as belonging to the "course of Abijah," the eighth division listed here in v.10).
Classification: Historical Narrative
Key Themes: Two of Aaron's four sons removed from the record in the first two verses for dying without children, a rota drawn by lot specifically to forestall any dispute over precedence, twenty-four courses established that were still running a thousand years later when Zacharias served in Luke's Gospel, seniority explicitly set aside so no elder son received a better rotation than a younger, and the same lot-casting method applied to every Levite family, principal fathers treated the same as their younger brethren
The Twenty-Four Courses of the Priests (vv.1-19): Aaron's four sons are named and two of them removed in the first two verses, Nadab and Abihu died before their father, and had no children. The rota is drawn from the remaining two houses and the method is stated to forestall any dispute: thus were they divided by lot, one sort with another, and the lots are cast in the presence of David, Zadok, Ahimelech and the chief fathers. Twenty-four names in order, and the eighth is Abijah, which is the course Zacharias belonged to in Luke 1. This rota was still running a thousand years later.
The Rest of the Sons of Levi (vv.20-31): The non-priestly Levites are then given the same treatment, family by family, and the closing verse repeats the fairness clause with one addition, these likewise cast lots over against their brethren the sons of Aaron, the principal fathers even as their younger brethren. Seniority is explicitly set aside in the drawing. The register is at pains to record that nobody was given a better rota because of his standing.

Map & Geography

  • Largely parallels 1-2 Samuel geographically. Jerusalem (Zion/City of David) is the theological and political center.
  • Key sites: Hebron (David's first capital, 7 years), the threshing floor of Ornan/Araunah (future Temple site, ch.21).
  • Chapters 1-9 (genealogies) contain tribal allotments but minimal narrative geography.

Commentary

  • Enduring Word (David Guzik): enduringword.com Guzik notes that this system of 24 priestly courses meant each division served approximately two weeks per year (plus the three major festivals when all served). He emphasizes that the use of lots removed human politics from sacred service, and highlights the remarkable endurance of this system through the New Testament era, connecting it directly to Zechariah's service in Luke 1.
  • Jamieson-Fausset-Brown (1871): blueletterbible.org A critical and explanatory commentary on the whole Bible, more concise than Matthew Henry and closer to the Hebrew and Greek, with attention to historical and geographical detail. Public domain.
  • Matthew Henry's Exposition (1710): blueletterbible.org A devotional verse-by-verse commentary in the Puritan tradition, still among the most widely read three centuries on. Public domain.

Reflection

  • Order in Worship (vv.1-6) — David organizes the descendants of Aaron into twenty-four rotating divisions rather than leaving priestly service to chance or convenience. Is your approach to worship, personal or corporate, thoughtfully prepared, or left to whatever feels easiest in the moment?
  • Qualification Comes From God (v.3) — David works with Zadok and Ahimelech to assign priestly roles by God's prescribed method rather than by self-appointment. Are you waiting on God's calling and the recognition of others for your ministry, or grasping for positions on your own?
  • Impartiality Reflects God's Character (v.5) — The priestly divisions are assigned by lot so that no family can claim superiority over another. Where might politics, popularity, or privilege be shaping how ministry gets assigned in your own church or community instead of God's leading?
  • Faithful Planning Outlasts You (v.10) — The eighth division, assigned to Abijah's family, endured so long that it is still functioning centuries later when Zechariah, father of John the Baptist, serves in it. What patterns are you establishing today that could still be bearing fruit generations from now?
  • Every Family Matters (vv.20-31) — The chapter extends its careful organization beyond the prominent priestly line to every remaining Levitical family. Do you treat faithfulness in an obscure role as being just as valuable as faithfulness in a visible one?
