1Then the LORD said to Moses, “Speak to Aaron’s sons, the priests, and tell them that a priest is not to defile himself for a dead person among his people,

2except for his immediate family—his mother, father, son, daughter, or brother,

3or his unmarried sister who is near to him, since she has no husband.

4He is not to defile himself for those related to him by marriage, and so profane himself.

5Priests must not make bald spots on their heads, shave off the edges of their beards, or make cuts in their bodies.

6They must be holy to their God and not profane the name of their God. Because they present to the LORD the food offerings, the food of their God, they must be holy.

7A priest must not marry a woman defiled by prostitution or divorced by her husband, for the priest is holy to his God.

8You are to regard him as holy, since he presents the food of your God. He shall be holy to you, because I the LORD am holy—I who set you apart.

9If a priest’s daughter defiles herself by prostituting herself, she profanes her father; she must be burned in the fire.

10The priest who is highest among his brothers, who has had the anointing oil poured on his head and has been ordained to wear the priestly garments, must not let his hair hang loose or tear his garments.

11He must not go near any dead body; he must not defile himself, even for his father or mother.

12He must not leave or desecrate the sanctuary of his God, for the consecration of the anointing oil of his God is on him. I am the LORD.

13The woman he marries must be a virgin.

14He is not to marry a widow, a divorced woman, or one defiled by prostitution. He is to marry a virgin from his own people,

15so that he does not defile his offspring among his people, for I am the LORD who sanctifies him.”

16Then the LORD said to Moses,

17“Say to Aaron, ‘For the generations to come, none of your descendants who has a physical defect may approach to offer the food of his God.

18No man who has any defect may approach—no man who is blind, lame, disfigured, or deformed;

19no man who has a broken foot or hand,

20or who is a hunchback or dwarf, or who has an eye defect, a festering rash, scabs, or a crushed testicle.

21No descendant of Aaron the priest who has a defect shall approach to present the food offerings to the LORD. Since he has a defect, he is not to come near to offer the food of his God.

22He may eat the most holy food of his God as well as the holy food,

23but because he has a defect, he must not go near the veil or approach the altar, so as not to desecrate My sanctuaries. For I am the LORD who sanctifies them.’”

Summary

The Lord instructs that ordinary priests may defile themselves for the death of only their closest relatives, and even then must not adopt pagan mourning practices like shaving the head or cutting the flesh, since they are holy to God (vv. 1-6). Priests may not marry a prostitute or a divorced woman, and a priest's daughter who becomes a prostitute is to be burned (vv. 7-9). The high priest is held to an even higher standard: he must never disorder his hair, tear his clothes, or go near a dead body, even for his own parents, and he must marry only a virgin from his own people (vv. 10-15). Finally, no descendant of Aaron who has a physical defect — blindness, lameness, deformity, or similar conditions — may approach the altar to offer sacrifices, though he may still eat the priestly food, so that the sanctuary is not profaned (vv. 16-24).

Authorship & Background

Author: Moses. Leviticus is the third book of the Pentateuch, written during the wilderness period (approximately 1445-1405 BC). Hebrew title: "Vayikra" — "And He called." God speaks from the newly completed Tabernacle, giving Israel instructions for worship, sacrifice, and holy living.
Historical Context: Chapter 21 shifts focus from the holiness required of ALL Israel (chapters 18-20) to the HIGHER holiness required of priests. If all Israel must be holy, the priests must be holier still — and the high priest holiest of all. The chapter establishes a graduated scale of holiness: common Israelite → priest → high priest. Each level has stricter requirements. The logic is proximity to God: the closer one serves to God's presence, the higher the standard. Priests handle holy things, offer God's "bread" (sacrifices), and mediate between God and the people. Their lives must reflect the holiness of the One they serve. The chapter addresses three areas: (1) restrictions on mourning and defilement by the dead (vv.1-6, 10-12); (2) restrictions on marriage (vv.7-8, 13-15); and (3) restrictions based on physical defects (vv.16-24). These regulations are not about the personal worth of the priest but about the symbolic integrity of the office. The priest represents God to the people — his life and person must visibly reflect God's wholeness and holiness. This chapter points forward to Christ, our perfect High Priest — without blemish, without defilement, wholly consecrated to God (Hebrews 7:26).
Priests and Defilement by the Dead (vv.1-6): God speaks to Moses for "the priests the sons of Aaron" (v.1). A priest "shall none be defiled for the dead among his people" (v.1) — contact with a corpse makes one unclean for seven days (Numbers 19:11). Exceptions: immediate family — mother, father, son, daughter, brother (v.2), and a virgin sister who has no husband (v.3). For these closest relatives, a priest may defile himself. But he "shall not defile himself, being a chief man among his people" (v.4) — his position limits his mourning. Priests must not make bald patches on their heads, shave the edges of their beards, or cut their flesh (v.5) — these were pagan mourning customs. "They shall be holy unto their God, and not profane the name of their God" (v.6). The reason: "the offerings of the LORD made by fire, and the bread of their God, they do offer: therefore they shall be holy" (v.6). Their function demands their holiness.
Priestly Marriage Restrictions (vv.7-9): A priest shall not marry a prostitute, a defiled woman, or a divorced woman (v.7). "For he is holy unto his God" (v.7). The congregation must "sanctify him" — treat him as holy (v.8). "He shall be holy unto thee: for I the LORD, which sanctify you, am holy" (v.8). A priest's daughter who becomes a prostitute "profaneth her father" — she is burned with fire (v.9). The priest's family reflects on his office.
The High Priest — Highest Standard (vv.10-15): "He that is the high priest among his brethren" — the one anointed and consecrated to wear the garments (v.10). He shall NOT uncover his head (let hair hang loose) or tear his clothes (v.10) — the normal signs of mourning are forbidden to him entirely. He shall not go near ANY dead body — not even his father or mother (v.11). He shall not leave the sanctuary (v.12). "The crown of the anointing oil of his God is upon him" (v.12) — his consecration overrides all other obligations. Marriage: he must take a virgin (v.13). Not a widow, divorced woman, defiled woman, or prostitute — only "a virgin of his own people" (v.14). The purpose: "that he may not profane his seed among his people" (v.15). The high priest's family line must be pure. He represents the highest standard of holiness in Israel.
Physical Blemishes and Priestly Service (vv.16-24): God speaks to Moses for Aaron (v.16). No descendant of Aaron with a physical blemish may "approach to offer the bread of his God" (v.17). The list: blind, lame, mutilated face, limb too long (v.18), injured foot or hand (v.19), hunchback, dwarf, eye defect, skin disease, scabs, crushed testicles (v.20). No blemished priest may "come nigh to offer the offerings of the LORD made by fire" (v.21). However — he MAY eat the priestly food, "both of the most holy, and of the holy" (v.22). He retains his priestly identity and provision — he simply cannot serve at the altar or enter beyond the veil (v.23). "That he profane not my sanctuaries: for I the LORD do sanctify them" (v.23). Moses communicates this to Aaron, his sons, and all Israel (v.24).

Reflection

  • Greater Access, Greater Responsibility (vv.1-4) — Priests face stricter rules about contact with the dead than ordinary Israelites because their role requires a higher standard. If God has given you spiritual responsibility over others, are you holding yourself to the standard that position requires?
  • Consecration Above Even Family Grief (v.11) — The high priest is forbidden to defile himself even for his own mother or father, since his consecration overrides every other claim. Is there any relationship or obligation you currently place ahead of your devotion to God?
  • Provided For, Even When Set Aside From Service (v.22) — A priest with a physical blemish cannot serve at the altar, but he is not cut off — he still eats from the priestly provision. When you feel disqualified from serving in some way, do you remember God still provides for and values you?
  • God Sanctifies What He Sets Apart (v.23) — Both the priests and the sanctuary are made holy by God's own action, not merely by human effort. Are you trying to manufacture your own holiness instead of cooperating with the God who is already doing that work in you?
