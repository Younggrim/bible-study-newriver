1Jacob also went on his way, and the angels of God met him.

2When Jacob saw them, he said, “This is the camp of God.” So he named that place Mahanaim.

3Jacob sent messengers ahead of him to his brother Esau in the land of Seir, the country of Edom.

4He instructed them, “You are to say to my master Esau, ‘Your servant Jacob says: I have been staying with Laban and have remained there until now.

5I have oxen, donkeys, flocks, menservants, and maidservants. I have sent this message to inform my master, so that I may find favor in your sight.’”

6When the messengers returned to Jacob, they said, “We went to your brother Esau, and now he is coming to meet you—he and four hundred men with him.”

7In great fear and distress, Jacob divided his people into two camps, as well as the flocks and herds and camels.

8He thought, “If Esau comes and attacks one camp, then the other camp can escape.”

9Then Jacob declared, “O God of my father Abraham, God of my father Isaac, the LORD who told me, ‘Go back to your country and to your kindred, and I will make you prosper,’

10I am unworthy of all the kindness and faithfulness You have shown Your servant. Indeed, with only my staff I came across the Jordan, but now I have become two camps.

11Please deliver me from the hand of my brother Esau, for I am afraid that he may come and attack me and the mothers and children with me.

12But You have said, ‘I will surely make you prosper, and I will make your offspring like the sand of the sea, too numerous to count.’”

13Jacob spent the night there, and from what he had brought with him, he selected a gift for his brother Esau:

14200 female goats, 20 male goats, 200 ewes, 20 rams,

1530 milk camels with their young, 40 cows, 10 bulls, 20 female donkeys, and 10 male donkeys.

16He entrusted them to his servants in separate herds and told them, “Go on ahead of me, and keep some distance between the herds.”

17He instructed the one in the lead, “When my brother Esau meets you and asks, ‘To whom do you belong, where are you going, and whose animals are these before you?’

18then you are to say, ‘They belong to your servant Jacob. They are a gift, sent to my lord Esau. And behold, Jacob is behind us.’”

19He also instructed the second, the third, and all those following behind the herds: “When you meet Esau, you are to say the same thing to him.

20You are also to say, ‘Look, your servant Jacob is right behind us.’” For he thought, “I will appease Esau with the gift that is going before me. After that I can face him, and perhaps he will accept me.”

21So Jacob’s gifts went on before him, while he spent the night in the camp.

22During the night Jacob got up and took his two wives, his two maidservants, and his eleven sons, and crossed the ford of the Jabbok.

23He took them and sent them across the stream, along with all his possessions.

24So Jacob was left all alone, and there a man wrestled with him until daybreak.

25When the man saw that he could not overpower Jacob, he struck the socket of Jacob’s hip and dislocated it as they wrestled.

26Then the man said, “Let me go, for it is daybreak.” But Jacob replied, “I will not let you go unless you bless me.”

27“What is your name?” the man asked. “Jacob,” he replied.

28Then the man said, “Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel, because you have struggled with God and with men, and you have prevailed.”

29And Jacob requested, “Please tell me your name.” But he replied, “Why do you ask my name?” Then he blessed Jacob there.

30So Jacob named the place Peniel, saying, “Indeed, I have seen God face to face, and yet my life was spared.”

31The sun rose above him as he passed by Penuel, and he was limping because of his hip.

32Therefore to this day the Israelites do not eat the tendon attached to the socket of the hip, because the socket of Jacob’s hip was struck near that tendon.

Summary

As Jacob approaches Canaan, angels of God meet him, and he sends messengers ahead to Esau, only to learn his brother is approaching with four hundred men, leaving Jacob greatly afraid and dividing his camp for safety (vv. 1-8). He prays for deliverance and sends generous gifts of livestock ahead to appease Esau (vv. 9-21). That night, after sending his family across the Jabbok river, Jacob is left alone and wrestles with a man until daybreak (vv. 22-24). The man dislocates Jacob's hip yet cannot overpower him, and when Jacob refuses to let go without a blessing, the man renames him Israel, signifying that he has contended with God and with men and prevailed (vv. 25-29). Jacob names the place Peniel, recognizing he has seen God face to face and survived, and walks away limping (vv. 30-32).

Authorship & Background

Author: Moses (see Chapter 1 notes for full authorship details).
Historical Context: Chapter 32 is one of the most dramatic and transformative chapters in Genesis — Jacob wrestling with God at Peniel. After 20 years away, Jacob returns to face Esau (and his 400 men). Terrified, he prays, plans, and then encounters God in a nightlong wrestling match. The result: a dislocated hip, a new name (Israel), and a transformed man. The schemer becomes the prince. The heel-grabber becomes the God-wrestler. This is Jacob's conversion moment — where self-reliance is broken and dependence on God begins.
Classification: Historical Narrative
Key Themes: angels meeting Jacob at the very moment he is about to face the brother he wronged twenty years earlier, a report that Esau is approaching with four hundred men driving Jacob to divide his own camp in fear, one of Scripture's finest prayers built on God's own command, Jacob's confessed unworthiness and a direct claim on God's promise, waves of gifts sent ahead in careful stages to appease a brother who has not yet spoken a word, and a nightlong wrestling match that leaves Jacob limping away with a new name, Israel, and a hip permanently marked by the encounter
Angels at Mahanaim (vv.1-2): Angels meet Jacob — "God's host" (v.2). He names the place Mahanaim ("two camps") — God's camp alongside his own.
Fear of Esau (vv.3-8): Jacob sends messengers to Esau (vv.3-5). Report: Esau is coming with 400 men (v.6). Jacob is "greatly afraid" — divides his company into two groups (vv.7-8).
Jacob's Prayer (vv.9-12): One of the finest prayers in Scripture. He appeals to God's command (v.9), confesses unworthiness (v.10), asks for deliverance (v.11), and claims God's promise (v.12). Humble, specific, faith-filled.
The Gifts for Esau (vv.13-23): Jacob sends waves of gifts ahead — 550+ animals (vv.14-15). Strategy: "I will appease him" (v.20). He sends his family across the Jabbok (vv.22-23).
Wrestling with God (vv.24-32): "Jacob was left alone" (v.24). A "man" wrestles him until dawn (v.24). The man touches Jacob's hip — dislocated (v.25). Jacob: "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me" (v.26). "What is thy name?" — "Jacob" (supplanter) (v.27). New name: "Israel" — "for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men" (v.28). Jacob names the place Peniel — "I have seen God face to face" (v.30). He limps away at sunrise (v.31).

Map & Geography

  • The Jabbok River (v.22) — a tributary of the Jordan River (modern Zarqa River in Jordan). Jacob crosses it heading westward into Canaan.
  • Peniel / Penuel (v.30) — meaning "face of God," located near the Jabbok. The site of Jacob's wrestling with God. He names it because "I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved."
  • Mahanaim (v.2) — meaning "two camps," east of the Jordan. Where angels meet Jacob at the border of the Promised Land.

Reflection

  • Fear That Drives to Strategy (v.7) — Word that Esau is coming with 400 men leaves Jacob terrified, and he immediately starts dividing and planning. When fear hits you, is your first move to plan your way out or to bring it honestly to God?
  • A Prayer of True Humility (v.10) — Jacob confesses he's unworthy of even the smallest of God's kindnesses shown to him. Does your own prayer life reflect genuine humility, or do you tend to approach God assuming you deserve His help?
  • Left Alone to Wrestle (v.24) — Stripped of his family, servants, and possessions for the night, Jacob is left utterly alone when the encounter with God begins. Are you willing to be alone with God, without the usual distractions and supports you lean on?
  • Refusing to Let Go (v.26) — Even injured, Jacob clings to the man and demands a blessing before he'll release him. Do you pray with that kind of desperate persistence, or do you give up on God too easily?
  • A New Name, A New Identity (v.28) — Jacob's name changes from the one who grasps and schemes to one who has struggled with God and men. What would it look like for you to let go of your old identity and live out of the new one God has given you?
  • A Permanent Limp (v.31) — Jacob walks away from the encounter marked for life by the fight. Are you willing to let an encounter with God leave a lasting mark on you, even if it costs you something?
