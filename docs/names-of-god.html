Names of God

Every name of God reveals another facet of who He is — Creator, Provider, Healer, Shepherd, Father.

Introduction

Overview

In Hebrew thought, a name was never a mere label — it revealed character, nature, and reputation. When Scripture gives God a name, it is unveiling something true about who He is, often in response to a specific human need or crisis. Across the Old and New Testaments, God progressively reveals Himself through names that build on one another: the mighty Creator (Elohim), the covenant-keeping I AM (Yahweh), the sovereign Master (Adonai), the all-sufficient Almighty (El Shaddai), and dozens more — each one a facet of the same infinite God, given at the moment His people needed to know Him that way.

Many of these names were born in the crucible of real human experience: Hagar named God "the One who sees me" while fleeing into the wilderness; Abraham called Him "the LORD will provide" on the mountain where his son's life hung in the balance; Gideon named an altar "the LORD is peace" while trembling in fear; Moses learned the tetragrammaton at a burning bush that would not be consumed. God's names are not abstract theology — they are testimonies, each one a stone of remembrance marking a place where God met someone in their deepest need.

Together these sixteen names paint a composite portrait of God as Creator and Sustainer, Warrior and Peacemaker, Judge and Healer, sovereign King and intimate Father. To know His names is to know Him more fully — and to discover that whatever season we are walking through, there is a name of God that speaks directly into it.

God the Creator

Elohim

Understanding Elohim

Elohim is the very first name for God that appears in Scripture, introduced in the opening verse of the Bible: "In the beginning, God (Elohim) created the heavens and the earth" (Genesis 1:1). The Hebrew word is grammatically plural, yet it is consistently used with singular verbs when referring to the one true God — a "plural of majesty" that hints at the fullness of God's nature and foreshadows the triune nature revealed more fully in the New Testament.

The root of Elohim is "El," meaning strength, might, or power. As Elohim, God is presented as the supreme Creator who spoke the universe into existence by the sheer authority of His word. He is not a distant clockmaker but an intimately involved architect who designed every star, every creature, and every human soul with intentionality and purpose.

Throughout the Old Testament, Elohim appears over 2,500 times, making it the most frequently used name for God in the Hebrew Scriptures — the name associated with God's role as sovereign judge, mighty warrior, and faithful Creator.

What This Name Means for Us

Knowing God as Elohim means we serve a Creator who is infinitely powerful and purposeful. Nothing in our lives is accidental or beyond His ability to redeem. The same God who spoke galaxies into existence is the God who knit us together in our mother's womb, and who continues to create new hearts, new beginnings, and new possibilities in the lives of those who trust Him.

When we feel small or insignificant, the name Elohim reminds us that we were fashioned by the most powerful being in existence. When we face problems that seem insurmountable, we can remember that our God creates ex nihilo — something out of nothing — and specializes in bringing order out of chaos.

The LORD — "I AM WHO I AM"

Yahweh

Understanding Yahweh

Yahweh is the most sacred and personal name of God in the Hebrew Bible, revealed to Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3:14 when God declared, "I AM WHO I AM." The Hebrew tetragrammaton YHWH was considered so holy that ancient Israelites would not pronounce it aloud, substituting "Adonai" in its place. This name appears over 6,800 times in the Old Testament — the most frequently used proper name for God.

The name is derived from the Hebrew verb "hayah," meaning "to be" or "to exist." It communicates God's self-existence — He is not contingent on anything or anyone else for His being. He is the uncaused cause, the eternal present, existence itself.

Yahweh is distinctly the covenant name of God — the name by which He bound Himself to Israel in faithful, steadfast love. It is intimate, personal, and relational: not a distant philosophical concept but a God who draws near, speaks, and acts on behalf of those He loves.

What This Name Means for Us

Knowing God as Yahweh means we are in relationship with a God who is eternally faithful. His covenant promises do not expire, and His character does not change. In a world of shifting circumstances and broken promises, Yahweh stands as the one constant — the "I AM" who is the same yesterday, today, and forever.

The name Yahweh also invites us into intimacy. He revealed His personal name because He desires to be known. When we call upon Yahweh, we are calling upon the God who stooped down to speak to a fugitive shepherd at a burning bush and who continues to hear the prayers of His people today.

Lord, Master

Adonai

Understanding Adonai

Adonai is the Hebrew word meaning "Lord" or "Master," speaking of God's absolute sovereignty and authority over all things. It is the plural form of "Adon," intensified to express supreme lordship — the One who has the right to command, direct, and govern every aspect of creation and human life.

This name became especially significant because devout Jews would say "Adonai" whenever they encountered the tetragrammaton in Scripture. When Isaiah beheld the Lord "high and lifted up" in his temple vision, the word used is Adonai — the sovereign Master seated upon His throne in unapproachable majesty.

To call God "Adonai" is to acknowledge total submission. Yet unlike earthly masters, Adonai rules with perfect wisdom, boundless love, and infinite justice. His lordship is not oppressive but liberating.

What This Name Means for Us

Calling God "Adonai" is an act of surrender — acknowledging that our lives are not our own. In a culture that celebrates autonomy, this name challenges us to release the illusion of control and place our trust in the One whose plans are always higher and better than our own.

Yet surrendering to Adonai is the truest freedom. When we submit to His lordship, we are freed from the tyranny of our own limited wisdom and the anxiety of carrying burdens too heavy for us. His authority is perfect love in action.

God Almighty

El Shaddai

Understanding El Shaddai

El Shaddai, translated "God Almighty," is the name God used when He appeared to Abraham in Genesis 17:1, saying, "I am God Almighty; walk before me faithfully and be blameless." Scholars debate the exact etymology — mountain (immovable strength), breast (nourishment and sufficiency), or a root meaning "to overpower." Together these paint a picture of a God who is both overwhelmingly powerful and tenderly sufficient.

This name appears most frequently in the book of Job and the patriarchal narratives of Genesis. When God called Himself El Shaddai, He was assuring Abraham that His power was more than adequate to fulfill seemingly impossible promises.

El Shaddai speaks of God's all-sufficiency: He is enough power for any obstacle, enough provision for any need, enough comfort for any sorrow, and enough grace for any failure.

What This Name Means for Us

When we face situations that seem impossible, El Shaddai reminds us that our God specializes in the impossible. There is no limitation, no obstacle, and no impossibility that can exhaust His almighty power or deplete His all-sufficient resources.

El Shaddai also invites us to rest rather than strive. We do not need to generate our own sufficiency — He is sufficient for us. In our weakness, His power is made perfect.

God Most High

El Elyon

Understanding El Elyon

El Elyon, meaning "God Most High," first appears in Genesis 14:18-20 when Melchizedek blessed Abraham "by God Most High, Creator of heaven and earth." This name declares God's supreme position above all other powers, authorities, and spiritual beings.

The title comes from a Hebrew root meaning "to go up" or "to ascend." When Nebuchadnezzar was humbled by God and restored to sanity, he declared: "I praised the Most High; I honored and glorified him who lives forever" — even the most powerful earthly ruler had to acknowledge El Elyon's unchallenged supremacy.

By revealing Himself as El Elyon, God declared that He is not merely the God of Israel but the supreme God over all nations. His sovereignty is not regional or tribal — it is universal and absolute.

What This Name Means for Us

Knowing God as El Elyon means that no power in heaven or on earth can override His purposes for our lives. When circumstances spiral beyond our control, El Elyon sits enthroned above it all, working all things according to the counsel of His will.

This name also gives proper perspective. When we are tempted to elevate career, relationships, or reputation to the highest place, El Elyon reminds us that only God belongs on the throne.

The LORD Will Provide

Jehovah Jireh

Understanding Jehovah Jireh

Jehovah Jireh — "The LORD Will Provide" — is the name Abraham gave to the place on Mount Moriah where God provided a ram as a substitute sacrifice for his son Isaac (Genesis 22:14). The Hebrew word "jireh" means "to see" or "to provide," carrying the implication that God sees our needs before we express them.

God tested Abraham by commanding him to sacrifice his only son. Abraham obeyed in faith, even telling Isaac, "God himself will provide the lamb." At the very moment of sacrifice, God stayed Abraham's hand and revealed a ram caught in a thicket — His provision perfectly timed.

This name foreshadows God's ultimate provision: His own Son, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world. Just as God provided a substitute for Isaac on Mount Moriah, so He provided His Son as our substitute on a cross just outside Jerusalem.

What This Name Means for Us

Jehovah Jireh assures us that God is never caught off guard by our needs. He sees the trial before it arrives and has already prepared His provision. He often provides at the last moment, not because He is slow, but because He is teaching us to trust.

This name also challenges anxiety and self-reliance. If Jehovah Jireh has promised to provide, then our worry is ultimately a question of faith: do we believe He will do what He has promised?

The LORD Who Heals

Jehovah Rapha

Understanding Jehovah Rapha

Jehovah Rapha — "The LORD Who Heals" — is revealed in Exodus 15:26, at the waters of Marah, which were bitter and undrinkable. God made the waters sweet and declared: "I am the LORD who heals you." The Hebrew word "rapha" encompasses physical healing, emotional restoration, and spiritual renewal.

Israel had just witnessed God's miraculous deliverance at the Red Sea, yet three days later they grumbled over bitter water. God's response was not anger but healing — a pattern that runs throughout Scripture: in our moments of bitterness and brokenness, God meets us with restoration.

Jehovah Rapha's healing extends beyond physical ailments. Isaiah prophesied that the Messiah would be "pierced for our transgressions" and that "by his wounds we are healed." In Jesus, Jehovah Rapha's healing ministry reaches its fullest expression.

What This Name Means for Us

In a world full of brokenness, Jehovah Rapha offers hope that wholeness is possible. He does not always heal in the way or timing we expect, but He is always at work restoring what sin and suffering have damaged.

Knowing God as Jehovah Rapha gives us permission to bring our wounds to Him honestly. The God who turned bitter water sweet invites us to bring our bitterest experiences to Him and trust Him to bring healing.

The LORD Our Banner

Jehovah Nissi

Understanding Jehovah Nissi

Jehovah Nissi — "The LORD Is My Banner" — is the name Moses gave to the altar he built after Israel's victory over the Amalekites at Rephidim (Exodus 17:15). A banner served as a rallying point for troops — a visible sign of identity and the cause for which they fought. By naming the altar Jehovah Nissi, Moses declared that God Himself was Israel's rallying point.

The battle was won not by military strategy but by divine power: as long as Moses held up his hands, Israel prevailed. Aaron and Hur held up Moses' hands until sunset, and Israel won. The victory belonged entirely to God.

A banner also signifies love and belonging: "His banner over me is love" (Song of Solomon 2:4). We march under His banner — identified with Him, protected by Him, and fighting for His cause.

What This Name Means for Us

Jehovah Nissi reminds us that the spiritual battles we face are not ours to win alone. Whether we struggle against temptation, opposition, or discouragement, we need only lift our eyes to Him and rally under His banner.

This name also speaks to our identity. In a world offering countless banners to march under, Jehovah Nissi calls us to pledge allegiance to Him alone, assured that ultimate victory is certain because He has already overcome the world.

The LORD Is Peace

Jehovah Shalom

Understanding Jehovah Shalom

Jehovah Shalom — "The LORD Is Peace" — is the name Gideon gave to the altar he built after the Angel of the LORD said, "Peace! Do not be afraid" (Judges 6:23-24). The Hebrew word "shalom" means far more than the absence of conflict — completeness, wholeness, harmony, and well-being in every dimension of life.

Israel was being oppressed by the Midianites, their crops destroyed. Into this chaos, God revealed Himself as Shalom — not because circumstances had changed, but because His presence transforms any situation.

Jesus — the Prince of Peace — said, "Peace I leave with you; my peace I give you. I do not give to you as the world gives." The world offers temporary truces. Jehovah Shalom offers a peace that guards hearts and minds.

What This Name Means for Us

In a world drowning in anxiety, Jehovah Shalom offers what nothing else can: a peace not contingent on circumstances. It requires only that we fix our minds on the One who IS peace.

Jehovah Shalom also calls us to be peacemakers — carrying His peace into our homes, workplaces, and communities, and pursuing reconciliation where there is division.

The God Who Sees Me

El Roi

Understanding El Roi

El Roi — "The God Who Sees Me" — is the only name for God given by a woman in Scripture. Hagar, mistreated and fleeing into the wilderness, was met by the Angel of the LORD beside a spring of water (Genesis 16:13). Alone, pregnant, and rejected, Hagar encountered a God who saw her in her desperation.

The Hebrew word "roi" describes an active, compassionate gaze — the kind that leads to action. El Roi does not merely observe our suffering; He sees with the intent to intervene. Hagar's amazed response was: "You are the God who sees me."

This name reveals that no one is invisible to God. Hagar was a slave with no advocate. Yet the God of the universe stooped down to see her in the wilderness. El Roi sees the forgotten, the marginalized, and the overlooked.

What This Name Means for Us

In moments when we feel unseen, El Roi reminds us that God's eyes are fixed on us. He sees the tears we cry alone, the sacrifices no one else notices, and the silent battles we fight without applause.

El Roi also brings both comfort and accountability. Living before El Roi means living transparently, finding freedom in vulnerability rather than fear — the God who sees everything still loves us completely.

LORD of Hosts

Yahweh Sabaoth

Understanding Yahweh Sabaoth

Yahweh Sabaoth — "The LORD of Hosts" or "The LORD of Armies" — is one of the most frequently used divine titles in the Old Testament prophets, appearing over 260 times. "Sabaoth" means armies or hosts, referring to the vast angelic armies of heaven and all the forces of creation that stand under God's command.

This name first appears prominently in 1 Samuel, when young David facing Goliath declared: "I come against you in the name of the LORD Almighty, the God of the armies of Israel." While Goliath trusted in sword and spear, David trusted in the name that commands legions of angels.

Isaiah's vision captures this name's majesty: the seraphim ceaselessly cry, "Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory." This name reveals God as the King of an infinite kingdom, surrounded by innumerable hosts who worship Him unceasingly.

What This Name Means for Us

Knowing God as Yahweh Sabaoth means we never face our battles alone. When Elisha's servant was terrified by a surrounding enemy army, God opened his eyes to see "the hills full of horses and chariots of fire." The forces arrayed for us always outnumber the forces arrayed against us.

This name also puts our enemies in proper perspective. Whatever threatens us is nothing compared to the LORD of Hosts, who commands the stars and before whom all other powers bow.

Father

Abba

Understanding Abba

Abba is an Aramaic word meaning "Father" — but the intimate, affectionate term a child uses, closer to "Daddy" or "Papa." Jesus used this word most poignantly in Gethsemane, crying "Abba, Father, everything is possible for you. Take this cup from me" (Mark 14:36).

In first-century Judaism, addressing God as "Abba" would have been shockingly intimate. Yet Jesus not only used this term but taught His disciples to pray "Our Father," and through the Spirit extended this privilege to all believers. Paul writes that the Spirit of adoption causes us to cry "Abba, Father" (Romans 8:15).

The name Abba represents the culmination of God's self-revelation: not only mighty Elohim and sovereign Adonai, but also Father — a loving parent who delights in His children and lavishes upon them an inheritance that can never perish.

What This Name Means for Us

Calling God "Abba" transforms our approach to the spiritual life. Prayer is no longer a formal audience with a distant monarch but an intimate conversation with a loving Father. We approach already beloved, already accepted, already His.

For those with absent or disappointing earthly fathers, the name Abba offers healing and redefinition. He is the perfect Father — protective, present, patient, and overflowing with unconditional love.

The LORD My Shepherd

Jehovah Rohi

Understanding Jehovah Rohi

Jehovah Rohi — "The LORD My Shepherd" — is revealed in the beloved opening of Psalm 23: "The LORD is my shepherd, I lack nothing." In the ancient Near East, a shepherd was a protector, provider, guide, and constant companion to his flock, living with them day and night.

David, who wrote Psalm 23, understood shepherding intimately, having fought lions and bears to protect his flock. When David declared God as his Shepherd, he was saying the same fierce protection and tender guidance he gave his sheep, God gives to him — only infinitely greater.

Jesus claimed this name explicitly: "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep" (John 10:11). Jehovah Rohi personally knows each sheep by name and ultimately gives His own life so they might live.

What This Name Means for Us

Jehovah Rohi means we are never without guidance, provision, or protection. The Shepherd goes before us into every situation. Our role is simply to follow — to listen for His voice and trust He is leading us to good places even through dark valleys.

This name also speaks to rest. Jehovah Rohi gives us permission to stop striving and receive the rest our souls desperately need, knowing nothing will pluck us from His hand.

The LORD Our Righteousness

Jehovah Tsidkenu

Understanding Jehovah Tsidkenu

Jehovah Tsidkenu — "The LORD Our Righteousness" — is the prophetic name given to the coming Messiah in Jeremiah 23:6. It declares that the righteousness God's people need does not come from their own moral performance but from God Himself.

This name was given during one of Israel's darkest periods, when kings were corrupt and judgment was imminent. Into this moral bankruptcy, God promised a righteous Branch — a King who would become righteousness for His people.

The New Testament reveals this fulfilled in Christ: "God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (2 Corinthians 5:21) — the great exchange.

What This Name Means for Us

Jehovah Tsidkenu frees us from the exhausting treadmill of trying to earn God's acceptance through moral effort. When God looks at believers, He sees the perfect righteousness of His Son draped over us like a royal robe.

This truth produces both humility and confidence. On our worst day, we are still clothed in His righteousness — not that we became good enough for God, but that God became our goodness.

The Everlasting God

El Olam

Understanding El Olam

El Olam — "The Everlasting God" — first appears in Genesis 21:33, where Abraham "planted a tamarisk tree in Beersheba, and there he called on the name of the LORD, the Eternal God." The Hebrew word "olam" means everlasting, without beginning and without end.

Abraham planted one of the slowest-growing trees in the desert — an act of faith in God's long-term faithfulness, declaring trust in a God whose promises span generations and whose faithfulness never expires.

Isaiah expands this revelation: "The LORD is the everlasting God, the Creator of the ends of the earth. He will not grow tired or weary." Unlike everything else in creation that decays, El Olam endures.

What This Name Means for Us

In a culture obsessed with the immediate, El Olam invites us into an eternal perspective. Our problems are temporary, but God's love, promises, and purposes are everlasting.

El Olam also means God never burns out. His patience with us is eternal, His mercy new every morning, flowing from an inexhaustible source that will never run out.

God With Us

Immanuel

Understanding Immanuel

Immanuel — "God With Us" — is perhaps the most intimate of all God's names. First prophesied by Isaiah to King Ahaz during a national crisis (Isaiah 7:14), this name declares that the infinite, transcendent God would enter into human experience Himself — becoming a baby, born of a virgin, dwelling among us in flesh and blood.

Matthew's Gospel connects this prophecy to Jesus' birth: "The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and they will call him Immanuel — which means 'God with us.'" The invisible God became visible; the untouchable became touchable.

The name Immanuel encapsulates the entire arc of Scripture: from Eden where God walked with Adam and Eve, through the tabernacle and temple, to the incarnation, to Revelation 21:3 — "God's dwelling place is now among the people, and he will dwell with them."

What This Name Means for Us

Immanuel means we are never alone — not in our darkest moments, not in our deepest grief. The God of the universe chose to be WITH us, not just above us or for us, but present with us in every moment.

This name also reminds us of the staggering cost of God's presence. To be "with us" required incarnation — the eternal Son taking on human flesh, suffering, and death. Immanuel is the most costly love in the universe, given freely.
