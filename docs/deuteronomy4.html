Deuteronomy — Chapter 4

1Hear now, O Israel, the statutes and ordinances I am teaching you to follow, so that you may live and may enter and take possession of the land that the LORD, the God of your fathers, is giving you.

2You must not add to or subtract from what I command you, so that you may keep the commandments of the LORD your God that I am giving you.

3Your eyes have seen what the LORD did at Baal-peor, for the LORD your God destroyed from among you all who followed Baal of Peor.

4But you who held fast to the LORD your God are alive to this day, every one of you.

5See, I have taught you statutes and ordinances just as the LORD my God has commanded me, so that you may follow them in the land that you are about to enter and possess.

6Observe them carefully, for this will show your wisdom and understanding in the sight of the peoples, who will hear of all these statutes and say, “Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people.”

7For what nation is great enough to have a god as near to them as the LORD our God is to us whenever we call on Him?

8And what nation is great enough to have righteous statutes and ordinances like this entire law I set before you today?

9Only be on your guard and diligently watch yourselves, so that you do not forget the things your eyes have seen, and so that they do not slip from your heart as long as you live. Teach them to your children and grandchildren.

10The day you stood before the LORD your God at Horeb, the LORD said to me, “Gather the people before Me to hear My words, so that they may learn to fear Me all the days they live on the earth, and that they may teach them to their children.”

11You came near and stood at the base of the mountain, a mountain blazing with fire to the heavens, with black clouds and deep darkness.

12And the LORD spoke to you out of the fire. You heard the sound of the words but saw no form; there was only a voice.

13He declared to you His covenant, which He commanded you to follow—the Ten Commandments that He wrote on two tablets of stone.

14At that time the LORD commanded me to teach you the statutes and ordinances you are to follow in the land that you are crossing the Jordan to possess.

15So since you saw no form of any kind on the day the LORD spoke to you out of the fire at Horeb, be careful

16that you do not act corruptly and make an idol for yourselves of any form or shape, whether in the likeness of a male or female,

17of any beast that is on the earth or bird that flies in the air,

18or of any creature that crawls on the ground or fish that is in the waters below.

19When you look to the heavens and see the sun and moon and stars—all the host of heaven—do not be enticed to bow down and worship what the LORD your God has apportioned to all the nations under heaven.

20Yet the LORD has taken you and brought you out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt, to be the people of His inheritance, as you are today.

21The LORD, however, was angry with me on account of you, and He swore that I would not cross the Jordan to enter the good land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.

22For I will not be crossing the Jordan, because I must die in this land. But you shall cross over and take possession of that good land.

23Be careful that you do not forget the covenant of the LORD your God that He made with you; do not make an idol for yourselves in the form of anything He has forbidden you.

24For the LORD your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God.

25After you have children and grandchildren and you have been in the land a long time, if you then act corruptly and make an idol of any form—doing evil in the sight of the LORD your God and provoking Him to anger—

26I call heaven and earth as witnesses against you this day that you will quickly perish from the land that you are crossing the Jordan to possess. You will not live long upon it, but will be utterly destroyed.

27Then the LORD will scatter you among the peoples, and only a few of you will survive among the nations to which the LORD will drive you.

28And there you will serve man-made gods of wood and stone, which cannot see or hear or eat or smell.

29But if from there you will seek the LORD your God, you will find Him if you seek Him with all your heart and with all your soul.

30When you are in distress and all these things have happened to you, then in later days you will return to the LORD your God and listen to His voice.

31For the LORD your God is a merciful God; He will not abandon you or destroy you or forget the covenant with your fathers, which He swore to them by oath.

32Indeed, ask now from one end of the heavens to the other about the days that long preceded you, from the day that God created man on earth: Has anything as great as this ever happened or been reported?

33Has a people ever heard the voice of God speaking out of the fire, as you have, and lived?

34Or has any god tried to take as his own a nation out of another nation—by trials, signs, wonders, and war, by a strong hand and an outstretched arm, and by great terrors—as the LORD your God did for you in Egypt, before your eyes?

35You were shown these things so that you would know that the LORD is God; there is no other besides Him.

36He let you hear His voice from heaven to discipline you, and on earth He showed you His great fire, and you heard His words out of the fire.

37Because He loved your fathers, He chose their descendants after them and brought you out of Egypt by His presence and great power,

38to drive out before you nations greater and mightier than you, and to bring you into their land and give it to you for your inheritance, as it is this day.

39Know therefore this day and take to heart that the LORD is God in heaven above and on the earth below; there is no other.

40Keep His statutes and commandments, which I am giving you today, so that you and your children after you may prosper, and that you may live long in the land that the LORD your God is giving you for all time.

41Then Moses set aside three cities across the Jordan to the east

42to which a manslayer could flee after killing his neighbor unintentionally without prior malice. To save one’s own life, he could flee to one of these cities:

43Bezer in the wilderness on the plateau belonging to the Reubenites, Ramoth in Gilead belonging to the Gadites, or Golan in Bashan belonging to the Manassites.

44This is the law that Moses set before the Israelites.

45These are the testimonies, statutes, and ordinances that Moses proclaimed to them after they had come out of Egypt,

46while they were in the valley across the Jordan facing Beth-peor in the land of Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon and was defeated by Moses and the Israelites after they had come out of Egypt.

47They took possession of the land belonging to Sihon and to Og king of Bashan—the two Amorite kings across the Jordan to the east—

48extending from Aroer on the rim of the Arnon Valley as far as Mount Siyon (that is, Hermon),

49including all the Arabah on the east side of the Jordan and as far as the Sea of the Arabah, below the slopes of Pisgah.

1Now therefore hearken, O Israel, unto the statutes and unto the judgments, which I teach you, for to do them, that ye may live, and go in and possess the land which the LORD God of your fathers giveth you.

2Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you.

3Your eyes have seen what the LORD did because of Baal-peor: for all the men that followed Baal-peor, the LORD thy God hath destroyed them from among you.

4But ye that did cleave unto the LORD your God are alive every one of you this day.

5Behold, I have taught you statutes and judgments, even as the LORD my God commanded me, that ye should do so in the land whither ye go to possess it.

6Keep therefore and do them; for this is your wisdom and your understanding in the sight of the nations, which shall hear all these statutes, and say, Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people.

7For what nation is there so great, who hath God so nigh unto them, as the LORD our God is in all things that we call upon him for?

8And what nation is there so great, that hath statutes and judgments so righteous as all this law, which I set before you this day?

9Only take heed to thyself, and keep thy soul diligently, lest thou forget the things which thine eyes have seen, and lest they depart from thy heart all the days of thy life: but teach them thy sons, and thy sons’ sons;

10Specially the day that thou stoodest before the LORD thy God in Horeb, when the LORD said unto me, Gather me the people together, and I will make them hear my words, that they may learn to fear me all the days that they shall live upon the earth, and that they may teach their children.

11And ye came near and stood under the mountain; and the mountain burned with fire unto the midst of heaven, with darkness, clouds, and thick darkness.

12And the LORD spake unto you out of the midst of the fire: ye heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice.

13And he declared unto you his covenant, which he commanded you to perform, even ten commandments; and he wrote them upon two tables of stone.

14And the LORD commanded me at that time to teach you statutes and judgments, that ye might do them in the land whither ye go over to possess it.

15Take ye therefore good heed unto yourselves; for ye saw no manner of similitude on the day that the LORD spake unto you in Horeb out of the midst of the fire:

16Lest ye corrupt yourselves, and make you a graven image, the similitude of any figure, the likeness of male or female,

17The likeness of any beast that is on the earth, the likeness of any winged fowl that flieth in the air,

18The likeness of any thing that creepeth on the ground, the likeness of any fish that is in the waters beneath the earth:

19And lest thou lift up thine eyes unto heaven, and when thou seest the sun, and the moon, and the stars, even all the host of heaven, shouldest be driven to worship them, and serve them, which the LORD thy God hath divided unto all nations under the whole heaven.

20But the LORD hath taken you, and brought you forth out of the iron furnace, even out of Egypt, to be unto him a people of inheritance, as ye are this day.

21Furthermore the LORD was angry with me for your sakes, and sware that I should not go over Jordan, and that I should not go in unto that good land, which the LORD thy God giveth thee for an inheritance:

22But I must die in this land, I must not go over Jordan: but ye shall go over, and possess that good land.

23Take heed unto yourselves, lest ye forget the covenant of the LORD your God, which he made with you, and make you a graven image, or the likeness of any thing, which the LORD thy God hath forbidden thee.

24For the LORD thy God is a consuming fire, even a jealous God.

25When thou shalt beget children, and children’s children, and ye shall have remained long in the land, and shall corrupt yourselves, and make a graven image, or the likeness of any thing, and shall do evil in the sight of the LORD thy God, to provoke him to anger:

26I call heaven and earth to witness against you this day, that ye shall soon utterly perish from off the land whereunto ye go over Jordan to possess it; ye shall not prolong your days upon it, but shall utterly be destroyed.

27And the LORD shall scatter you among the nations, and ye shall be left few in number among the heathen, whither the LORD shall lead you.

28And there ye shall serve gods, the work of men’s hands, wood and stone, which neither see, nor hear, nor eat, nor smell.

29But if from thence thou shalt seek the LORD thy God, thou shalt find him, if thou seek him with all thy heart and with all thy soul.

30When thou art in tribulation, and all these things are come upon thee, even in the latter days, if thou turn to the LORD thy God, and shalt be obedient unto his voice;

31(For the LORD thy God is a merciful God;) he will not forsake thee, neither destroy thee, nor forget the covenant of thy fathers which he sware unto them.

32For ask now of the days that are past, which were before thee, since the day that God created man upon the earth, and ask from the one side of heaven unto the other, whether there hath been any such thing as this great thing is, or hath been heard like it?

33Did ever people hear the voice of God speaking out of the midst of the fire, as thou hast heard, and live?

34Or hath God assayed to go and take him a nation from the midst of another nation, by temptations, by signs, and by wonders, and by war, and by a mighty hand, and by a stretched out arm, and by great terrors, according to all that the LORD your God did for you in Egypt before your eyes?

35Unto thee it was shewed, that thou mightest know that the LORD he is God; there is none else beside him.

36Out of heaven he made thee to hear his voice, that he might instruct thee: and upon earth he shewed thee his great fire; and thou heardest his words out of the midst of the fire.

37And because he loved thy fathers, therefore he chose their seed after them, and brought thee out in his sight with his mighty power out of Egypt;

38To drive out nations from before thee greater and mightier than thou art, to bring thee in, to give thee their land for an inheritance, as it is this day.

39Know therefore this day, and consider it in thine heart, that the LORD he is God in heaven above, and upon the earth beneath: there is none else.

40Thou shalt keep therefore his statutes, and his commandments, which I command thee this day, that it may go well with thee, and with thy children after thee, and that thou mayest prolong thy days upon the earth, which the LORD thy God giveth thee, for ever.

41Then Moses severed three cities on this side Jordan toward the sunrising;

42That the slayer might flee thither, which should kill his neighbour unawares, and hated him not in times past; and that fleeing unto one of these cities he might live:

43Namely, Bezer in the wilderness, in the plain country, of the Reubenites; and Ramoth in Gilead, of the Gadites; and Golan in Bashan, of the Manassites.

44And this is the law which Moses set before the children of Israel:

45These are the testimonies, and the statutes, and the judgments, which Moses spake unto the children of Israel, after they came forth out of Egypt,

46On this side Jordan, in the valley over against Beth-peor, in the land of Sihon king of the Amorites, who dwelt at Heshbon, whom Moses and the children of Israel smote, after they were come forth out of Egypt:

47And they possessed his land, and the land of Og king of Bashan, two kings of the Amorites, which were on this side Jordan toward the sunrising;

48From Aroer, which is by the bank of the river Arnon, even unto mount Sion, which is Hermon,

49And all the plain on this side Jordan eastward, even unto the sea of the plain, under the springs of Pisgah.

1Now, Israel, pay attention to the statutes and ordinances I am about to teach you, so that you might live and go on to enter and take possession of the land that the Lord, the God of your ancestors, is giving you.

2Do not add a thing to what I command you nor subtract from it, so that you may keep the commandments of the Lord your God that I am delivering to you.

3You have witnessed what the Lord did at Baal Peor, how he eradicated from your midst everyone who followed Baal Peor.

4But you who remained faithful to the Lord your God are still alive to this very day, every one of you.

5Look! I have taught you statutes and ordinances just as the Lord my God told me to do, so that you might carry them out in the land you are about to enter and possess.

6So be sure to do them, because this will testify of your wise understanding to the people who will learn of all these statutes and say, “Indeed, this great nation is a very wise people.”

7In fact, what other great nation has a god so near to them like the Lord our God whenever we call on him?

8And what other great nation has statutes and ordinances as just as this whole law that I am about to share with you today?

9Again, however, pay very careful attention, lest you forget the things you have seen and disregard them for the rest of your life; instead teach them to your children and grandchildren.

10You stood before the Lord your God at Horeb and he said to me, “Assemble the people before me so that I can tell them my commands. Then they will learn to revere me all the days they live in the land, and they will instruct their children.”

11You approached and stood at the foot of the mountain, a mountain ablaze to the sky above it and yet dark with a thick cloud.

12Then the Lord spoke to you from the middle of the fire; you heard speech but you could not see anything—only a voice was heard.

13And he revealed to you the covenant he has commanded you to keep, the Ten Commandments, writing them on two stone tablets.

14Moreover, at that same time the Lord commanded me to teach you statutes and ordinances for you to keep in the land that you are about to enter and possess.

15Be very careful, then, because you saw no form at the time the Lord spoke to you at Horeb from the middle of the fire.

16I say this so you will not corrupt yourselves by making an image in the form of any kind of figure. This includes the likeness of a human male or female,

17any kind of land animal, any bird that flies in the sky,

18anything that crawls on the ground, or any fish in the deep waters under the earth.

19When you look up to the sky and see the sun, moon, and stars—the whole heavenly creation—you must not be seduced to worship and serve them, for the Lord your God has assigned them to all the people of the world.

20You, however, the Lord has selected and brought from Egypt, that iron-smelting furnace, to be his special people as you are today.

21But the Lord became angry with me because of you and vowed that I would never cross the Jordan nor enter the good land that he is about to give you.

22So I must die here in this land; I will not cross the Jordan. But you are going over and will possess that good land.

23Be on guard so that you do not forget the covenant of the Lord your God that he has made with you, and that you do not make an image of any kind, just as he has forbidden you.

24For the Lord your God is a consuming fire; he is a jealous God.

25After you have produced children and grandchildren and have been in the land a long time, if you become corrupt and make an image of any kind and do other evil things before the Lord your God that enrage him,

26I invoke heaven and earth as witnesses against you today that you will surely and swiftly be removed from the very land you are about to cross the Jordan to possess. You will not last long there because you will surely be annihilated.

27Then the Lord will scatter you among the peoples and there will be very few of you among the nations where the Lord will drive you.

28There you will worship gods made by human hands—wood and stone that can neither see, hear, eat, nor smell.

29But if you seek the Lord your God from there, you will find him, if, indeed, you seek him with all your heart and soul.

30In your distress when all these things happen to you in future days, if you return to the Lord your God and obey him

31(for he is a merciful God), he will not let you down or destroy you, for he cannot forget the covenant with your ancestors that he confirmed by oath to them.

Summary

Moses urges Israel to keep God's statutes without addition or subtraction, reminding them of what they witnessed at Baal-peor and warning them not to forget what they saw at Horeb (vv. 1-14). He warns sharply against making any carved image of God, since Israel saw no form when the LORD spoke to them from the fire, and against turning to worship the sun, moon, or stars (vv. 15-19). Moses foresees future exile for disobedience but promises that God will still be found by those who seek Him with all their heart, since He is a merciful God who keeps His covenant (vv. 25-31). He closes by appointing three cities of refuge east of the Jordan (vv. 41-43) and setting the stage for repeating the law (vv. 44-49).

Authorship & Background

Author: Moses. Deuteronomy is the fifth and final book of the Pentateuch, written at the end of the 40 years of wilderness wandering (approximately 1406 BC). Hebrew title: "Devarim" — "Words/Things." Greek title: "Deuteronomion" — "Second Law" (a repetition/expansion of the Law for the new generation). The book consists of Moses' farewell speeches to Israel on the plains of Moab, just before they enter the Promised Land. Moses will not enter with them — these are his final words to the nation he has led for 40 years.
Historical Context: Chapter 4 is the climax of Moses' first address — a passionate exhortation to obedience. Having reviewed the history (chapters 1-3), Moses now draws the application: remember what you saw, keep the commandments, do not make idols, and teach your children. This chapter contains some of the most exalted theology in the Old Testament: God's uniqueness ("there is none else beside him," v.35), His nearness ("what nation is there so great, who hath God so nigh unto them?" v.7), His character as consuming fire and jealous God (v.24), yet also merciful — He will not forsake His covenant (v.31). Moses warns prophetically of future apostasy and exile (vv.25-28) but promises restoration for those who seek God with all their heart (vv.29-31). The chapter concludes with Moses designating three cities of refuge east of the Jordan (vv.41-43) and a transitional summary (vv.44-49) that introduces the second address (chapters 5-26).
Classification: Historical Narrative
Key Themes: a covenant explained rather than merely repeated for a generation that mostly never heard it delivered at Horeb, a nation asked whether any other people has a god so near or laws so righteous, no image permitted of anything because Israel heard only a voice at the mountain and saw no form to copy, exile foretold for a generation not yet born alongside a promise that seeking God with all the heart will still find him even there, and three cities of refuge set apart east of the Jordan in the very middle of an otherwise theological address
The Call to Obedience (vv.1-4): "Hearken, O Israel" (v.1) — listen and do, "that ye may live, and go in and possess the land." Do not add to or diminish from God's word (v.2). The warning of Baal-peor is fresh: those who followed that idol were destroyed; those who "did cleave unto the LORD" are alive today (vv.3-4). Obedience is life; disobedience is death.
Israel's Unique Wisdom (vv.5-8): Keeping God's statutes is Israel's "wisdom and understanding in the sight of the nations" (v.6). Two rhetorical questions: (1) "What nation is there so great, who hath God so nigh unto them?" (v.7) — no nation has a God who is near and who answers prayer. (2) "What nation is there so great, that hath statutes and judgments so righteous?" (v.8) — no nation has laws as just as God's. The nations will look at Israel and say: "Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people" (v.6).
Remember Horeb — No Idols (vv.9-24): Moses commands: "take heed to thyself, and keep thy soul diligently, lest thou forget" (v.9). Teach your children and grandchildren (v.9). Remember the day at Horeb: the mountain burned with fire, darkness, clouds (v.11). God spoke from the fire — "ye heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice" (v.12). This is the theological foundation for the prohibition of images: God revealed Himself in word, not in form. Therefore: no carved image of any figure — male, female, animal, bird, creeping thing, fish (vv.16-18). No worship of sun, moon, or stars (v.19). God took Israel from "the iron furnace" of Egypt to be His own people (v.20). Warning: "the LORD thy God is a consuming fire, even a jealous God" (v.24).
Exile Foretold, and the Way Back (vv.25-31): Moses looks past the conquest to a generation not yet born, when thou shalt beget children, and children's children, and shall have remained long in the land, and describes idolatry, then scattering. Ye shall be left few in number among the heathen. The description of exile is unsparing and includes serving gods of wood and stone. But the passage does not end there. If from thence thou shalt seek the LORD thy God, thou shalt find him, if thou seek him with all thy heart and with all thy soul. The ground of the return is not their improvement but his character, for the LORD thy God is a merciful God, he will not forsake thee, neither destroy thee, nor forget the covenant of thy fathers. Seven hundred years before the exile, the way out of it is already written down.
The Uniqueness of God (vv.32-40): Moses appeals to all of history: has anything like this ever happened? (v.32). Has any people heard God's voice from fire and lived? (v.33). Has any god taken a nation from another nation by signs, wonders, war, and mighty hand? (v.34). The conclusion: "the LORD he is God; there is none else beside him" (v.35). He loved their fathers, chose their offspring, brought them out of Egypt by His own presence (v.37). Therefore: "know therefore this day, and consider it in thine heart, that the LORD he is God in heaven above, and upon the earth beneath: there is none else" (v.39). Keep His commandments "that it may go well with thee, and with thy children after thee" (v.40).
Cities of Refuge East of Jordan (vv.41-43): Moses sets apart three cities for the manslayer who kills unintentionally: Bezer (for Reuben), Ramoth in Gilead (for Gad), Golan in Bashan (for Manasseh) (v.43). Even in the midst of theological exhortation, Moses attends to practical justice.
Transitional Summary (vv.44-49): A heading for the second address: "this is the law which Moses set before the children of Israel" (v.44). Location: beyond the Jordan, opposite Beth-peor, in the land of Sihon (vv.46-47). Territory: from Aroer to Hermon, the entire eastern Arabah (vv.48-49).

Setting: The plains of Moab, in the last weeks of Moses' life, about forty years after the Exodus. Chapters 1–3 retold the journey from Sinai (Horeb) to the edge of Canaan: the failure at Kadesh, the long wandering, the victories over Sihon and Og, and God's refusal to let Moses cross the Jordan. Now the history turns into a call to action. "And now, O Israel, listen" (v1). This chapter is the climax of Moses' first sermon, and it is one of the richest theological passages in the Old Testament. It explains why Israel must not make images of God, warns of exile if they do, promises mercy if they return, and proclaims that "the LORD is God… there is no other." In the shape of an ancient treaty, this is the point where the history gives way to the call to loyalty. The chapter ends with a short narrative note about three cities of refuge, and then a heading that introduces the main body of the law.

Moses Commands Obedience (vv1–14)

  • "Listen to the statutes and the rules… and do them, that you may live" (v1). "Listen" (shema) means hear and obey, not just hear. Life in the land depends on it. "Statutes and rules" is a common pair in Deuteronomy for the whole body of God's instruction.
  • "You shall not add to the word that I command you, nor take from it" (v2). God's word is complete for its purpose. Adding human rules to it, or quietly dropping the parts we dislike, both put man in charge of what God says. The principle repeats in 12:32 and Proverbs 30:6, and the last chapter of the Bible closes with a similar warning (Rev 22:18–19). Jesus rebuked the Pharisees for doing exactly this, making void the word of God through their tradition (Mark 7:8–13).
  • "Your eyes have seen what the LORD did at Baal-peor" (v3). Only months before, Israel had joined in the sexual rites of Baal of Peor and 24,000 died (Num 25). Baal was the Canaanite storm and fertility god, worshiped under many local names. "But you who held fast to the LORD your God are all alive today" (v4). "Held fast" is dabaq, to cling, the word used of a husband clinging to his wife (Gen 2:24). The survivors are standing in front of Moses as living proof.
  • "That will be your wisdom and your understanding in the sight of the peoples" (v6). Israel's obedience would be a witness. The nations would see a people whose life was shaped by good laws and say, "Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people." Wisdom in the Bible begins with obeying God (Prov 1:7).
  • "What great nation is there that has a god so near to it… whenever we call upon him?" (v7). Israel's greatness was not its size or army but God's nearness. Other nations' gods were distant, needing rituals and bribes to get their attention. The LORD hears whenever his people call.
  • "Statutes and rules so righteous as all this law" (v8). Other ancient law codes existed, such as Hammurabi's, and there are real similarities. But Israel's law protected the poor, the foreigner and the slave in ways those codes did not, and it flowed from the character of a righteous God, not from a king's will.
  • "Keep your soul diligently, lest you forget" (v9). The great danger is forgetting. "Make them known to your children and your children's children." Faith is passed on through families. Deuteronomy repeats this again and again (6:7, 20–25; 11:19).
  • "The day that you stood before the LORD your God at Horeb" (v10). Moses speaks to the new generation as if they had been there. Many were, as children, and the rest stood there in their parents. The covenant was made with the whole people, across generations (5:3; 29:14–15).
  • "The mountain burned with fire to the heart of heaven, wrapped in darkness, cloud, and gloom" (v11). God revealed himself and hid himself at the same moment. The fire showed his presence. The darkness kept his glory from consuming them (Exod 19:16–19; 20:18–21).
  • "You heard the sound of words, but saw no form; there was only a voice" (v12). This is the key to the whole chapter. At Sinai God spoke but did not show a shape. Israel's relationship with God was to be built on his word, not on an image.
  • "His covenant… the Ten Commandments, and he wrote them on two tablets of stone" (v13). "Ten Commandments" is literally "ten words." The two tablets may have been two complete copies, one for each party, as was done with ancient treaties. Both were kept in the ark (Exod 25:16).
  • Commentators: JFB reads v2 as forbidding "the introduction of any heathen superstition or forms of worship" and the neglect of what was commanded. It adds that the law itself was "a temporary economy" for the church's infancy, which is a Christian reading looking back from the New Testament. On v7, Henry says God is "ready to give an answer of peace to every prayer of faith." On v6, both JFB and Guzik note that some ancient thinkers admired Israel's laws and borrowed from them. Guzik stresses that faith rests on real events that people saw, and connects this to the eyewitness testimony of the resurrection (1 John 1:1–3). JFB has no notes on vv11, 13 or 14.

Idolatry Forbidden (vv15–31)

  • "Since you saw no form… beware lest you act corruptly by making a carved image" (vv15–16). Because God showed no form, any image of him is a lie about him. This is not just a rule against worshiping other gods. It forbids making an image even of the true God. Israel learned this painfully with the golden calf, which Aaron called "the LORD" (Exod 32:4–5).
  • "Male or female… animal… bird… creeping thing… fish" (vv16–18). The list runs through creation in roughly the reverse order of Genesis 1, and it covers the gods of Egypt and Canaan: human-shaped gods, bulls, falcons, snakes, fish. Paul echoes this list exactly when he describes humanity exchanging "the glory of the immortal God for images resembling mortal man and birds and animals and creeping things" (Rom 1:23).
  • "The sun and the moon and the stars, all the host of heaven" (v19). Sun, moon and star worship was everywhere in the ancient world, including Egypt and later Babylon. Israel later fell into it (2 Kgs 21:3–5; Jer 8:2; Ezek 8:16). "Things that the LORD your God has allotted to all the peoples." This phrase is debated. Some read it as saying God gave the heavenly bodies to all humanity as lights and signs of the seasons (Gen 1:14), so they belong to everyone and are not gods. Others connect it to 32:8–9, where God divided the nations and kept Israel for himself, and think it means God allowed other nations to go their own way for a time (Acts 14:16; 17:30). Both readings agree that Israel was not to worship them.
  • "The iron furnace, out of Egypt" (v20). An iron-smelting furnace was the hottest fire people knew. Egypt was a place of suffering, but also of refining. God brought Israel out "to be a people of his own inheritance." Israel is God's inheritance (32:9).
  • "I must die in this land… But you shall go over" (vv21–22). Moses mentions his own exclusion for the third time (1:37; 3:26). The point now is a warning: if even Moses fell short and paid for it, how seriously must the people take God's commands?
  • "The LORD your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God" (v24). God's holiness is like fire: it purifies and it destroys. "Jealous" (qanna) is used only of God in the Old Testament. It is not petty envy. It is the right, fierce love of a husband who will not share his wife with another lover. Hebrews quotes this verse: "our God is a consuming fire" (Heb 12:29).
  • "When you… have grown old in the land" (v25). The danger will come not in the hard years but in the comfortable ones, when the miracles are a distant memory (6:10–12; 8:11–14).
  • "I call heaven and earth to witness against you today" (v26). Ancient treaties named the gods as witnesses. Israel had no other gods, so Moses calls on creation itself (30:19; 31:28; Isa 1:2).
  • "The LORD will scatter you among the peoples" (v27). This was fulfilled in the Assyrian exile of the northern kingdom (722 BC) and the Babylonian exile of Judah (586 BC). "There you will serve gods of wood and stone… that neither see, nor hear, nor eat, nor smell" (v28). A grim irony: if Israel wants idols, it will be sent to lands full of them. Psalm 115:4–8 and Isaiah 44:9–20 mock idols in the same way.
  • "But from there you will seek the LORD your God and you will find him, if you search after him with all your heart and with all your soul" (v29). The great promise of the chapter. Exile is not the end. Jeremiah quotes this verse almost word for word in his letter to the exiles in Babylon: "You will seek me and find me, when you seek me with all your heart" (Jer 29:13). Daniel's prayer (Dan 9) and Nehemiah's (Neh 1:8–9) rest on it.
  • "When you are in tribulation… in the latter days, you will return" (v30). "The latter days" (acharit hayyamim) means "the end of the days," the future after the judgment. The prophets later use the same phrase for the age when God would restore his people and send the Messiah (Isa 2:2; Hos 3:5). "Return" (shuv) is the Old Testament's word for repentance.
  • "For the LORD your God is a merciful God. He will not leave you or destroy you or forget the covenant" (v31). The consuming fire of v24 and the merciful God of v31 are the same God. His jealousy and his mercy both come from covenant love.
  • Commentators: JFB calls v20's furnace a "furnace for smelting iron," a picture of Israel's bondage and affliction, while the Tyndale notes add the idea of refining. On idols, Henry asks, "shall we serve those that were made to serve us?" On v29 he writes, "Afflictions engage and quicken us to seek God." Guzik warns that no leader stands above God's word, as Moses' exclusion shows. He passes on Thompson's point that God's jealousy is not like human jealousy but a holy zeal, which agrees with the Tyndale notes. He reads the scattering as fulfilled in the Babylonian exile and stresses that God's mercy stays open even then. JFB says calling heaven and earth as witnesses is figurative. JFB has no notes on vv21–25, 27, 29 or 31.

The LORD Alone Is God (vv32–40)

  • "Ask now of the days that are past… since the day that God created man" (v32). Moses invites Israel to search all of history and all of space. Has anything like this ever happened?
  • "Did any people ever hear the voice of a god speaking out of the midst of the fire… and still live?" (v33). Hearing God's voice should have been fatal (5:24–26; Exod 33:20). Israel heard and lived.
  • "Has any god ever attempted to go and take a nation for himself from the midst of another nation" (v34). Ancient peoples had many stories of gods, but none of a god who freed a nation of slaves from a superpower "by trials, by signs, by wonders, and by war, by a mighty hand and an outstretched arm." Those last two phrases are the language of the Exodus (Exod 6:6) and become a refrain in Deuteronomy.
  • "That you might know that the LORD is God; there is no other besides him" (v35). This is plain monotheism: not just "worship only the LORD," but "there is no other god." Isaiah says it again and again: "I am the LORD, and there is no other" (Isa 45:5–6, 18, 21–22).
  • "Out of heaven he let you hear his voice… on earth he let you see his great fire" (v36). God's voice came from heaven and his fire stood on earth. He is God in both places, as v39 will say.
  • "Because he loved your fathers and chose their offspring" (v37). This is the first time Deuteronomy says God loved, and it explains everything. Israel was not chosen because it was great or good, but because God loved (7:7–8). "With his own presence" is literally "with his face." God himself went with them (Exod 33:14).
  • "Know therefore today, and lay it to your heart, that the LORD is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath; there is no other" (v39). Knowing this is not just a matter of the mind. It must be "laid to the heart," shaping how one lives. Rahab the Canaanite later says almost exactly this: "the LORD your God, he is God in the heavens above and on the earth beneath" (Josh 2:11).
  • "That it may go well with you and with your children after you" (v40). Obedience is not a burden laid on Israel to earn God's love. It is the way of life in the land that love has given.
  • Commentators: Henry says Israel's laws are "above the statutes and judgments of any of the nations." He also sees Sinai's fire as "an earnest of the day of judgment, in which the Lord Jesus shall be revealed in flaming fire" (2 Thess 1:7–8). Guzik stresses that Israel was chosen for a role in God's plan and not saved by ancestry (Matt 3:9), and that obedience is the only fitting answer to such love. JFB has no notes on vv32–40.

Cities of Refuge (vv41–43)

  • "Then Moses set apart three cities in the east beyond the Jordan" (v41). The sermon pauses for a brief narrative note. Numbers 35 required six cities of refuge, places where someone who had killed accidentally could flee from the avenger of blood and have a fair hearing. Moses designates the three on the east side now, since that land was already in Israel's hands. The other three would be named after the conquest (Josh 20:7).
  • "Bezer… Ramoth in Gilead… Golan in Bashan" (v43). One for each eastern tribe: Bezer in Reuben's tableland, Ramoth-gilead in Gad's territory, and Golan in Manasseh's region of Bashan. The modern Golan Heights take their name from this town.
  • Why here? The Tyndale notes suggest the placement fits the promise of long life in the land just made (v40): the cities protect innocent life. It also shows Moses doing what he could before he died, setting up justice for the land he would not enter.
  • Commentators: Guzik notes Moses acted on the part of the command he could carry out now rather than waiting for everything to be in place. JFB simply cross-references Joshua 20.

Introduction to the Law (vv44–49)

  • "This is the law that Moses set before the people of Israel" (v44). A new heading. The history and the call to loyalty are done. Now comes the main body of the covenant, from chapter 5 to chapter 26. "Law" is torah, which comes from a verb meaning "to teach." It is instruction from a Father, not just a legal code.
  • "The testimonies, the statutes, and the rules" (v45). Three words for God's instruction. "Testimonies" are the solemn terms of the covenant, the witness of what God requires.
  • "In the valley opposite Beth-peor, in the land of Sihon" (v46). The setting is restated: the very place of Israel's recent sin (v3) and its recent victories (vv46–47).
  • "As far as Mount Sirion (that is, Hermon)" (v48). The Hebrew text here actually reads "Sion" (not the Zion of Jerusalem), an otherwise unknown name for Hermon. The ESV follows the Syriac version and reads "Sirion," the name used in 3:9. Either way, it is the great mountain in the north.
  • Commentators: Guzik mentions the "Sion" reading and links it to Sirion in 3:9 and Psalm 29:6.

Pointing to Christ

Israel saw no form at Sinai, only heard a voice, and was forbidden to make any image of God. The New Testament answers that ban not by relaxing it but by fulfilling it. "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (John 1:18). Christ is "the image of the invisible God" (Col 1:15), the one true image, not made by human hands. The God who spoke from the fire has now "spoken to us by his Son" (Heb 1:1–2). And the promise of v29, that those scattered far away would seek God and find him, is how Paul describes God's purpose for all nations: "that they should seek God… yet he is actually not far from each one of us" (Acts 17:27). The "God so near" of v7 has come nearer still in Jesus, Immanuel, "God with us."

Commentary

  • Enduring Word (David Guzik): enduringword.com Guzik emphasizes the uniqueness of Israel's relationship with God — no other nation had a god who was near, who spoke, who answered prayer. He notes that the prohibition of images flows from the nature of the Horeb revelation: God spoke but showed no form. He highlights the prophetic accuracy of vv.25-31 — fulfilled in the Babylonian exile and subsequent restorations.

Reflection

  • The Privilege of Nearness (v.7) — Moses tells Israel that no other nation has a God as near to them as the Lord is to Israel. Do you treat God's nearness as your greatest asset, above comfort, success, or possessions?
  • Guarding Against Forgetting (v.9) — Moses warns Israel to watch themselves closely so they do not forget what they saw and fail to teach it to their children. What spiritual truths are you at risk of quietly forgetting or failing to pass on?
  • A God Heard, Not Seen (v.12) — At Horeb Israel heard God's voice but saw no form, which becomes the basis for the ban on idols. Where might you be tempted to shrink God down into something manageable rather than trusting Him as He has actually revealed Himself?
  • A Jealous, Consuming Love (v.24) — God describes Himself as a consuming fire who is jealous for His people's undivided devotion. Does anything currently compete with God for first place in your affection?
  • Found by Those Who Seek Wholeheartedly (v.29) — Even after exile and judgment, Moses promises that seeking God with all your heart and soul will lead to finding Him. Is there a season of distance from God in your life where wholehearted seeking could bring you back?
  • A Covenant God Will Not Forget (v.31) — Moses grounds Israel's hope in God's mercy, promising He will not abandon His covenant even after their unfaithfulness. Where do you need to rest in God's faithfulness rather than your own track record?
