1Jesus said to His disciples, “It is inevitable that stumbling blocks will come, but woe to the one through whom they come!

2It would be better for him to have a millstone hung around his neck and to be thrown into the sea than to cause one of these little ones to stumble.

3Watch yourselves. If your brother sins, rebuke him; and if he repents, forgive him.

4Even if he sins against you seven times in a day, and seven times returns to say, ‘I repent,’ you must forgive him.”

6And the Lord answered, “If you have faith the size of a mustard seed, you can say to this mulberry tree, ‘Be uprooted and planted in the sea,’ and it will obey you.

7Which of you whose servant comes in from plowing or shepherding in the field will say to him, ‘Come at once and sit down to eat’?

8Instead, won’t he tell him, ‘Prepare my meal and dress yourself to serve me while I eat and drink, and afterward you may eat and drink’?

9Does he thank the servant because he did what he was told?

10So you also, when you have done everything commanded of you, should say, ‘We are unworthy servants; we have only done our duty.’”

11While Jesus was on His way to Jerusalem, He was passing between Samaria and Galilee.

12As He entered one of the villages, He was met by ten lepers. They stood at a distance

13and raised their voices, shouting, “Jesus, Master, have mercy on us!”

14When Jesus saw them, He said, “Go, show yourselves to the priests.” And as they were on their way, they were cleansed.

15When one of them saw that he was healed, he came back, praising God in a loud voice.

16He fell facedown at Jesus’ feet in thanksgiving to Him—and he was a Samaritan.

18Was no one found except this foreigner to return and give glory to God?”

28It was the same in the days of Lot: People were eating and drinking, buying and selling, planting and building.

29But on the day Lot left Sodom, fire and sulfur rained down from heaven and destroyed them all.

30It will be just like that on the day the Son of Man is revealed.

Summary

Jesus warns His disciples against causing others to sin and calls them to forgive a repentant brother repeatedly, even seven times a day (vv. 1-4). When the apostles ask Him to increase their faith, He says even faith the size of a mustard seed could uproot a tree, then illustrates humble obedience with the picture of a servant who does his duty without expecting thanks (vv. 5-10). Traveling toward Jerusalem, He heals ten lepers, but only one, a Samaritan, returns to thank Him, prompting Jesus to note how few give glory to God (vv. 11-19). Asked by the Pharisees when the kingdom of God will come, He says it does not arrive with visible signs but is already among them, then warns His disciples that the day of the Son of Man will come suddenly, like lightning, after He first suffers and is rejected (vv. 20-25). He compares that day to the sudden judgment in the days of Noah and Lot, urging people not to look back like Lot's wife, since some will be taken and others left without warning (vv. 26-37).

Authorship & Background

Author: Luke (see Chapter 1 notes for full authorship details).
Historical Context: Chapter 17 covers four topics: causing others to stumble and the duty of forgiveness (vv.1-10), the healing of ten lepers (vv.11-19), and teaching about the coming of the kingdom (vv.20-37). Jesus is still on His journey toward Jerusalem.
Offences, Forgiveness, and Faith as a Grain (vv.1-6): Three sayings in six verses and each is uncomfortable. The first is a warning to whoever causes a little one to stumble, better that a millstone were hanged about his neck. The second sets no limit on forgiveness and no limit on the frequency, if he trespass against thee seven times in a day, and seven times in a day turn again to thee, saying, I repent, thou shalt forgive him. The apostles' response to that is the one recorded prayer for more faith in the Gospels, Lord, increase our faith, and the answer is about size rather than quantity, if ye had faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye might say unto this sycamine tree, Be thou plucked up by the root.
Unprofitable Servants (vv.7-10): A challenging teaching on the nature of obedience. After doing everything commanded, a servant should say "We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do." Obedience is not extra credit — it is the baseline. We don't earn God's favor by doing what He commands; we simply fulfill our obligation.
The Ten Lepers (vv.11-19): Ten lepers cried out for mercy. Jesus told them to go show themselves to the priests (the required step for being declared clean, Leviticus 14). As they went, they were healed. Only one returned to give thanks — and he was a Samaritan. Jesus' question "Where are the nine?" is one of the most convicting questions in the Gospels.
The Coming of the Kingdom (vv.20-37): The Pharisees asked when the kingdom would come. Jesus said it doesn't come "with observation" (v.20) — it's not a visible political event you can point to. It is "within you" (or "among you"). But the Son of Man's return will be visible — like lightning across the sky (v.24).

Map & Geography

  • The border of Samaria and Galilee (v.11): Jesus traveled along the border between Samaria and Galilee on His way south to Jerusalem. The ten lepers were healed in this region.
  • Sodom (v.29): Referenced as a geographic and moral example — the city near the Dead Sea destroyed by fire from heaven.

Reflection

  • Unlimited Forgiveness (v.4) — Jesus commands forgiving a brother who sins and repents seven times in a day, refusing any limit on repeated forgiveness. Is there someone you've stopped forgiving because they keep failing in the same way?
  • Faith Like a Mustard Seed (v.6) — When the apostles ask Jesus to increase their faith, He says faith the size of a mustard seed is enough to accomplish the impossible. Where do you hold back from acting because you feel your faith isn't big enough yet?
  • Obedience Isn't Extra Credit (v.10) — Jesus teaches that after doing everything commanded, a servant has only done his duty and shouldn't expect special credit. Where are you expecting God to owe you something for simply doing what He already asked?
  • The One Who Returned to Give Thanks (vv.15-16) — Of ten lepers healed, only one comes back to thank Jesus, and he's the outsider you'd least expect. What has God done for you recently that you haven't stopped to thank Him for?
  • Remember Lot's Wife (v.32) — Jesus points to a woman who physically left a doomed city but looked back because her heart never really left. What are you physically walking away from but still emotionally clinging to?
  • Losing Your Life to Save It (v.33) — Jesus warns that clinging to self-preservation actually costs you your life, while surrendering it saves you. Where is self-preservation keeping you from following Christ fully right now?
