1After David had finished speaking with Saul, the souls of Jonathan and David were knit together, and Jonathan loved him as himself.

2And from that day Saul kept David with him and did not let him return to his father’s house.

3Then Jonathan made a covenant with David because he loved him as himself.

4And Jonathan removed the robe he was wearing and gave it to David, along with his tunic, his sword, his bow, and his belt.

5So David marched out and prospered in everything Saul sent him to do, and Saul set him over the men of war. And this was pleasing in the sight of all the people, and of Saul’s officers as well.

6As the troops were returning home after David had killed the Philistine, the women came out of all the cities of Israel to meet King Saul with singing and dancing, with joyful songs, and with tambourines and other instruments.

7And as the women danced, they sang out: “Saul has slain his thousands, and David his tens of thousands.”

8And Saul was furious and resented this song. “They have ascribed tens of thousands to David,” he said, “but only thousands to me. What more can he have but the kingdom?”

9And from that day forward Saul kept a jealous eye on David.

10The next day a spirit of distress sent from God came upon Saul, and he prophesied inside the house while David played the harp as usual. Now Saul was holding a spear,

11and he hurled it, thinking, “I will pin David to the wall.” But David eluded him twice.

12So Saul was afraid of David, because the LORD was with David but had departed from Saul.

13Therefore Saul sent David away and gave him command of a thousand men. David led the troops out to battle and back,

14and he continued to prosper in all his ways, because the LORD was with him.

15When Saul saw that David was very successful, he was afraid of him.

16But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he was leading them out to battle and back.

17Then Saul said to David, “Here is my older daughter Merab. I will give her to you in marriage. Only be valiant for me and fight the LORD’s battles.” But Saul was thinking, “I need not raise my hand against him; let the hand of the Philistines be against him.”

18And David said to Saul, “Who am I, and what is my family or my father’s clan in Israel, that I should become the son-in-law of the king?”

19So when it was time to give Saul’s daughter Merab to David, she was given in marriage to Adriel of Meholah.

20Now Saul’s daughter Michal loved David, and when this was reported to Saul, it pleased him.

21“I will give her to David,” Saul thought, “so that she may be a snare to him, and the hand of the Philistines may be against him.” So Saul said to David, “For a second time now you can be my son-in-law.”

22Then Saul ordered his servants, “Speak to David privately and tell him, ‘Behold, the king is pleased with you, and all his servants love you. Now therefore, become his son-in-law.’”

23But when Saul’s servants relayed these words to David, he replied, “Does it seem trivial in your sight to be the son-in-law of the king? I am a poor man and lightly esteemed.”

24And the servants told Saul what David had said.

25Saul replied, “Say to David, ‘The king desires no other dowry but a hundred Philistine foreskins as revenge on his enemies.’” But Saul intended to cause David’s death at the hands of the Philistines.

26When the servants reported these terms to David, he was pleased to become the king’s son-in-law. Before the wedding day arrived,

27David and his men went out and killed two hundred Philistines. He brought their foreskins and presented them as payment in full to become the king’s son-in-law. Then Saul gave his daughter Michal to David in marriage.

28When Saul realized that the LORD was with David and that his daughter Michal loved David,

29he grew even more afraid of David. So from then on Saul was David’s enemy.

30Every time the Philistine commanders came out for battle, David was more successful than all of Saul’s officers, so that his name was highly esteemed.

Summary

After David's victory, Jonathan's soul is knit to David's in deep friendship, and the two make a covenant, with Jonathan giving David his robe, armor, sword, and bow (vv. 1-4). David serves successfully under Saul, but when the women celebrate his return singing that Saul has slain his thousands and David his ten thousands, Saul grows jealous and suspicious that only the kingdom remains for David to gain (vv. 5-9). The next day, tormented by an evil spirit, Saul twice hurls a javelin at David while he plays the harp, and afterward, fearing David because the LORD is with him, removes him to command a thousand men, where David continues to succeed and grow in the people's love (vv. 10-16). Saul then offers his daughter Merab to David as a trap meant to expose him to the Philistines, but she is given to another man instead; when Michal, Saul's other daughter, is found to love David, Saul demands a dowry of a hundred Philistine foreskins hoping David will be killed in the attempt (vv. 17-25). David instead kills two hundred Philistines and marries Michal, but Saul, seeing that the LORD is with David, becomes his lifelong enemy even as David continues to prove more skilled than all of Saul's servants (vv. 26-30).

Authorship & Background

Author: Unknown, traditionally attributed to Samuel (for the early chapters), Nathan, and Gad (1 Chronicles 29:29). Originally one book with 2 Samuel in the Hebrew Bible. Written approximately 1050-950 BC. The book covers the transition from the judges to the monarchy — from the birth of Samuel through the death of Saul (approximately 1105-1010 BC). Key themes: God's sovereignty in raising and removing leaders, the danger of rejecting God's rule, the contrast between Saul (man's choice) and David (God's choice), and the principle that 'man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart' (16:7).
Historical Context: Chapter 18 marks the beginning of Saul's active persecution of David. After the Goliath victory, David enters Saul's court permanently and two defining relationships form: his covenant friendship with Jonathan and his growing enmity with Saul. The chapter traces a devastating progression in Saul — from jealousy (v.8) to suspicion (v.9) to murderous rage (v.11) to fear (v.12) to scheming (v.17, 21, 25) to permanent enmity (v.29). Meanwhile, David's trajectory is the opposite: success, wisdom, love from the people, and the LORD's presence. The contrast is stark and intentional — everything David touches prospers because "the LORD was with him" (vv.12, 14, 28), while Saul spirals downward because the LORD "was departed from Saul" (v.12). Jonathan's covenant with David is remarkable: the crown prince voluntarily surrenders his royal insignia (robe, armor, sword, bow, belt) to the man who will take his throne. This is not political naivety but spiritual discernment — Jonathan recognizes God's hand on David and submits to it. The women's song — "Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands" (v.7) — becomes the catalyst for Saul's jealousy. From this point forward, Saul's life is consumed with one obsession: destroying David. Every scheme fails because God protects His anointed, and each failure only deepens Saul's fear and hatred.
Classification: Historical Narrative
Key Themes: A crown prince's soul knit to David's and royal robe and armor handed over as though the throne itself is being surrendered, a celebration song turned into a threat the moment Saul starts counting thousands against ten thousands, a spear hurled twice at a man playing the harp for the very king who throws it, a bride price of a hundred Philistine foreskins set as a trap that David survives by doubling, and success that keeps multiplying no matter how many ways Saul tries to redirect it toward the Philistines instead
Jonathan's Covenant with David (vv.1-4): After David's speech to Saul (end of ch.17), "the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David" (v.1). This is one of Scripture's great friendships — a bond of souls, not merely of convenience. Jonathan "loved him as his own soul" (v.1, 3). They make a covenant (v.3), and Jonathan gives David his robe, armor, sword, bow, and belt (v.4). These are royal insignia — Jonathan is symbolically transferring his right to the throne. The crown prince recognizes God's choice and willingly yields. This is the opposite of Saul's response: where Saul clings to power, Jonathan surrenders it.
David's Success and the Women's Song (vv.5-9): David succeeds in every mission Saul gives him and is promoted over the men of war (v.5). He "behaved himself wisely" (KJV) / "was successful" (ESV) — Hebrew "maskil" = acted prudently, prospered. Upon returning from battle, the women of Israel sing: "Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands" (v.7). This is a celebration song, not a political statement — but Saul takes it as a threat: "what can he have more but the kingdom?" (v.8). From this day, "Saul eyed David" (v.9) — watched him with suspicion and jealousy. The Hebrew "ayin" (to eye) suggests hostile surveillance.
Saul's First Murder Attempts (vv.10-16): The evil spirit returns, and Saul "prophesied in the midst of the house" (KJV) / "raved within his house" (ESV) — Hebrew "yithnabe" can mean prophesied or raved ecstatically. While David plays the harp, Saul hurls his spear twice (v.11). David evades both times. Saul's fear grows because "the LORD was with him, and was departed from Saul" (v.12). Saul removes David from his presence and makes him a field commander — hoping the Philistines will kill him. Instead, David succeeds even more (v.14), and "all Israel and Judah loved David" (v.16).
Saul's Schemes: Merab and Michal (vv.17-30): Saul offers his elder daughter Merab as wife — but the condition is fighting "the LORD's battles" (v.17). Saul's hidden motive: "Let not mine hand be upon him, but let the hand of the Philistines be upon him" (v.17). He wants David killed in battle. David responds with humility (v.18), but Saul breaks his promise and gives Merab to Adriel (v.19). When Michal (Saul's younger daughter) loves David, Saul sees another opportunity: "she may be a snare to him" (v.21). He sets the bride-price at 100 Philistine foreskins — expecting David to die in the attempt (v.25). David brings 200 (v.27), doubling the requirement. Saul must give Michal to David. The result: "Saul saw and knew that the LORD was with David" (v.28) — and "Saul was David's enemy continually" (v.29).

Commentary

  • Enduring Word (David Guzik): enduringword.com Guzik notes that Jonathan's gift of his royal garments was an extraordinary act of submission to God's will. He also emphasizes that Saul's jealousy was rooted in his awareness that God had left him — he was not merely envious of David's popularity but terrified of God's obvious favor on his rival.
  • Jamieson-Fausset-Brown (1871): blueletterbible.org A critical and explanatory commentary on the whole Bible, more concise than Matthew Henry and closer to the Hebrew and Greek, with attention to historical and geographical detail. Public domain.
  • Matthew Henry's Exposition (1710): blueletterbible.org A devotional verse-by-verse commentary in the Puritan tradition, still among the most widely read three centuries on. Public domain.

Reflection

  • Friendship That Surrenders Its Own Claim (vv.1-4) — Jonathan's heart is bound to David's, and he hands over his royal robe and weapons — his own claim to the throne — without being asked. Is your love for others generous enough to give away something valuable, or do you keep score of what you get in return?
  • When Someone Else's Success Feels Threatening (v.9) — After hearing the women's song crediting David with even greater victories than his own, Saul begins watching him with suspicion instead of celebrating him. Whose success tempts you to feel threatened rather than glad?
  • Playing Music for a Man Who Wants You Dead (v.11) — David keeps serving Saul with his harp even after Saul hurls a spear at him, not once but twice. How do you respond when the people you're trying to help or serve turn on you?
  • A Trap Disguised as an Opportunity (vv.20-25) — Saul offers his daughter Michal in marriage but sets a bride price he expects will get David killed in battle. Have you ever seen a generous-looking offer turn out to have hidden motives, and how did you discern the difference?
  • Seeing the Truth and Still Refusing to Change (v.29) — Saul recognizes plainly that the Lord is with David, yet that recognition only deepens his hostility instead of leading him to repentance. When you sense God at work in a situation you don't like, do you yield to it or dig in against it?
