Daniel — Chapter 12

1“At that time Michael, the great prince who stands watch over your people, will rise up. There will be a time of distress, the likes of which will not have occurred from the beginning of nations until that time. But at that time your people—everyone whose name is found written in the book—will be delivered.

2And many who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake, some to everlasting life, but others to shame and everlasting contempt.

3Then the wise will shine like the brightness of the heavens, and those who lead many to righteousness will shine like the stars forever and ever.

4But you, Daniel, shut up these words and seal the book until the time of the end. Many will roam to and fro, and knowledge will increase.”

5Then I, Daniel, looked and saw two others standing there, one on this bank of the river and one on the opposite bank.

6One of them said to the man dressed in linen, who was above the waters of the river, “How long until the fulfillment of these wonders?”

7And the man dressed in linen, who was above the waters of the river, raised his right hand and his left hand toward heaven, and I heard him swear by Him who lives forever, saying, “It will be for a time, and times, and half a time. When the power of the holy people has finally been shattered, all these things will be completed.”

8I heard, but I did not understand. So I asked, “My lord, what will be the outcome of these things?”

9“Go on your way, Daniel,” he replied, “for the words are closed up and sealed until the time of the end.

10Many will be purified, made spotless, and refined, but the wicked will continue to act wickedly. None of the wicked will understand, but the wise will understand.

11And from the time the daily sacrifice is abolished and the abomination of desolation set up, there will be 1,290 days.

12Blessed is he who waits and reaches the end of the 1,335 days.

13But as for you, go on your way until the end. You will rest, and then you will arise to your inheritance at the end of the days.”

Summary

At that time, Michael, the great prince who watches over Israel, will arise during a time of distress unmatched in history, and everyone whose name is found written in the book will be delivered (v. 1). The dead will be raised, some to everlasting life and some to shame and everlasting contempt, and the wise who lead others to righteousness will shine like the stars forever (vv. 2-3). Daniel is told to seal up the scroll until the time of the end, when knowledge will increase (v. 4). Asked how long until these wonders are fulfilled, Daniel is told it will be for a time, times, and half a time, ending when the power of the holy people is fully shattered (vv. 5-7). Though he does not understand, Daniel is told the words are sealed until the end, when the wicked will keep acting wickedly but the wise will understand; he is given the numbers 1,290 and 1,335 days, with blessing on those who wait for the latter (vv. 8-12). Daniel himself is told to go his way until the end, and that he will rest and then rise to receive his inheritance at the end of days (v. 13).

Authorship & Background

Author: Daniel son of nobility, exiled to Babylon in 605 BC. This chapter concludes the vision begun in chapter 10 (536 BC). Daniel was approximately 85 years old — this is his final recorded revelation. Written in Hebrew, addressing Israel's ultimate destiny.
Classification: Apocalyptic Prophecy / Eschatology Key Themes: The great tribulation, bodily resurrection, eternal destinies (life vs. contempt), the sealed book, the blessing of patient waiting, Daniel's personal assurance of resurrection, the wise shining like stars
Historical Context: Chapter 12 completes the longest continuous prophecy in Daniel (chs.10-12). After detailing centuries of political conflict (ch.11), the vision now leaps to "the end" — the ultimate resolution. Michael "stands up" (v.1) — no longer merely assisting but taking full protective action over Israel during the worst crisis in human history.
The resurrection promise (v.2) is the clearest Old Testament statement of bodily resurrection. Job hinted at it (Job 19:26), Isaiah proclaimed it (Isaiah 26:19), but Daniel DETAILS it: many (not all — suggesting a selective resurrection or a first and second resurrection as in Revelation 20) awake from "the dust of the earth" to two contrasting eternities. This was revolutionary theology — the Sadducees later denied it; the Pharisees affirmed it (Acts 23:8).
The "sealed book" (v.4) indicates these prophecies would become CLEARER as history approached their fulfillment. Contrast with Revelation 22:10: "Seal NOT the sayings of the prophecy of this book: for the time is at hand." Daniel seals; John unseals — because the end has drawn near.
Structure:
  • Michael Stands Up: "A Time of Trouble" (v.1)
  • The Resurrection: Two Eternities (v.2)
  • The Wise Shine Like Stars (v.3)
  • "Seal the Book" Until the End (v.4)
  • "How Long?" — Two Angels and the Man in Linen (vv.5-7)
  • Daniel's Final Question and God's Answer (vv.8-10)
  • Time Markers: 1,290 and 1,335 Days (vv.11-12)
  • "Blessed Is He That Waiteth" (v.12)
  • Daniel's Personal Promise: "Thou Shalt Rest and Rise" (v.13)

Reflection

  • Written in the Book (v.1) — Deliverance in the coming time of trouble belongs specifically to everyone found written in God's book. Do you have settled assurance that your name is secured there, or does that question still linger unresolved for you?
  • Made to Shine, Not Just Survive (v.3) — The wise are promised they will shine like the brightness of the sky, and those who turn others to righteousness shine like stars forever. Who are you actively investing in and pointing toward righteousness right now?
  • Living With Urgency as Knowledge Increases (v.4) — The sealed prophecy is tied to an age marked by widespread travel and increasing knowledge. If you believe you're living in such a time, does that shape the urgency with which you're using what you already know to be true?
  • The Blessing of Waiting (v.12) — Those who wait and reach the appointed time are specifically called blessed, framing patience as a rewarded act of faith rather than mere passivity. In a culture built for instant results, can you wait on God's timing without losing trust?
  • A Reserved Place Beyond Death (v.13) — Daniel personally is promised rest and then standing in his own inheritance at the end of days. Does the certainty of your own reserved place in eternity change how you view the hardships of today?
