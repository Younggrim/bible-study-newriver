The Trinity

All Subjects
God the Father God the Son God the Holy Spirit In the Old Testament In the New Testament How They Work Together

One God in three persons — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — equal in nature, distinct in person, united in purpose. The Trinity is not three gods, and not one God wearing three masks, but one God who eternally exists as three co-equal, co-eternal persons in perfect love and unity. This page traces that mystery through Scripture: who each person is, how the doctrine unfolds across the Old and New Testaments, and how the three work together as one.

The First Person

God the Father

God the Father is the first person of the Trinity — the source and initiator of all things. He is the one Jesus addressed in prayer, the one who sent the Son into the world, and the one who pours out the Holy Spirit on believers. He is not distant or detached but intimately involved in creation, redemption, and the daily lives of His children.

The title "Father" reveals God's relational nature. He is not merely a creator or a force but a person who desires relationship with those He has made. Through Christ, we are adopted into His family and given the right to call Him "Abba" — an intimate term of affection and trust that transforms our understanding of the Almighty from fearful distance to familial closeness.

The Father's love is the origin of everything good in the gospel. It was the Father who "so loved the world" that He gave His Son. It was the Father who planned redemption before the foundation of the world. It was the Father who chose us, adopted us, and blessed us with every spiritual blessing. His love is not reactive but initiating — reaching toward us before we ever thought to reach toward Him.

Key Scriptures

  • Matthew 6:9 — "Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name."
  • John 3:16 — "For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son."
  • Romans 8:15 — "The Spirit you received brought about your adoption to sonship. And by him we cry, Abba, Father."
  • 1 John 3:1 — "See what great love the Father has lavished on us, that we should be called children of God!"
  • Ephesians 1:3-6 — "Praise be to the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in the heavenly realms."

Significance for Us

Understanding God as Father transforms our identity. We are not orphans struggling to earn approval but beloved children secure in a Father's love. This changes everything: our prayers become conversation rather than petition to a stranger, our obedience flows from love rather than fear, and our failures are met with discipline rather than rejection.

The Fatherhood of God also gives us a model for earthly relationships. His patience, generosity, discipline, and unconditional commitment show us what love looks like when it is perfected. Even those with imperfect earthly fathers can find healing and wholeness in the perfect Fatherhood of God — the Father from whom every family derives its name.

Reflection Questions

  • How does your relationship with your earthly father affect your view of God as Father?
  • What does it mean to you personally that God chose to adopt you as His child?
  • How might a deeper understanding of God's fatherly love change the way you pray?

The Second Person

God the Son

God the Son — Jesus Christ — is the second person of the Trinity: eternally God, yet one who became human to reveal the Father and redeem humanity. He is not a created being but the Creator Himself, through whom all things were made and in whom all things hold together. The Son shares fully in the divine nature from all eternity.

The incarnation is the great mystery of the Son: the eternal Word became flesh. Without ceasing to be God, He became fully human — experiencing hunger, fatigue, sorrow, temptation, and ultimately death. This was not a diminishment but a deliberate descent motivated by love, making it possible for God and humanity to be reconciled through His sacrifice.

Jesus is the perfect revelation of God. When Philip asked to see the Father, Jesus replied, "Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father." In Christ's compassion, we see God's compassion. In Christ's justice, we see God's justice. In Christ's sacrifice, we see God's love. The Son makes the invisible God visible, the unknowable God knowable, the distant God near.

Key Scriptures

  • John 1:1-14 — "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God... The Word became flesh."
  • Colossians 1:15-17 — "The Son is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation. In him all things hold together."
  • Hebrews 1:3 — "The Son is the radiance of God's glory and the exact representation of his being."
  • Philippians 2:5-11 — "Who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be grasped... he humbled himself."
  • John 14:9 — "Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father."

Significance for Us

The Son's significance for us is total: He is our salvation, our mediator, our example, and our hope. Through His death, we are forgiven. Through His resurrection, we have new life. Through His intercession, we have access to the Father. Through His return, we have a future. Every aspect of the Christian life is anchored in the person and work of God the Son.

Because the Son became human, He understands our weaknesses and sympathizes with our struggles. We do not have a distant God who cannot relate to human experience — we have a Savior who was tempted in every way we are, yet without sin. This gives us confidence to approach God's throne boldly, knowing we will find grace and help in our time of need.

Reflection Questions

  • How does the fact that Jesus is fully God affect your trust in His sacrifice for you?
  • What does it mean to you that Jesus understands human weakness from personal experience?
  • How does seeing Jesus as the exact representation of God change your view of God's character?

The Third Person

God the Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is the third person of the Trinity — fully God, fully personal, and actively present in the life of every believer. He is not a force or an influence but a divine person who thinks, feels, speaks, and acts. Jesus called Him "another Advocate" — another of the same kind as Himself — who would be with believers forever.

The Spirit's role is multifaceted: He convicts of sin, regenerates hearts, indwells believers, produces spiritual fruit, distributes gifts for service, illuminates Scripture, guides into truth, empowers for witness, and intercedes in prayer. He is the immediate presence of God within us — the one who makes the Christian life possible from the inside out.

Before His ascension, Jesus told the disciples it was actually to their advantage that He was leaving — because the Spirit would come. While Jesus could only be in one place at one time in His human body, the Spirit would indwell every believer simultaneously, making God's presence constant, universal, and internal rather than external and localized.

Key Scriptures

  • John 14:16-17 — "I will ask the Father, and he will give you another advocate to help you and be with you forever — the Spirit of truth."
  • Acts 1:8 — "You will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you; and you will be my witnesses."
  • Romans 8:26-27 — "The Spirit himself intercedes for us through wordless groans."
  • Galatians 5:22-23 — "The fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, forbearance, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control."
  • John 16:13 — "When he, the Spirit of truth, comes, he will guide you into all the truth."

Significance for Us

The Holy Spirit's significance for us is practical and immediate. He is our daily companion, our source of power for holy living, our teacher who illuminates truth, and our comforter who sustains us through difficulty. Without the Spirit, the Christian life would be mere rule-following; with Him, it becomes a supernatural partnership with God Himself.

The Spirit also connects us to the broader body of Christ, distributing gifts that build up the community and empowering us for mission in the world. He is the one who produces Christ-like character in us — not through our striving but through our surrender. As we walk in step with the Spirit, His fruit naturally grows in our lives.

Reflection Questions

  • How aware are you of the Holy Spirit's presence and activity in your daily life?
  • Which fruit of the Spirit do you most need the Spirit to develop in you right now?
  • How might you more consistently yield to the Spirit's guidance and power?

Tracing the Doctrine

Trinity in the Old Testament

While the doctrine of the Trinity is fully revealed in the New Testament, its seeds are planted throughout the Old Testament. From the very first chapter of Genesis, God speaks in the plural: "Let us make mankind in our image." This plurality within unity hints at the multi-personal nature of God that would later be revealed as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

The Old Testament contains numerous passages where God appears to speak to another divine person, where the Spirit of God acts as a distinct agent, and where mysterious figures — like the Angel of the Lord — receive worship reserved for God alone. These passages puzzled ancient interpreters but make perfect sense in light of the Trinity.

Isaiah 48:16 is particularly striking: "And now the Sovereign LORD has sent me, with his Spirit." Here a divine speaker distinguishes Himself from both the LORD who sends and the Spirit who accompanies — a clearly Trinitarian structure embedded in the Hebrew prophet's oracle. The Trinity did not begin at Pentecost; it was simply fully revealed there.

Key Scriptures

  • Genesis 1:26 — "Let us make mankind in our image, in our likeness" — God speaks in plural.
  • Isaiah 48:16 — "And now the Sovereign LORD has sent me, with his Spirit" — a Trinitarian statement from Isaiah.
  • Genesis 18:1-2 — "The LORD appeared to Abraham near the great trees of Mamre" — a theophany revealing God's nature.
  • Psalm 110:1 — "The LORD says to my lord: Sit at my right hand" — David recognizes two distinct persons.
  • Isaiah 63:10 — "They rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit" — the Spirit presented as a distinct person who can be grieved.

Significance for Us

Recognizing the Trinity in the Old Testament shows us that God has always been Triune — Father, Son, and Spirit working together in creation, revelation, and redemption from the very beginning. The Old Testament is not a different God from the New but the same Triune God progressively revealing His nature across redemptive history.

This also strengthens our confidence in the unity of Scripture. The same divine community that creates in Genesis, speaks through prophets in Isaiah, and appears to Abraham in Mamre is the same Trinity that incarnates in Christ, descends at Pentecost, and indwells believers today. The Bible tells one coherent story of one Triune God's love for His creation.

Reflection Questions

  • How does seeing hints of the Trinity in the Old Testament affect your view of Scripture's unity?
  • What does God's plural language in Genesis reveal about His nature?
  • How does the Trinity's presence in the Old Testament show that God has always been relational?

Tracing the Doctrine

Trinity in the New Testament

The New Testament reveals the Trinity with unmistakable clarity. At Jesus' baptism, all three persons are simultaneously present and active: the Son emerges from the water, the Spirit descends as a dove, and the Father speaks from heaven. This is not one God playing three roles but three distinct persons united in one divine being.

Jesus' farewell discourse in John 14-16 unfolds the Trinitarian relationships with beautiful intimacy. The Son asks the Father, who sends "another Advocate" — the Spirit. The Spirit glorifies the Son, the Son reveals the Father, and the Father loves the Son and those who love Him. Each person has distinct actions yet works in perfect harmony with the others.

The baptismal formula of Matthew 28:19 — "in the name (singular) of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" — captures the doctrine perfectly. One name, three persons. Not three gods, not one person wearing three masks, but one God who exists eternally as three distinct, co-equal, co-eternal persons in perfect love and unity.

Key Scriptures

  • Matthew 28:19 — "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."
  • 2 Corinthians 13:14 — "May the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all."
  • Matthew 3:16-17 — "Jesus was baptized... the Spirit descended like a dove... a voice from heaven said, This is my Son."
  • John 14:16-17 — "I will ask the Father, and he will give you another advocate — the Spirit of truth."
  • Ephesians 4:4-6 — "There is one body and one Spirit... one Lord... one God and Father of all."

Significance for Us

The New Testament revelation of the Trinity means that our entire Christian experience is Trinitarian. We are loved by the Father, saved by the Son, and empowered by the Spirit. We pray to the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit. We are chosen by the Father, redeemed by the Son, and sealed by the Spirit. Every aspect of salvation involves all three persons.

Understanding the Trinity also reveals that God is inherently relational. Before creation, within the Godhead, there was love, communication, and fellowship. God did not create us because He was lonely — He was already in perfect relationship within Himself. He created us to share in the joy of that divine love, inviting us into the fellowship of the Trinity.

Reflection Questions

  • How does the baptism of Jesus help you understand the distinct persons of the Trinity?
  • In what ways do you experience each person of the Trinity in your daily spiritual life?
  • How does the Trinitarian nature of God assure you that love is at the very heart of reality?

One God, Three Persons

How They Work Together

The three persons of the Trinity work together in perfect harmony, each with distinct roles yet united in purpose and love. The Father plans, the Son accomplishes, and the Spirit applies — yet all three are present and active in every divine work. There is no competition, no conflict, no independent action — only seamless cooperation flowing from eternal love.

In creation, the Father spoke, the Son was the Word through whom all things were made, and the Spirit hovered over the waters giving life. In redemption, the Father sent the Son, the Son obeyed and gave His life, and the Spirit applies that redemption to believers' hearts. In sanctification, the Father disciplines, the Son intercedes, and the Spirit transforms from within.

John 16:13-15 reveals the beautiful dynamic: the Spirit glorifies the Son by taking what belongs to Christ and making it known to believers. The Son glorifies the Father by doing His will perfectly. The Father glorifies the Son by exalting Him above every name. This mutual glorification within the Trinity is the model for all healthy relationships — others-centered love.

Key Scriptures

  • John 16:13-15 — "The Spirit will glorify me because it is from me that he will receive what he will make known to you."
  • Romans 8:9-11 — "If the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead is living in you, he who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies."
  • 1 Corinthians 12:4-6 — "There are different kinds of gifts, but the same Spirit... the same Lord... the same God."
  • 2 Corinthians 13:14 — "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all."
  • Ephesians 1:3-14 — "In him we were chosen... to the praise of his glory... sealed with the Holy Spirit."

Significance for Us

Understanding how the Trinity works together gives us confidence that every aspect of our salvation is secure. The Father chose us, the Son redeemed us, and the Spirit sealed us. If our salvation depended on any one of these alone, perhaps we might worry. But with all three persons of the Godhead committed to our redemption, nothing can separate us from God's love.

The Trinity's cooperative work also models community for us. Just as the three persons serve, honor, and glorify one another rather than competing, we are called to the same in the body of Christ. The church at its best reflects Trinitarian love — diverse persons working in unity, each contributing unique gifts for the flourishing of all, with no one seeking their own glory.

Reflection Questions

  • How does the Trinity's cooperative work give you confidence in your salvation?
  • In what ways does the Trinity model healthy relationships and community for you?
  • How can your church community better reflect the mutual love and service of the Trinity?
