1This is the account of Aaron and Moses at the time the LORD spoke with Moses on Mount Sinai.

2These are the names of the sons of Aaron: Nadab the firstborn, then Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.

3These were Aaron’s sons, the anointed priests, who were ordained to serve as priests.

4Nadab and Abihu, however, died in the presence of the LORD when they offered unauthorized fire before the LORD in the Wilderness of Sinai. And since they had no sons, only Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests during the lifetime of their father Aaron.

5Then the LORD said to Moses,

6“Bring the tribe of Levi and present them to Aaron the priest to assist him.

7They are to perform duties for him and for the whole congregation before the Tent of Meeting, attending to the service of the tabernacle.

8They shall take care of all the furnishings of the Tent of Meeting and fulfill obligations for the Israelites by attending to the service of the tabernacle.

9Assign the Levites to Aaron and his sons; they have been given exclusively to him from among the Israelites.

10So you shall appoint Aaron and his sons to carry out the duties of the priesthood; but any outsider who approaches the tabernacle must be put to death.”

11Again the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

12“Behold, I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel in place of every firstborn Israelite from the womb. The Levites belong to Me,

13for all the firstborn are Mine. On the day I struck down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, I consecrated to Myself all the firstborn in Israel, both man and beast. They are Mine; I am the LORD.”

14Then the LORD spoke to Moses in the Wilderness of Sinai, saying,

15“Number the Levites by their families and clans. You are to count every male a month old or more.”

16So Moses numbered them according to the word of the LORD, as he had been commanded.

17These were the sons of Levi by name: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.

18These were the names of the sons of Gershon by their clans: Libni and Shimei.

19The sons of Kohath by their clans were Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel.

20And the sons of Merari by their clans were Mahli and Mushi. These were the clans of the Levites, according to their families.

21From Gershon came the Libnite clan and the Shimeite clan; these were the Gershonite clans.

22The number of all the males a month old or more was 7,500.

23The Gershonite clans were to camp on the west, behind the tabernacle,

24and the leader of the families of the Gershonites was Eliasaph son of Lael.

25The duties of the Gershonites at the Tent of Meeting were the tabernacle and tent, its covering, the curtain for the entrance to the Tent of Meeting,

26the curtains of the courtyard, the curtain for the entrance to the courtyard that surrounds the tabernacle and altar, and the cords—all the service for these items.

27From Kohath came the clans of the Amramites, the Izharites, the Hebronites, and the Uzzielites; these were the clans of the Kohathites.

28The number of all the males a month old or more was 8,600. They were responsible for the duties of the sanctuary.

29The clans of the Kohathites were to camp on the south side of the tabernacle,

30and the leader of the families of the Kohathites was Elizaphan son of Uzziel.

31Their duties were the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars, the articles of the sanctuary used with them, and the curtain—all the service for these items.

32The chief of the leaders of the Levites was Eleazar son of Aaron the priest; he oversaw those responsible for the duties of the sanctuary.

33From Merari came the clans of the Mahlites and Mushites; these were the Merarite clans.

34The number of all the males a month old or more was 6,200.

35The leader of the families of the Merarites was Zuriel son of Abihail; they were to camp on the north side of the tabernacle.

36The duties assigned to the sons of Merari were the tabernacle’s frames, crossbars, posts, bases, and all its equipment—all the service for these items,

37as well as the posts of the surrounding courtyard with their bases, tent pegs, and ropes.

38Moses, Aaron, and Aaron’s sons were to camp to the east of the tabernacle, toward the sunrise, before the Tent of Meeting. They were to perform the duties of the sanctuary as a service on behalf of the Israelites; but any outsider who approached the sanctuary was to be put to death.

39The total number of Levites that Moses and Aaron counted by their clans at the LORD’s command, including all the males a month old or more, was 22,000.

40Then the LORD said to Moses, “Number every firstborn male of the Israelites a month old or more, and list their names.

41You are to take the Levites for Me—I am the LORD—in place of all the firstborn of Israel, and the livestock of the Levites in place of all the firstborn of the livestock of the Israelites.”

42So Moses numbered all the firstborn of the Israelites, as the LORD had commanded him.

43The total number of the firstborn males a month old or more, listed by name, was 22,273.

44Again the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

45“Take the Levites in place of all the firstborn of Israel, and the livestock of the Levites in place of their livestock. The Levites belong to Me; I am the LORD.

46To redeem the 273 firstborn Israelites who outnumber the Levites,

47you are to collect five shekels for each one, according to the sanctuary shekel of twenty gerahs.

48Give the money to Aaron and his sons as the redemption price for the excess among the Israelites.”

49So Moses collected the redemption money from those in excess of the number redeemed by the Levites.

50He collected the money from the firstborn of the Israelites: 1,365 shekels, according to the sanctuary shekel.

51And Moses gave the redemption money to Aaron and his sons in obedience to the word of the LORD, just as the LORD had commanded him.

1These also are the generations of Aaron and Moses in the day that the LORD spake with Moses in mount Sinai.

2And these are the names of the sons of Aaron; Nadab the firstborn, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.

3These are the names of the sons of Aaron, the priests which were anointed, whom he consecrated to minister in the priest’s office.

4And Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD, when they offered strange fire before the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no children: and Eleazar and Ithamar ministered in the priest’s office in the sight of Aaron their father.

5And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

6Bring the tribe of Levi near, and present them before Aaron the priest, that they may minister unto him.

7And they shall keep his charge, and the charge of the whole congregation before the tabernacle of the congregation, to do the service of the tabernacle.

8And they shall keep all the instruments of the tabernacle of the congregation, and the charge of the children of Israel, to do the service of the tabernacle.

9And thou shalt give the Levites unto Aaron and to his sons: they are wholly given unto him out of the children of Israel.

10And thou shalt appoint Aaron and his sons, and they shall wait on their priest’s office: and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.

11And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

12And I, behold, I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel instead of all the firstborn that openeth the matrix among the children of Israel: therefore the Levites shall be mine;

13Because all the firstborn are mine; for on the day that I smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I hallowed unto me all the firstborn in Israel, both man and beast: mine shall they be: I am the LORD.

14And the LORD spake unto Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, saying,

15Number the children of Levi after the house of their fathers, by their families: every male from a month old and upward shalt thou number them.

16And Moses numbered them according to the word of the LORD, as he was commanded.

17And these were the sons of Levi by their names; Gershon, and Kohath, and Merari.

18And these are the names of the sons of Gershon by their families; Libni, and Shimei.

19And the sons of Kohath by their families; Amram, and Izehar, Hebron, and Uzziel.

20And the sons of Merari by their families; Mahli, and Mushi. These are the families of the Levites according to the house of their fathers.

21Of Gershon was the family of the Libnites, and the family of the Shimites: these are the families of the Gershonites.

22Those that were numbered of them, according to the number of all the males, from a month old and upward, even those that were numbered of them were seven thousand and five hundred.

23The families of the Gershonites shall pitch behind the tabernacle westward.

24And the chief of the house of the father of the Gershonites shall be Eliasaph the son of Lael.

25And the charge of the sons of Gershon in the tabernacle of the congregation shall be the tabernacle, and the tent, the covering thereof, and the hanging for the door of the tabernacle of the congregation,

26And the hangings of the court, and the curtain for the door of the court, which is by the tabernacle, and by the altar round about, and the cords of it for all the service thereof.

27And of Kohath was the family of the Amramites, and the family of the Izeharites, and the family of the Hebronites, and the family of the Uzzielites: these are the families of the Kohathites.

28In the number of all the males, from a month old and upward, were eight thousand and six hundred, keeping the charge of the sanctuary.

29The families of the sons of Kohath shall pitch on the side of the tabernacle southward.

30And the chief of the house of the father of the families of the Kohathites shall be Elizaphan the son of Uzziel.

31And their charge shall be the ark, and the table, and the candlestick, and the altars, and the vessels of the sanctuary wherewith they minister, and the hanging, and all the service thereof.

32And Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest shall be chief over the chief of the Levites, and have the oversight of them that keep the charge of the sanctuary.

33Of Merari was the family of the Mahlites, and the family of the Mushites: these are the families of Merari.

34And those that were numbered of them, according to the number of all the males, from a month old and upward, were six thousand and two hundred.

35And the chief of the house of the father of the families of Merari was Zuriel the son of Abihail: these shall pitch on the side of the tabernacle northward.

36And under the custody and charge of the sons of Merari shall be the boards of the tabernacle, and the bars thereof, and the pillars thereof, and the sockets thereof, and all the vessels thereof, and all that serveth thereto,

37And the pillars of the court round about, and their sockets, and their pins, and their cords.

38But those that encamp before the tabernacle toward the east, even before the tabernacle of the congregation eastward, shall be Moses, and Aaron and his sons, keeping the charge of the sanctuary for the charge of the children of Israel; and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.

39All that were numbered of the Levites, which Moses and Aaron numbered at the commandment of the LORD, throughout their families, all the males from a month old and upward, were twenty and two thousand.

40And the LORD said unto Moses, Number all the firstborn of the males of the children of Israel from a month old and upward, and take the number of their names.

41And thou shalt take the Levites for me (I am the LORD) instead of all the firstborn among the children of Israel; and the cattle of the Levites instead of all the firstlings among the cattle of the children of Israel.

42And Moses numbered, as the LORD commanded him, all the firstborn among the children of Israel.

43And all the firstborn males by the number of names, from a month old and upward, of those that were numbered of them, were twenty and two thousand two hundred and threescore and thirteen.

44And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

45Take the Levites instead of all the firstborn among the children of Israel, and the cattle of the Levites instead of their cattle; and the Levites shall be mine: I am the LORD.

46And for those that are to be redeemed of the two hundred and threescore and thirteen of the firstborn of the children of Israel, which are more than the Levites;

47Thou shalt even take five shekels apiece by the poll, after the shekel of the sanctuary shalt thou take them: (the shekel is twenty gerahs:)

48And thou shalt give the money, wherewith the odd number of them is to be redeemed, unto Aaron and to his sons.

49And Moses took the redemption money of them that were over and above them that were redeemed by the Levites:

50Of the firstborn of the children of Israel took he the money; a thousand three hundred and threescore and five shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary:

51And Moses gave the money of them that were redeemed unto Aaron and to his sons, according to the word of the LORD, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Summary

Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu die for offering unauthorized fire before the Lord, leaving Eleazar and Ithamar to serve as priests alongside their father (vv. 1-4). God sets the tribe of Levi apart to assist Aaron and care for the tabernacle, dividing them into three clans — Gershon, Kohath, and Merari — each assigned specific furnishings and duties to guard and carry (vv. 5-37). The Levites are formally given to serve in place of Israel's firstborn sons, who technically belonged to God since the exodus (vv. 11-13, 40-45). Because the Levites (22,000) fall short of the number of firstborn Israelites (22,273), the extra 273 are redeemed with a five-shekel payment collected and given to Aaron (vv. 46-51).

Authorship & Background

Author: Moses. Numbers is the fourth book of the Pentateuch, written during the wilderness period (approximately 1445-1405 BC). Hebrew title: "Bemidbar" — "In the wilderness." The book records Israel's 38 years of wilderness wandering between Sinai and the Promised Land — a journey that should have taken 11 days (Deuteronomy 1:2) but took 40 years due to unbelief.
Historical Context: Chapter 3 focuses entirely on the Levites — their genealogy, their numbering, their camp positions, their duties, and their theological significance as substitutes for the firstborn. After the death of Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10), only Eleazar and Ithamar remain as Aaron's priestly sons. The Levites as a whole tribe are given to Aaron to assist in Tabernacle service. The key theological principle: God claims all the firstborn of Israel as His own (based on the Passover — Exodus 13:2), but He accepts the Levites as substitutes. This is the principle of substitution — one standing in the place of another — which runs throughout Scripture and culminates in Christ. The Levites are numbered differently from the other tribes: every male from one month old (not twenty years), because this is not a military census but a sacred census. Total: 22,000 Levites. Three clans — Gershon, Kohath, Merari — each with specific duties and camp positions around the Tabernacle.
Classification: Historical Narrative
Key Themes: a priestly genealogy that opens by naming two sons and then removing them in the same breath, Nadab and Abihu dead without children, an entire tribe of Levites handed over to Aaron as a gift rather than hired as staff, redemption of the firstborn grounded explicitly in the night the LORD spared Israel's own firstborn in Egypt, three Levite clans assigned three different regions of the camp and three different sets of Tabernacle furniture to guard, and a public headcount that comes up two hundred seventy-three short, redeemed at five shekels a head rather than simply waived
The Sons of Aaron, and Two Who Died (vv.1-4): The chapter opens with Aaron's four sons named in order, and then two of them removed in the same breath: Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD, when they offered strange fire in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no children. The census of a priestly family begins by recording a gap in it. Eleazar and Ithamar are what is left, and the entire later priesthood descends from those two.
The Levites Given to Aaron (vv.5-10): God commands: "Bring the tribe of Levi near" (v.6). They are to minister to Aaron, keep charge of the Tabernacle, guard its furnishings, and serve the congregation (vv.7-8). "Thou shalt give the Levites unto Aaron and to his sons: they are wholly given unto him" (v.9). The Levites are a gift from God to the priests. Aaron and his sons guard the priesthood itself; "the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death" (v.10).
The Levites Instead of the Firstborn (vv.11-13): I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel instead of all the firstborn. The substitution is grounded in the night of the exodus, on the day that I smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I hallowed unto me all the firstborn in Israel. God's claim on every eldest son is real and is being met by a tribe standing in their place, which is why the Levites are described as given rather than employed.
The Levite Census (vv.14-20): God commands a separate census of Levi — every male from one month old and upward (v.15). Not a military count but a sacred count. Three sons of Levi: Gershon, Kohath, Merari (v.17). Their sub-clans are listed (vv.18-20).
Gershonites — West Side (vv.21-26): Two clans: Libnites and Shimeites (v.21). Number: 7,500 males (v.22). Camp position: behind (west of) the Tabernacle (v.23). Chief: Eliasaph son of Lael (v.24). Duty: the Tabernacle coverings — the tent, its covering, the entrance screen, the court hangings, the court entrance screen, and the cords (vv.25-26).
Kohathites — South Side (vv.27-32): Four clans: Amramites, Izharites, Hebronites, Uzzielites (v.27). Number: 8,600 males (v.28). Camp position: south side (v.29). Chief: Elizaphan son of Uzziel (v.30). Duty: the most holy objects — the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars, the sacred vessels, and the screen (v.31). Eleazar son of Aaron is chief over all the Levite chiefs (v.32). The Kohathites have the most sacred responsibility — they handle the holiest items.
Merarites — North Side (vv.33-37): Two clans: Mahlites and Mushites (v.33). Number: 6,200 males (v.34). Camp position: north side (v.35). Chief: Zuriel son of Abihail (v.35). Duty: the structural components — frames, bars, pillars, bases, and all accessories; also the court pillars, bases, pegs, and cords (vv.36-37).
Moses and Aaron — East Side (v.38): Moses, Aaron, and his sons camp on the east (the entrance side) — guarding the sanctuary itself "for the charge of the children of Israel" (v.38). The east is the most vulnerable position (the entrance) and the most honored.
The Redemption Count (vv.39-51): The arithmetic is done in public and it does not come out even. Twenty-two thousand Levites against twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three firstborn, leaving two hundred and seventy-three unaccounted for. Those are redeemed at five shekels a head, one thousand three hundred and sixty-five shekels paid to Aaron. A doctrine of substitution is settled here by counting heads and finding a shortfall, and the shortfall is paid rather than waived.

Deep Dive

Setting: Still at Mount Sinai, in the second year after the exodus. Chapter 1 counted Israel's fighting men and set the tribe of Levi aside, and chapter 2 placed the twelve tribes around the tabernacle. Chapter 3 now fills in the inner ring: the priests (Aaron's family) and the Levites (the rest of the tribe of Levi), who camp right around the sanctuary. It answers two questions. What do the Levites do? They serve the priests and guard and carry the tabernacle. And why them? Because God takes them in place of Israel's firstborn sons, whom he claimed on the night of the Passover. The chapter ends with a small, exact transaction about 273 extra firstborn, and that redemption price is the heart of it.

The Sons of Aaron (vv1–4)

  • "These are the generations of Aaron and Moses" (v1). "Generations" (toledot) is the word that marks the big sections of Genesis ("these are the generations of…"). Here it introduces the priestly family. Aaron is named first, though Moses was the greater leader, because the priesthood belonged to Aaron's line. Moses' own sons, Gershom and Eliezer, are not even mentioned. They were counted as ordinary Levites (1 Chr 23:14). Moses sought no dynasty.
  • "The anointed priests, whom he ordained" (v3). "Ordained" is literally "whose hand he filled," the Hebrew idiom for installing a priest, probably from placing the first offerings in their hands (Exod 29:9, 24; Lev 8:27–33).
  • "Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD when they offered unauthorized fire" (v4). The story is in Leviticus 10:1–2. "Unauthorized fire" is esh zarah, literally "strange fire," fire God had not commanded. The same root gives the word for "outsider" (zar) in vv10 and 38, someone not authorized to come near. Nadab and Abihu were priests, yet they acted as outsiders. Their deaths are mentioned again and again (Lev 16:1; Num 26:61; 1 Chr 24:2), and the Tyndale notes connect them with Ananias and Sapphira (Acts 5:1–11) as a warning that God's holiness is not to be handled casually.
  • "And they had no children" (v4). Their line ended with them. The priesthood passes through Eleazar and Ithamar, and later history follows both families (1 Chr 24:1–6). Eleazar will succeed Aaron as high priest (20:25–28), and his son Phinehas will receive a "covenant of a perpetual priesthood" (25:13).
  • Commentators: JFB explains why the record is dated "when the LORD spoke with Moses on Mount Sinai": at that moment Aaron's family was still complete, and two of the four "enjoyed but a brief term of office." Guzik notes that the repeated mention of the deaths shows God wanted the lesson remembered, and that the priests were only one small family within the much larger tribe of Levi, so being a Levite and being a priest were not the same thing.

Duties of the Levites (vv5–39)

  • "Bring the tribe of Levi near, and set them before Aaron" (v6). "Bring near" is the language of presenting an offering. Chapter 8 will make it explicit: the Levites are presented to God as a living "wave offering" on behalf of Israel (8:11–13).
  • "That they may minister to him… keep guard" (vv6–8). Two jobs: helping the priests and guarding. The verb shamar, "keep" or "guard," and its noun mishmeret, "charge" or "guard duty," run through the whole chapter (vv7, 8, 10, 25, 28, 31, 32, 36, 38). The Levites were the sanctuary's attendants and its security detail. In later centuries they also taught the law (Deut 33:10; Neh 8:7–9) and led the temple music (1 Chr 15:16).
  • "They are wholly given to him" (v9). The Hebrew doubles the word: netunim netunim, "given, given." It is emphatic: completely handed over. The same idea returns in 8:16 and 18:6. Later, temple servants called the Nethinim, "the given ones," take their name from this root (1 Chr 9:2; Ezra 2:43).
  • "They shall guard their priesthood. But if any outsider comes near, he shall be put to death" (v10). The Levites serve, but only Aaron's sons may act as priests at the altar and inside the sanctuary. Korah, a Levite, will try to cross this line in chapter 16, and King Uzziah will try centuries later and be struck with leprosy (2 Chr 26:16–21).
  • "I have taken the Levites… instead of every firstborn" (v12). On the night of the Passover God spared Israel's firstborn when he struck Egypt's, and from then on every firstborn son belonged to him (Exod 13:1–2, 11–15). Now one tribe stands in for all of them. It is a clear picture of substitution: the Levites belong to God in place of others.
  • "On the day that I struck down all the firstborn in the land of Egypt" (v13). The Levites' whole existence is a reminder of the Passover. Every Levite at the tabernacle preached, by his presence, that Israel's sons were alive because a lamb had died in their place. "I am the LORD" closes the sentence like a seal.
  • Why Levi? Levi was not Jacob's firstborn, and Jacob's last words about him were a rebuke (Gen 49:5–7). But at the golden calf, the Levites alone rallied to Moses (Exod 32:26–29). The curse of being "scattered in Israel" became a blessing: they were spread through the land in forty-eight towns as God's servants (Josh 21).
  • "Every male from a month old and upward" (v15). The other tribes counted men of twenty and over who could fight. The Levites were not soldiers, and this count was about matching them with the firstborn, who were also counted from a month old (v40). Chapter 4 adds a second count of working Levites aged thirty to fifty.
  • Three clans (vv17–20). Levi's sons were Gershon, Kohath and Merari (Gen 46:11; Exod 6:16–19). Each clan gets a number, a camp site, a chief and a job, all in the same order. The jobs are spelled out in detail in chapter 4.
  • The Gershonites: 7,500, west (vv21–26). They camped behind the tabernacle, between it and the Rachel tribes. Their charge was the fabrics: the tabernacle's curtains and coverings, the entrance screen, the court hangings and the cords.
  • The Kohathites: 8,600, south (vv27–32). Moses and Aaron were themselves Kohathites through Amram (Exod 6:18–20), and this clan carried the holiest objects: the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars and the sacred vessels. Their chief was Elizaphan son of Uzziel (v30), one of the two cousins who carried the bodies of Nadab and Abihu out of the camp (Lev 10:4). The rebel Korah came from this clan too, from the Izharites (16:1). A Jewish tradition suggests Korah resented Elizaphan, from a younger branch, being made chief over him. That is only a guess, but the text does show that the most privileged clan produced the most dangerous rebellion. The Kohathites camped on the south, next to Reuben, and two Reubenites, Dathan and Abiram, joined Korah's revolt (16:1).
  • "8,600" (v28). Some Greek manuscripts read 8,300. In Hebrew "six" (shesh) and "three" (shalosh) are close in their consonants, and a slip here is a likely explanation of the arithmetic puzzle in v39.
  • "Eleazar… chief over the chiefs of the Levites" (v32). Aaron's son is the overseer of the whole Levitical workforce. His brother Ithamar supervises the Gershonites and Merarites (4:28, 33).
  • The Merarites: 6,200, north (vv33–37). Theirs was the heavy work: the frames, bars, pillars, bases, pegs and cords, the skeleton of the tabernacle and its courtyard. Later they were given wagons to help (7:8).
  • "Moses and Aaron and his sons… on the east" (v38). The best position, right at the only entrance. Those closest to God's presence are those who guard it, "to protect the people of Israel." The repeated warning about the outsider (vv10, 38) is not God keeping his people away for its own sake. It is mercy, keeping them from the death that careless approach would bring.
  • "Were 22,000" (v39). The three clans add up to 22,300 (7,500 + 8,600 + 6,200). Three main explanations: a copying error in one figure, most likely the Kohathites' 8,300 (see v28); the 300 were themselves firstborn Levites, who could not stand in for anyone else (an old Jewish explanation); or a round number. The first is the simplest. Also in this verse, the Hebrew text has unusual scribal dots over the words "and Aaron," one of about fifteen such places in the Hebrew Bible, probably marking words the scribes doubted. The Samaritan Pentateuch and the Syriac lack "and Aaron."
  • Commentators: JFB says the choice of Levi "did not originate in the legislative wisdom of Moses" but in God's own appointment, and reads "I am the LORD" as "I decree it to be so." Henry divides the chapter around the Levites' two roles: as "attendants and assistants to the priests" and as substitutes for the firstborn. Guzik sees the Levites' humble, unseen service as just as valuable to God as the priests' public work, and stresses that a Levite still had no right to take a priest's role. All three treat the Levites' count from one month as tied to the firstborn exchange, not to war. On the 22,000, JFB lists the same options and prefers a copyist's error, where "one letter was… taken for another of like form but smaller value." Guzik, following Wenham, settles on 8,300 for Kohath. JFB calls the east side "the post of honor." Henry notes the tribe was "mustered and marshalled by itself, and not in common with the other tribes," a mark of its "particular honour" and "particular duty."

Redemption of the Firstborn (vv40–51)

  • "List all the firstborn males… from a month old" (v40). Now Israel's firstborn are counted: 22,273 (v43). That seems low for a nation of 603,550 fighting men, and scholars have offered reasons: perhaps only firstborn sons born since the exodus were counted (Guzik's view), or only those still in their parents' homes, or the word "firstborn" here meant firstborn of the mother, not the father. The text does not say.
  • "And the cattle of the Levites instead of… the cattle" (vv41, 45). Firstborn animals also belonged to God (Exod 13:12). The Levites' livestock was accepted in place of theirs, as a one-time exchange.
  • "The 273… over and above" (v46). One Levite for one firstborn leaves 273 firstborn with no one to stand in their place. They cannot simply be overlooked. Each must be redeemed with a price.
  • "Five shekels per head… the shekel of the sanctuary" (v47). Five shekels is the value set in Leviticus 27:6 for a boy between one month and five years, and it becomes the standard price for redeeming a firstborn son (18:16). The "shekel of the sanctuary" was the official weight of twenty gerahs (Exod 30:13), so no one could pay in light coin. A shekel was about 11–12 grams of silver.
  • "1,365 shekels" (v50). That is 273 × 5, exact to the shekel. Who paid is not said. JFB passes on a Jewish tradition that lots were drawn among the firstborn to decide which ones paid, and the money went to Aaron and his sons (v51).
  • Mary and Joseph. This law stands behind Luke 2:22–23, when Mary and Joseph bring the infant Jesus to the temple "to present him to the Lord," because "every male who first opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord." God's own firstborn Son was presented under the very law that pictured redemption.
  • Commentators: Guzik observes that the extra firstborn were redeemed with silver, but believers are redeemed "not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ" (1 Pet 1:18–19). He also notes that in the new covenant all believers are priests (1 Pet 2:9), though the church still has recognized offices. JFB notes that the Levites' cattle stood in for Israel's firstlings, so "the firstlings were not brought then" to the altar. The Tyndale notes, like Guzik, see in the whole arrangement a picture of Christ's substitution.

Pointing to Christ

Every firstborn in Israel lived because a lamb died on Passover night, and every Levite was a walking reminder of it. When there were not enough substitutes, a price had to be paid, counted to the last shekel. The New Testament takes up both pictures. Jesus is the substitute who stands in our place, giving "his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45), and the price is not silver but "the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish" (1 Pet 1:18–19). Those he redeems become "the assembly of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven" (Heb 12:23), wholly given to God, and, unlike Israel's outsiders, invited to draw near.

Reflection

  • Substitution Is God's Principle (v.12) — God accepts the whole tribe of Levi as a stand-in for every firstborn son in Israel, one group taking the place of another. Do you grasp that Christ standing in your place is the same principle at its fullest, and how does that shape your gratitude?
  • Different Duties, Equal Importance (vv.25-37) — Gershonites carry curtains, Kohathites carry the ark, and Merarites carry frames — three very different jobs, all necessary for the Tabernacle to function. Do you undervalue a role in your church or family just because it looks less glamorous than someone else's?
  • Guarding What Is Holy (v.38) — Moses and Aaron camp at the entrance, and anyone unauthorized who approaches the sanctuary faces death. Do you treat access to God's presence with the seriousness it deserves, or has it become casual to you?
  • Precise Accounting (vv.43-51) — When the Levites don't exactly match the number of firstborn, the leftover 273 are redeemed individually with silver rather than rounded off or ignored. Does it change anything for you to know that God's care for you is this exact and personal?
