1Meanwhile, Joseph had been taken down to Egypt, where an Egyptian named Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard, bought him from the Ishmaelites who had taken him there.

2And the LORD was with Joseph, and he became a successful man, serving in the household of his Egyptian master.

3When his master saw that the LORD was with him and made him prosper in all he did,

4Joseph found favor in his sight and became his personal attendant. Potiphar put him in charge of his household and entrusted him with everything he owned.

5From the time that he put Joseph in charge of his household and all he owned, the LORD blessed the Egyptian’s household on account of him. The LORD’s blessing was on everything he owned, both in his house and in his field.

6So Potiphar left all that he owned in Joseph’s care; he did not concern himself with anything except the food he ate. Now Joseph was well-built and handsome,

7and after some time his master’s wife cast her eyes upon Joseph and said, “Sleep with me.”

8But he refused. “Look,” he said to his master’s wife, “with me here, my master does not concern himself with anything in his house, and he has entrusted everything he owns to my care.

9No one in this house is greater than I am. He has withheld nothing from me except you, because you are his wife. So how could I do such a great evil and sin against God?”

10Although Potiphar’s wife spoke to Joseph day after day, he refused to go to bed with her or even be near her.

11One day, however, Joseph went into the house to attend to his work, and not a single household servant was inside.

12She grabbed Joseph by his cloak and said, “Sleep with me!” But leaving his cloak in her hand, he escaped and ran outside.

13When she saw that he had left his cloak in her hand and had run out of the house,

14she called her household servants. “Look,” she said, “this Hebrew has been brought to us to make sport of us. He came to me so he could sleep with me, but I screamed as loud as I could.

15When he heard me scream for help, he left his cloak beside me and ran out of the house.”

16So Potiphar’s wife kept Joseph’s cloak beside her until his master came home.

17Then she told him the same story: “The Hebrew slave you brought us came to me to make sport of me,

18but when I screamed for help, he left his cloak beside me and ran out of the house.”

19When his master heard the story his wife told him, saying, “This is what your slave did to me,” he burned with anger.

20So Joseph’s master took him and had him thrown into the prison where the king’s prisoners were confined. While Joseph was there in the prison,

21the LORD was with him and extended kindness to him, granting him favor in the eyes of the prison warden.

22And the warden put all the prisoners under Joseph’s care, so that he was responsible for all that was done in the prison.

23The warden did not concern himself with anything under Joseph’s care, because the LORD was with Joseph and gave him success in whatever he did.

Summary

Joseph is sold to Potiphar, an Egyptian official, and the Lord causes him to prosper so that Potiphar puts him in charge of his entire household (vv. 1-6). Potiphar's wife repeatedly tries to seduce Joseph, but he refuses, insisting he will not sin against God (vv. 7-10). When Joseph flees her advances one day, she falsely accuses him of assault, using his abandoned garment as evidence (vv. 11-18). Potiphar has Joseph thrown into prison, yet the Lord remains with him there too, granting him favor with the jailer so that he is put in charge of the other prisoners (vv. 20-23).

Authorship & Background

Author: Moses (see Chapter 1 notes for full authorship details).
Historical Context: Chapter 39 resumes Joseph's story in Egypt. Sold as a slave to Potiphar, Joseph rises to manage the entire household — because "the LORD was with Joseph" (repeated four times: vv.2, 3, 21, 23). Then comes the great test: Potiphar's wife's repeated sexual advances. Joseph refuses — not because of consequences but because "how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?" (v.9). Falsely accused, he's imprisoned — but even there, "the LORD was with Joseph" (v.21). The chapter demonstrates that faithfulness doesn't prevent suffering but God's presence persists through it.
Joseph in Potiphar's House (vv.1-6): "The LORD was with Joseph" — he prospers (v.2). Potiphar sees God's blessing and promotes Joseph over everything (vv.3- 4). "The LORD blessed the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake" (v.5). Joseph is given total authority (v.6). He is "goodly" and "well favoured" (v.6).
The Temptation (vv.7-12): Potiphar's wife: "Lie with me" (v.7). Joseph refuses — three reasons: (1) his master trusts him (v.8), (2) she is another man's wife (v.9), (3) it would be sin against GOD (v.9). She persists "day by day" (v.10). He avoids her (v.10). One day she grabs his garment — he flees, leaving it behind (vv.11-12).
The False Accusation (vv.13-20): She uses the garment as "evidence" (vv.13- 15). Tells the household servants (vv.14-15). Tells Potiphar (vv.17-18). Potiphar's "wrath was kindled" — Joseph imprisoned (vv.19-20).
Joseph in Prison (vv.21-23): "The LORD was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy" (v.21). He gains favor with the prison keeper (v.21). Put in charge of all prisoners (v.22). "The LORD was with him, and that which he did, the LORD made it to prosper" (v.23).

Map & Geography

  • Egypt — Joseph is now in the house of Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, likely in or near the capital city (possibly in the Nile Delta region, where later pharaohs had residences).
  • The Egyptian prison — Joseph is placed in the royal prison where the king's prisoners are held, indicating proximity to the seat of government.

Reflection

  • The Presence That Changes Everything (v.2) — Joseph's success in Potiphar's house is tied directly to the fact that the Lord is with him. Is God's presence something you actively rely on day to day, or more of a background belief you rarely think about?
  • Sin Seen As Against God (v.9) — When pressured toward sin, Joseph's first thought is that it would be a wickedness against God, not just against Potiphar or himself. When you're tempted, is your resistance rooted in fear of consequences, or in a real awareness that it would grieve God?
  • Running Instead of Negotiating (v.12) — Joseph doesn't linger to reason with temptation — he physically flees, even at the cost of his garment. Are there situations where you need to simply leave rather than trust yourself to stay and resist?
  • Punished for Doing Right (v.20) — Joseph's integrity earns him a false accusation and prison instead of the reward he deserved. Are you willing to keep doing what's right even when it isn't rewarded fairly, or even when it costs you?
  • Present Even in the Prison (v.21) — God's presence with Joseph doesn't stop when his circumstances get worse; it follows him from the household into the prison cell. Where in your own hardship do you need to trust that God hasn't left just because your situation has gotten harder?
