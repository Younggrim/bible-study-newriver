2 Chronicles — Chapter 31

1When all this had ended, the Israelites in attendance went out to the cities of Judah and broke up the sacred pillars, chopped down the Asherah poles, and tore down the high places and altars throughout Judah and Benjamin, as well as in Ephraim and Manasseh, until they had utterly destroyed them all. Then all the Israelites returned to their cities, each to his own property.

2Hezekiah reestablished the divisions of the priests and Levites—each of them according to their duties as priests or Levites—for the burnt offerings and peace offerings, for ministry, for giving thanks, and for singing praises at the gates of the LORD’s dwelling.

3The king contributed from his own possessions for the regular morning and evening burnt offerings and for the burnt offerings on the Sabbaths, New Moons, and appointed feasts, as written in the Law of the LORD.

4Moreover, he commanded the people living in Jerusalem to make a contribution for the priests and Levites so that they could devote themselves to the Law of the LORD.

5As soon as the order went out, the Israelites generously provided the firstfruits of the grain, new wine, oil, and honey, and of all the produce of the field, and they brought in an abundance—a tithe of everything.

6And the Israelites and Judahites who lived in the cities of Judah also brought a tithe of their herds and flocks and a tithe of the holy things consecrated to the LORD their God, and they laid them in large heaps.

7In the third month they began building up the heaps, and they finished in the seventh month.

8When Hezekiah and his officials came and viewed the heaps, they blessed the LORD and His people Israel.

9Then Hezekiah questioned the priests and Levites about the heaps,

10and Azariah, the chief priest of the household of Zadok, answered him, “Since the people began to bring their contributions into the house of the LORD, we have had enough to eat, and there is plenty left over, because the LORD has blessed His people; this great abundance is what is left over.”

11Then Hezekiah commanded them to prepare storerooms in the house of the LORD, and they did so.

12And they faithfully brought in the contributions, tithes, and dedicated gifts. Conaniah the Levite was the officer in charge of them, and his brother Shimei was second.

13Jehiel, Azaziah, Nahath, Asahel, Jerimoth, Jozabad, Eliel, Ismachiah, Mahath, and Benaiah were overseers under the authority of Conaniah and his brother Shimei, by appointment of King Hezekiah and of Azariah the chief official of the house of God.

14Kore son of Imnah the Levite, the keeper of the East Gate, was in charge of the freewill offerings given to God, distributing the contributions to the LORD and the consecrated gifts.

15Under his authority, Eden, Miniamin, Jeshua, Shemaiah, Amariah, and Shecaniah faithfully distributed portions to their fellow priests in their cities, according to their divisions, old and young alike.

16In addition, they distributed portions to the males registered by genealogy who were three years of age or older—to all who would enter the house of the LORD for their daily duties for service in the responsibilities of their divisions—

17and to the priests enrolled according to their families in the genealogy, as well as to the Levites twenty years of age or older, according to their responsibilities and divisions.

18The genealogy included all the little ones, wives, sons, and daughters of the whole assembly. For they had faithfully consecrated themselves as holy.

19As for the priests, the descendants of Aaron, who lived on the farmlands around each of their cities or in any other city, men were designated by name to distribute a portion to every male among the priests and to every Levite listed by the genealogies.

20So this is what Hezekiah did throughout Judah. He did what was good and upright and true before the LORD his God.

21He acted with all his heart in every work that he began in the service of the house of God, and in the law and the commandments, in order to seek his God. And so he prospered.

15And next him were Eden, and Miniamin, and Jeshua, and Shemaiah, Amariah, and Shecaniah, in the cities of the priests, in their set office, to give to their brethren by courses, as well to the great as to the small:

16Beside their genealogy of males, from three years old and upward, even unto every one that entereth into the house of the LORD, his daily portion for their service in their charges according to their courses;

20And thus did Hezekiah throughout all Judah, and wrought that which was good and right and truth before the LORD his God.

Summary

Following the Passover, the people fan out across Judah, Benjamin, Ephraim, and Manasseh, smashing sacred pillars, cutting down Asherah poles, and tearing down every high place and altar before returning home (v. 1). Hezekiah reorganizes the priests and Levites into divisions for offering sacrifices and leading worship at the temple, and dedicates his own resources for the regular offerings prescribed in the Law (vv. 2-3). He also commands the people of Jerusalem to support the priests and Levites, and they respond with such abundant firstfruits and tithes of grain, wine, oil, honey, and livestock that the gifts pile up in heaps for months (vv. 4-8). When Hezekiah asks about the surplus, the chief priest Azariah reports that since the offerings began, the priests have had more than enough and a great store remains (vv. 9-10). Hezekiah has storage chambers prepared and appoints Levite officials, including Cononiah and Shimei, to oversee faithful distribution of the tithes to priests and Levites of every age by their genealogical divisions (vv. 11-19). The chapter closes noting that Hezekiah did what was good and right before the Lord in all he undertook, seeking Him wholeheartedly, and he prospered (vv. 20-21).

Authorship & Background

Author: Traditionally attributed to Ezra the scribe. Originally one book with 1 Chronicles. Written post-exile (approximately 450-400 BC). 2 Chronicles covers Solomon's reign through the Babylonian exile and Cyrus's decree of return (approximately 970-538 BC). The Chronicler focuses exclusively on Judah (ignoring the northern kingdom) and emphasizes Temple worship, reform movements, and the principle of 2 Chronicles 7:14: "If my people... shall humble themselves, and pray... then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land."
Historical Context: Chapter 31 records the practical outworking of the spiritual revival begun in Hezekiah's Passover (chapter 30). The pattern is consistent throughout Scripture: genuine worship produces reformation of life. The people did not merely celebrate and return to the status quo — they went out with holy zeal to destroy idolatry throughout the land. The reforms extended beyond Judah into Ephraim and Manasseh (v.1), demonstrating that the northern worshipers who attended the Passover carried the revival spirit home with them.
Hezekiah then organized the institutional support necessary for sustained worship. He reestablished the priestly and Levitical divisions (v.2), funded the sacrificial system from his own resources (v.3), and commanded the people to provide tithes and offerings for the ministers' support (v.4). The response was overwhelming — the people gave so abundantly that offerings were piled in "heaps" from the third month through the seventh month (vv.6-7), and Azariah the chief priest reported they had "enough to eat, and have left plenty" (v.10). New storerooms had to be prepared to contain the surplus (v.11).
The detailed administrative structure for distributing resources (vv.12-19) reveals Hezekiah's organizational wisdom. Named overseers managed the tithes; distribution reached every priest and Levite by name, including their families — wives, children, and dependents (v.18). No minister was overlooked; no family went unsupported. This system ensured that those who served God's house could devote themselves entirely to their sacred work without financial anxiety.
The chapter's summary (vv.20-21) provides the Chronicler's evaluation of Hezekiah's entire reform program: he did "what was good and right and truth before the LORD" and "did it with all his heart, and prospered." This summary echoes the Chronicler's theology throughout: wholehearted devotion to God produces prosperity. For the post-exilic community rebuilding under Ezra and Nehemiah, this chapter provided both a model for organizing Temple support (cf. Nehemiah 10:37-39; 13:10-13) and motivation — when God's people give generously, God's workers are sustained and God's work prospers.

Map & Geography

  • Parallels 1-2 Kings but focuses exclusively on Judah (the southern kingdom). Jerusalem and the Temple are the theological center throughout.
  • Key locations include various battle sites, reform locations, and high places destroyed or rebuilt by successive kings.
  • The book ends with exile to Babylon (586 BC) and Cyrus's decree permitting return — the geographic arc moves from Jerusalem to Babylon and back.

Commentary

  • Enduring Word (David Guzik): enduringword.com Guzik emphasizes that the people's generous giving followed genuine revival — they were not coerced but responded from transformed hearts. He notes the principle that God's workers deserve material support so they can focus on spiritual service, and highlights the organizational wisdom of Hezekiah in creating systems for faithful distribution.

Reflection

  • Worship That Overflows Into Action (v.1) — After the Passover celebration ends, the people don't just go home, they go out and tear down idols throughout the land. Does your worship ever translate into concrete changes in how you actually live during the week?
  • Freeing Others to Focus on Ministry (v.4) — Hezekiah has the people provide for the priests and Levites specifically so they can devote themselves fully to God's law. Are you supporting the people who serve you spiritually in a way that frees them for that work?
  • Giving That Comes Immediately and Freely (v.5) — As soon as the instruction goes out, the people respond abundantly without hesitation or reluctance. Does your own giving flow readily, or does it usually require convincing?
  • More Than Enough to Go Around (v.10) — The priest reports that the people's generosity has produced surplus, not scarcity, because the Lord has blessed his people. Have you seen generosity actually multiply rather than deplete what you have?
  • Wholehearted Effort Leading to Real Prosperity (v.21) — Hezekiah's success is tied directly to doing the work with all his heart, not with divided or half-hearted effort. Where in your life could you trade partial effort for genuine wholeheartedness?
