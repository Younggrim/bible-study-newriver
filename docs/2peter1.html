1Simon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ, To those who through the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ have received a faith as precious as ours:

2Grace and peace be multiplied to you through the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord.

3His divine power has given us everything we need for life and godliness through the knowledge of Him who called us by His own glory and excellence.

4Through these He has given us His precious and magnificent promises, so that through them you may become partakers of the divine nature, now that you have escaped the corruption in the world caused by evil desires.

5For this very reason, make every effort to add to your faith virtue; and to virtue, knowledge;

6and to knowledge, self-control; and to self-control, perseverance; and to perseverance, godliness;

7and to godliness, brotherly kindness; and to brotherly kindness, love.

8For if you possess these qualities and continue to grow in them, they will keep you from being ineffective and unproductive in your knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.

9But whoever lacks these traits is nearsighted to the point of blindness, having forgotten that he has been cleansed from his past sins.

10Therefore, brothers, strive to make your calling and election sure. For if you practice these things you will never stumble,

11and you will receive a lavish reception into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

12Therefore I will always remind you of these things, even though you know them and are established in the truth you now have.

13I think it is right to refresh your memory as long as I live in the tent of my body,

14because I know that this tent will soon be laid aside, as our Lord Jesus Christ has made clear to me.

15And I will make every effort to ensure that after my departure, you will be able to recall these things at all times.

16For we did not follow cleverly devised fables when we made known to you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but we were eyewitnesses of His majesty.

17For He received honor and glory from God the Father when the voice came to Him from the Majestic Glory, saying, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

18And we ourselves heard this voice from heaven when we were with Him on the holy mountain.

19We also have the word of the prophets as confirmed beyond doubt. And you will do well to pay attention to it, as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts.

20Above all, you must understand that no prophecy of Scripture comes from one’s own interpretation.

21For no such prophecy was ever brought forth by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

Summary

Peter greets his readers and reminds them that God's divine power has granted them everything needed for life and godliness through the knowledge of Christ, along with precious promises that let them share in the divine nature and escape the world's corruption (vv. 1-4). He urges them to build on their faith a chain of qualities — virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection, and love — since possessing these in increasing measure keeps a believer fruitful, while lacking them leaves a person blind and forgetful of having been cleansed from past sins (vv. 5-9). He calls them to confirm their calling and election through such diligence, promising a rich entrance into Christ's eternal kingdom (vv. 10-11). Knowing his death is near, Peter says he intends to keep reminding them of these truths so they remember after he is gone (vv. 12-15). He insists that the apostles' message rests not on cleverly invented stories but on their own eyewitness experience of Christ's majesty, including the voice they heard from heaven on the mountain of transfiguration (vv. 16-18). This confirms the prophetic word, to which readers do well to pay attention, since no prophecy of Scripture came from human will but from men who spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit (vv. 19-21).

Authorship & Background

Author: Simon Peter (v.1). Written approximately AD 65-68, shortly before Peter's martyrdom (v.14; cf. John 21:18-19). This is Peter's final letter — his "last will and testament" to the church.
Recipient: The same audience as 1 Peter (3:1) — believers scattered throughout Asia Minor.
Purpose: To warn against false teachers (ch.2) and scoffers who deny Christ's return (ch.3), while encouraging believers to grow in knowledge and godliness (ch.1). Peter knows his death is near and wants to leave a lasting reminder.
Theme: True knowledge of God vs. false teaching. "Knowledge" (gnōsis/ epignōsis) appears 16 times in 3 chapters. True knowledge produces godliness; false knowledge produces immorality.
Classification: Epistle — General
Key Themes: Everything needed for life and godliness already given, virtues added in sequence, a calling made sure, an eyewitness of the transfiguration, and prophecy that did not originate with its speakers
Greeting: Like Precious Faith (vv.1-2): Peter writes as “a servant and an apostle” and addresses those who have obtained “like precious faith” — faith of equal standing with his own. For a letter that will spend chapter 2 attacking false teachers who claimed superior knowledge, beginning by levelling himself with his readers is a considered move. Grace and peace are wished “through the knowledge of God”, and knowledge is the word the whole letter contests.
All Things Given, Great Promises (vv.3-4): The claim is that divine power has already given everything pertaining to life and godliness. Nothing further is awaited and nothing secret is missing, which cuts directly against teachers offering hidden advancement. “Partakers of the divine nature” is strong language and is bounded by the same verse: it is the means of escaping corruption, not absorption into deity. The promises are called “exceeding great and precious”, and they are the mechanism, not a reward held in reserve.
Add to Your Faith: The Chain of Virtues (vv.5-9): Seven qualities are added in sequence — virtue, knowledge, temperance, patience, godliness, brotherly kindness, charity — each supplied on top of the last, with love at the end. The construction is deliberate: this is growth with an order rather than a checklist. Verse 8 makes the promise practical, that these things keep a believer from being barren or unfruitful, and v.9 gives the alternative bluntly — whoever lacks them is blind and has forgotten he was cleansed.
Make Your Calling Sure (vv.10-15): “Give diligence to make your calling and election sure.” The certainty in view is the believer's own assurance, evidenced by the growth just described, and the promise attached is that such a person will never fall. Peter then says plainly that he intends to keep reminding them as long as he is in “this tabernacle”, and that he knows his death is near — which reads as a man deliberately leaving something behind. The letter is being written as a legacy.
We Were Eyewitnesses (vv.16-18): Against the charge of following “cunningly devised fables” Peter offers testimony: he was on the holy mount and heard the voice. The transfiguration is cited not as a mystical credential but as evidence in a dispute about reliability. He is a witness rather than a theorist, and the distinction is the point — the majesty he speaks of was seen with eyes.
Prophecy Not of Private Interpretation (vv.19-21): The written word is called “a light that shineth in a dark place” and in one sense preferred to his own experience, since it is available to every reader while the mount was not. Verse 20's “private interpretation” is most naturally about origin rather than reading method, as v.21 explains: prophecy came not by the will of man but as men spoke moved by the Holy Ghost. The argument sets a text with a divine source against teachers with only themselves to appeal to.

Reflection

  • Given Everything for Life and Godliness (v.3) — Peter says God's power has already granted believers everything needed for life and godliness through knowing Christ. Are you drawing on what's already yours, or still searching for something more?
  • Building the Ladder of Christian Character (vv.5-7) — Peter lists qualities believers should deliberately add to their faith, one building on the next, from self-control to brotherly love. Which quality on that list needs the most attention in your life right now?
  • Making Your Calling and Election Sure (v.10) — Believers are urged to confirm their calling through diligent, visible growth rather than passive assumption. Does your life produce real evidence of a growing faith?
  • Eyewitnesses of Christ's Majesty (vv.16-18) — Peter insists his message isn't invented myth but firsthand testimony of seeing Christ's glory on the mountain. Does your confidence in the gospel rest on that kind of solid ground?
  • Scripture Carried Along by the Holy Spirit (v.21) — Peter explains that Scripture didn't originate in human imagination but came as men were carried along by the Holy Spirit. Do you treat the Bible as God's word, or quietly as just another human opinion?
