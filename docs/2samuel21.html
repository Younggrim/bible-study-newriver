1During the reign of David there was a famine for three successive years, and David sought the face of the LORD. And the LORD said, “It is because of the blood shed by Saul and his family, because he killed the Gibeonites.”

2At this, David summoned the Gibeonites and spoke to them. (Now the Gibeonites were not Israelites, but a remnant of the Amorites. The Israelites had taken an oath concerning them, but in his zeal for Israel and Judah, Saul had sought to kill them.)

3So David asked the Gibeonites, “What shall I do for you? How can I make amends so that you may bless the inheritance of the LORD?”

4The Gibeonites said to him, “We need no silver or gold from Saul or his house, nor should you put to death anyone in Israel for us.” “Whatever you ask, I will do for you,” he replied.

5And they answered the king, “As for the man who consumed us and plotted against us to exterminate us from existing within any border of Israel,

6let seven of his male descendants be delivered to us so that we may hang them before the LORD at Gibeah of Saul, the chosen of the LORD.” “I will give them to you,” said the king.

7Now the king spared Mephibosheth son of Jonathan, the son of Saul, because of the oath before the LORD between David and Jonathan son of Saul.

8But the king took Armoni and Mephibosheth, the two sons whom Rizpah daughter of Aiah had borne to Saul, as well as the five sons whom Merab daughter of Saul had borne to Adriel son of Barzillai the Meholathite.

9And he delivered them into the hands of the Gibeonites, and they hanged them on the hill before the LORD. So all seven of them fell together; they were put to death in the first days of the harvest, at the beginning of the barley harvest.

10And Rizpah the daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and spread it out for herself on a rock. From the beginning of the harvest until the rain from heaven poured down on the bodies, she did not allow the birds of the air to rest on them by day, nor the beasts of the field by night.

11When David was told what Saul’s concubine Rizpah, daughter of Aiah, had done,

12he went and took the bones of Saul and his son Jonathan from the men of Jabesh-gilead, who had stolen them from the public square of Beth-shan where the Philistines had hung the bodies after they had struck down Saul at Gilboa.

13So David had the bones of Saul and his son Jonathan brought from there, and they also gathered the bones of those who had been hanged.

14And they buried the bones of Saul and his son Jonathan in Zela in the land of Benjamin, in the tomb of Saul’s father Kish. After they had done everything the king had commanded, God answered their prayers for the land.

15Once again the Philistines waged war against Israel, and David and his servants went down and fought against the Philistines. But David became exhausted.

16Then Ishbi-benob, a descendant of Rapha, whose bronze spear weighed three hundred shekels and who was bearing a new sword, resolved to kill David.

17But Abishai son of Zeruiah came to his aid, struck the Philistine, and killed him. Then David’s men swore to him, “You must never again go out with us to battle, so that the lamp of Israel may not be extinguished.”

18Some time later at Gob, there was another battle with the Philistines. At that time Sibbecai the Hushathite killed Saph, a descendant of Rapha.

19Once again there was a battle with the Philistines at Gob, and Elhanan son of Jair the Bethlehemite killed the brother of Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver’s beam.

20And there was also a battle at Gath, where there was a man of great stature with six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot—twenty-four in all. He too was descended from Rapha,

21and when he taunted Israel, Jonathan the son of David’s brother Shimei killed him.

22So these four descendants of Rapha in Gath fell at the hands of David and his servants.

Summary

A three-year famine leads David to seek God, who reveals it is due to Saul's earlier slaughter of the Gibeonites in violation of Israel's ancient covenant with them (vv. 1-2). David asks the Gibeonites what will atone for the wrong, and they request seven of Saul's male descendants be handed over for execution, which David does, sparing only Mephibosheth (vv. 3-9). Rizpah, mother of two of the executed men, guards their bodies from scavengers for months until David has them properly buried along with the bones of Saul and Jonathan (vv. 10-14). The chapter closes with four battles against Philistine giants descended from Rapha, in which David's mighty men kill each giant in turn (vv. 15-22).

Authorship & Background

Author: Unknown, traditionally attributed to Nathan and Gad (1 Chronicles 29:29). Originally one book with 1 Samuel in the Hebrew Bible. The book covers David's entire reign — from his lament over Saul's death to the purchase of the threshing floor for the future Temple (approximately 1010-970 BC). Key themes: the Davidic covenant (chapter 7), the consequences of sin even for the forgiven (chapters 11-20), God's faithfulness despite human failure, and the establishment of the eternal throne fulfilled in Christ.
Historical Context: Chapters 21-24 form an appendix to 2 Samuel, arranged thematically rather than chronologically. These chapters look back over David's reign and gather material that illustrates key theological themes: the consequences of covenant-breaking, God's deliverance, the valor of David's warriors, and the sovereignty of God over judgment and mercy. Chapter 21 addresses two distinct subjects: the famine caused by Saul's violation of the Gibeonite covenant, and the exploits of David's warriors against Philistine giants. The Gibeonite episode likely occurred early in David's reign, while the giant-slaying accounts span various periods. The theological connection between the two halves is the theme of bloodguilt and divine justice — God holds nations accountable for broken covenants, and He raises up warriors to defeat His enemies.
The Famine and the Gibeonites (vv.1-14): A three-year famine strikes Israel, and David seeks the LORD's face. God reveals the cause: Saul's violation of the ancient covenant with the Gibeonites (Joshua 9:3-27). Though the Gibeonites had deceived Joshua, Israel had sworn an oath before the LORD to spare them. Saul, in misguided "zeal" for Israel, had attempted to exterminate them — a sin God had not forgotten. The Gibeonites request seven of Saul's descendants, and David delivers them to be executed "before the LORD" at Gibeah. David spares Mephibosheth (Jonathan's son) because of his oath to Jonathan. Rizpah's vigil over the bodies (v.10) — protecting them from birds and beasts from barley harvest until the rains came — is one of the most poignant scenes in Scripture. Her devotion moves David to gather the bones of Saul and Jonathan from Jabesh-gilead and give them proper burial in the family tomb. After this, "God was intreated for the land" (v.14 KJV).
Wars Against the Philistine Giants (vv.15-22): Four battles against descendants of the giants in Gath are recorded. David himself grows weary in battle and nearly falls to Ishbi-benob, but Abishai rescues him. David's men then forbid him from going to battle again, calling him "the light of Israel" (v.17 KJV) or "the lamp of Israel" (v.17 ESV). Three other giants are slain: Saph by Sibbechai, the brother of Goliath by Elhanan, and a six-fingered giant by Jonathan (David's nephew). The summary states: "These four were born to the giant in Gath, and fell by the hand of David, and by the hand of his servants" (v.22).

Commentary

  • Enduring Word (David Guzik): enduringword.com Guzik notes that the famine was God's way of bringing an unresolved sin to light. Saul's attack on the Gibeonites was never recorded elsewhere in Scripture — it was a hidden sin that God nonetheless remembered. Guzik emphasizes that Rizpah's vigil is a picture of faithful, persistent love that moves even a king to action.
  • Jamieson-Fausset-Brown (1871): blueletterbible.org A critical and explanatory commentary on the whole Bible, more concise than Matthew Henry and closer to the Hebrew and Greek, with attention to historical and geographical detail. Public domain.
  • Matthew Henry's Exposition (1710): blueletterbible.org A devotional verse-by-verse commentary in the Puritan tradition, still among the most widely read three centuries on. Public domain.

Reflection

  • Broken Covenants Don't Stay Hidden (vv.1-2) — Saul's attack on the Gibeonites, generations after Israel had sworn to spare them, was never recorded anywhere else in Scripture, yet God still held the nation accountable for it. What promise or commitment might you assume no one remembers, that God actually does?
  • Faithful Presence in Unchangeable Grief (v.10) — Rizpah couldn't save her sons from execution, but she kept vigil over their bodies for months, refusing to let them be dishonored. When you can't fix someone's suffering, are you still willing to simply stay present with them in it?
  • Knowing Your Limits Isn't Weakness (v.17) — David grew faint in battle and nearly died, so his men insisted he stop going out to fight himself. Can you recognize when it's wisdom, not failure, to step back and let others carry a burden you once carried alone?
  • God Raises Up the Next Generation (v.22) — David could no longer fight the giants personally, but God had already prepared other warriors to finish the work. Are you investing in the people who will carry on what you can no longer do yourself?
