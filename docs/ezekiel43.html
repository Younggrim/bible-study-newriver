1Then the man brought me back to the gate that faces east,

2and I saw the glory of the God of Israel coming from the east. His voice was like the roar of many waters, and the earth shone with His glory.

3The vision I saw was like the vision I had seen when He came to destroy the city and like the visions I had seen by the River Kebar. I fell facedown,

4and the glory of the LORD entered the temple through the gate facing east.

5Then the Spirit lifted me up and brought me into the inner court, and the glory of the LORD filled the temple.

6While the man was standing beside me, I heard someone speaking to me from inside the temple,

7and He said to me, “Son of man, this is the place of My throne and the place for the soles of My feet, where I will dwell among the Israelites forever. The house of Israel will never again defile My holy name—neither they nor their kings—by their prostitution and by the funeral offerings for their kings at their deaths.

8When they placed their threshold next to My threshold and their doorposts beside My doorposts, with only a wall between Me and them, they defiled My holy name by the abominations they committed. Therefore I have consumed them in My anger.

9Now let them remove far from Me their prostitution and the funeral offerings for their kings, and I will dwell among them forever.

10As for you, son of man, describe the temple to the people of Israel, so that they may be ashamed of their iniquities. Let them measure the plan,

11and if they are ashamed of all they have done, then make known to them the design of the temple—its arrangement and its exits and entrances—its whole design along with all its statutes, forms, and laws. Write it down in their sight, so that they may keep its complete design and all its statutes and may carry them out.

12This is the law of the temple: All its surrounding territory on top of the mountain will be most holy. Yes, this is the law of the temple.

13These are the measurements of the altar in long cubits (a cubit and a handbreadth): Its gutter shall be a cubit deep and a cubit wide, with a rim of one span around its edge. And this is the height of the altar:

14The space from the gutter on the ground to the lower ledge shall be two cubits, and the ledge one cubit wide. The space from the smaller ledge to the larger ledge shall be four cubits, and the ledge one cubit wide.

15The altar hearth shall be four cubits high, and four horns shall project upward from the hearth.

16The altar hearth shall be square at its four corners, twelve cubits long and twelve cubits wide.

17The ledge shall also be square, fourteen cubits long and fourteen cubits wide, with a rim of half a cubit and a gutter of a cubit all around it. The steps of the altar shall face east.”

18Then He said to me: “Son of man, this is what the Lord GOD says: ‘These are the statutes for the altar on the day it is constructed, so that burnt offerings may be sacrificed on it and blood may be splattered on it:

19You are to give a young bull from the herd as a sin offering to the Levitical priests who are of the family of Zadok, who approach Me to minister before Me, declares the Lord GOD.

20You are to take some of its blood and put it on the four horns of the altar, on the four corners of the ledge, and all around the rim; thus you will cleanse the altar and make atonement for it.

21Then you are to take away the bull for the sin offering and burn it in the appointed part of the temple area outside the sanctuary.

22On the second day you are to present an unblemished male goat as a sin offering, and the altar is to be cleansed as it was with the bull.

23When you have finished the purification, you are to present a young, unblemished bull and an unblemished ram from the flock.

24You must present them before the LORD; the priests are to sprinkle salt on them and sacrifice them as a burnt offering to the LORD.

25For seven days you are to provide a male goat daily for a sin offering; you are also to provide a young bull and a ram from the flock, both unblemished.

26For seven days the priests are to make atonement for the altar and cleanse it; so they shall consecrate it.

27At the end of these days, from the eighth day on, the priests are to present your burnt offerings and peace offerings on the altar. Then I will accept you, declares the Lord GOD.’”

1Afterward he brought me to the gate, even the gate that looketh toward the east:

2And, behold, the glory of the God of Israel came from the way of the east: and his voice was like a noise of many waters: and the earth shined with his glory.

3And it was according to the appearance of the vision which I saw, even according to the vision that I saw when I came to destroy the city: and the visions were like the vision that I saw by the river Chebar; and I fell upon my face.

4And the glory of the LORD came into the house by the way of the gate whose prospect is toward the east.

5So the spirit took me up, and brought me into the inner court; and, behold, the glory of the LORD filled the house.

6And I heard him speaking unto me out of the house; and the man stood by me.

7And he said unto me, Son of man, the place of my throne, and the place of the soles of my feet, where I will dwell in the midst of the children of Israel for ever, and my holy name, shall the house of Israel no more defile, neither they, nor their kings, by their whoredom, nor by the carcases of their kings in their high places.

8In their setting of their threshold by my thresholds, and their post by my posts, and the wall between me and them, they have even defiled my holy name by their abominations that they have committed: wherefore I have consumed them in mine anger.

9Now let them put away their whoredom, and the carcases of their kings, far from me, and I will dwell in the midst of them for ever.

10Thou son of man, shew the house to the house of Israel, that they may be ashamed of their iniquities: and let them measure the pattern.

11And if they be ashamed of all that they have done, shew them the form of the house, and the fashion thereof, and the goings out thereof, and the comings in thereof, and all the forms thereof, and all the ordinances thereof, and all the forms thereof, and all the laws thereof: and write it in their sight, that they may keep the whole form thereof, and all the ordinances thereof, and do them.

12This is the law of the house; Upon the top of the mountain the whole limit thereof round about shall be most holy. Behold, this is the law of the house.

13And these are the measures of the altar after the cubits: The cubit is a cubit and an hand breadth; even the bottom shall be a cubit, and the breadth a cubit, and the border thereof by the edge thereof round about shall be a span: and this shall be the higher place of the altar.

14And from the bottom upon the ground even to the lower settle shall be two cubits, and the breadth one cubit; and from the lesser settle even to the greater settle shall be four cubits, and the breadth one cubit.

15So the altar shall be four cubits; and from the altar and upward shall be four horns.

16And the altar shall be twelve cubits long, twelve broad, square in the four squares thereof.

17And the settle shall be fourteen cubits long and fourteen broad in the four squares thereof; and the border about it shall be half a cubit; and the bottom thereof shall be a cubit about; and his stairs shall look toward the east.

18And he said unto me, Son of man, thus saith the Lord GOD; These are the ordinances of the altar in the day when they shall make it, to offer burnt offerings thereon, and to sprinkle blood thereon.

19And thou shalt give to the priests the Levites that be of the seed of Zadok, which approach unto me, to minister unto me, saith the Lord GOD, a young bullock for a sin offering.

20And thou shalt take of the blood thereof, and put it on the four horns of it, and on the four corners of the settle, and upon the border round about: thus shalt thou cleanse and purge it.

21Thou shalt take the bullock also of the sin offering, and he shall burn it in the appointed place of the house, without the sanctuary.

22And on the second day thou shalt offer a kid of the goats without blemish for a sin offering; and they shall cleanse the altar, as they did cleanse it with the bullock.

23When thou hast made an end of cleansing it, thou shalt offer a young bullock without blemish, and a ram out of the flock without blemish.

24And thou shalt offer them before the LORD, and the priests shall cast salt upon them, and they shall offer them up for a burnt offering unto the LORD.

25Seven days shalt thou prepare every day a goat for a sin offering: they shall also prepare a young bullock, and a ram out of the flock, without blemish.

26Seven days shall they purge the altar and purify it; and they shall consecrate themselves.

27And when these days are expired, it shall be, that upon the eighth day, and so forward, the priests shall make your burnt offerings upon the altar, and your peace offerings; and I will accept you, saith the Lord GOD.

Summary

In the climactic moment of Ezekiel's prophecy, the glory of the LORD — the same glory that departed Jerusalem through the east gate in chapters 10-11 — returns from the east and fills the new temple (vv. 1-5). God declares this to be the place of His throne, where He will dwell among the people of Israel forever, if they put away their idolatry and unfaithfulness (vv. 6-12). The chapter then gives the dimensions and consecration instructions for the great altar of burnt offering, including a seven-day ordination ceremony (vv. 13-27).

Authorship & Background

Author: Ezekiel son of Buzi (see Chapter 1 notes for full details).
Classification: Prophetic Vision — The Return of God's Glory
Key Themes: The glory of God returning, permanent divine indwelling, God's throne established forever, the altar of burnt offering dimensions and consecration, the call to repentance through the vision of holiness
Historical Context: This is the moment everything in Ezekiel has been building toward. In chapters 8-11, Ezekiel witnessed the most devastating event in Israel's spiritual history: the glory of the LORD departing from the temple — first from the cherubim to the threshold (9:3), then to the east gate (10:19), then to the mountain east of the city (11:23). God left His own house because of Israel's abominations.
The Glory Returns from the East (vv.1-5): Behold, the glory of the God of Israel came from the way of the east, and his voice was like a noise of many waters, and the earth shined with his glory. The prophet identifies it himself, and his cross-reference is exact, it was according to the vision that I saw when I came to destroy the city, and according to the vision that I saw by the river Chebar. Then the direction of travel, and the glory of the LORD came into the house by the way of the gate whose prospect is toward the east. Chapters 9 to 11 tracked the departure in three stages, threshold, east gate, mountain. It returns by the same route in reverse.
This Is My Throne, and My Holy Name (vv.6-9): Son of man, the place of my throne, and the place of the soles of my feet, where I will dwell in the midst of the children of Israel for ever. The offense that had to be cleared away is described as a matter of distance rather than of doctrine, they have set their threshold by my threshold, and their post by my post, and there was but a wall between me and them. The palace and the temple had shared a wall, and the royal graves were on the other side of it. Now let them put away their whoredom, and the carcases of their kings, far from me, and I will dwell in the midst of them for ever.
Show the House to Israel, That They May Be Ashamed (vv.10-12): This is the purpose statement for the entire survey and it is easy to miss in the middle of the measurements. Shew the house to the house of Israel, that they may be ashamed of their iniquities, and let them measure the pattern. The order is worth following: seeing the dimensions is expected to produce shame, and only then, if they be ashamed of all that they have done, shew them the form of the house. A measurable description of holiness is being offered to people who need to see the difference between it and what they had. And the summary, this is the law of the house, most holy.
The Altar Measured (vv.13-17): The altar is given its own paragraph and its own units, the bottom, the settle, the greater settle, the border round about and the four horns, rising in stages like a stepped tower. The stairs face east, which puts the officiating priest with his back to the rising sun and his face to the house, the exact reverse of the twenty-five men at 8:16.
Seven Days to Consecrate the Altar (vv.18-27): The consecration is set out as a procedure: a young bullock for a sin offering, blood put on the four horns and on the four corners of the settle and upon the border, the carcase burnt outside the sanctuary, then a kid of the goats daily for seven days, with a bullock and a ram. And on the eighth day and so forward the regular offerings begin, and I will accept you, saith the Lord GOD. It is worth being plain about what these chapters are doing here: a full sacrificial system is being restored in operational detail. Readers have taken that three ways, as a literal blueprint for a temple still to be built, as an idealized pattern never intended for construction, and as a symbolic description of later worship. The text supplies measurements and a procedure and does not supply an interpretation, and a note that pretends otherwise is adding something.

Deep Dive

Setting: The temple vision of chapters 40–48 continues, dated to the spring of 573 BC, fourteen years after Jerusalem fell (40:1). For three chapters Ezekiel's guide has measured walls, gates, courts, rooms and the temple building itself, ending at the outer wall that separates "the holy and the common" (42:20). But a house is only a building until someone lives in it. Back in chapters 8–11 Ezekiel watched the glory of the LORD leave Solomon's temple in stages, pausing at the threshold, then at the east gate, then on the mountain east of the city (10:4, 18–19; 11:23). Chapter 43 is the climax the whole book has been waiting for: the glory comes back. The rest of the chapter turns from the building to the life that goes on in it, beginning with the altar at its center.

The Glory of the Lord Fills the Temple (vv1–12)

  • "Then he led me to the gate, the gate facing east" (v1). The guide brings Ezekiel back to where the tour began (40:6). This is the gate the glory last used on its way out (10:19). The direction matters: east was where the sun rose, and where the exiles had gone, toward Babylon.
  • "The glory of the God of Israel was coming from the east" (v2). The glory left by the east and returns by the east, as if retracing its steps (11:23). The Tyndale notes contrast the two movements: the departure was slow and reluctant, in stages, while the return is sudden and overwhelming. God goes slowly in judgment and comes quickly in mercy.
  • "The sound of his coming was like the sound of many waters" (v2). This is the same sound Ezekiel heard in the wings of the living creatures in his first vision (1:24). John hears the risen Christ's voice the same way (Rev 1:15). "The earth shone with his glory" recalls Isaiah's seraphim: "the whole earth is full of his glory" (Isa 6:3).
  • "Just like the vision that I had seen when he came to destroy the city" (v3). Ezekiel ties together three visions: the one at the Chebar canal (ch 1), the one when the glory left in judgment (chs 8–11), and this one. Most Hebrew manuscripts read "when I came to destroy the city," meaning when Ezekiel came to announce its destruction (as in 9:1–8). A few manuscripts read "he," and the ESV follows them. Either way the point is the same: the God who returns is the same God who judged. He has not changed. His people have been changed.
  • "And I fell on my face" (v3). Ezekiel's response to the glory is always the same (1:28; 3:23; 44:4). Familiarity never turned into casualness.
  • "The Spirit lifted me up and brought me into the inner court" (v5). The Spirit has carried Ezekiel before, to the exiles (3:12–14), to Jerusalem (8:3), and to the valley of bones (37:1). Now he is set down where he can see. "The glory of the LORD filled the temple." This is what happened when Moses finished the tabernacle (Exod 40:34–35) and when Solomon dedicated the first temple (1 Kings 8:10–11; 2 Chr 7:1–2). Ezekiel's temple receives the same seal of God's approval.
  • No record of a return to the second temple. When the exiles rebuilt the temple under Zerubbabel around 516 BC, Scripture records no cloud of glory filling it (Ezra 6:15–18), and older people wept because it seemed like nothing next to Solomon's (Ezra 3:12; Hag 2:3). Haggai promised that "the latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former" (Hag 2:9). Christians have long read that promise, and this one, as answered when the Lord himself came to his temple in Jesus (Mal 3:1; John 2:19–21).
  • "While the man was standing beside me, I heard one speaking to me out of the temple" (v6). The bronze-like guide (40:3) stands by, silent. The voice now comes from inside the house. This is God himself speaking.
  • "This is the place of my throne and the place of the soles of my feet" (v7). The ark was called God's footstool (1 Chr 28:2; Ps 132:7), and God was said to be enthroned above the cherubim (1 Sam 4:4). The temple was his palace, the earthly point where heaven's throne touched the ground. No ark is mentioned in Ezekiel's temple. Jeremiah foresaw a day when no one would miss the ark, because "Jerusalem shall be called the throne of the LORD" (Jer 3:16–17).
  • "Where I will dwell in the midst of the people of Israel forever" (v7). "Dwell" is shakan, the root behind "tabernacle" (mishkan) and the later Jewish word Shekinah for God's dwelling glory. "Forever" is the new thing. The first temple's glory could leave, and did. This promise echoes the covenant of peace in 37:26–28: "My dwelling place shall be with them."
  • "Their whoring and… the dead bodies of their kings at their high places" (v7). "Whoring" is Ezekiel's word for idolatry, Israel's unfaithfulness to her divine husband (ch 16; ch 23). "Dead bodies" is pegarim. It can mean corpses, and some think of kings buried close to the temple. But Manasseh and Amon were buried in a palace garden, not in the temple (2 Kings 21:18, 26). The word can also mean memorial monuments or offerings to the dead, and the Tyndale notes take it that way: shrines to dead kings set up in the grounds of the living God's house. "At their high places" may also be read "in their death" with a slight change of vowels. The point stands either way: human kings were being honored where only God should be.
  • "Setting their threshold by my threshold… with only a wall between me and them" (v8). Solomon's palace complex stood directly beside the temple on the same hill (1 Kings 7:1–12). The king's house and God's house shared a wall, and the royal chapel blurred into the palace. Later kings brought idols, altars and pagan objects right into the courts (2 Kings 16:10–18; 21:4–5; Ezek 8). In the new plan the prince's land sits away from the sanctuary (45:7–8; 48:21). God will not share a wall with anyone again.
  • "So I have consumed them in my anger. Now let them put away their whoring… and I will dwell in their midst forever" (vv8–9). The sentence turns on "now." Past judgment, present call, future promise. God's dwelling is a gift, but it is not compatible with idols kept on the side.
  • "Describe to the house of Israel the temple, that they may be ashamed of their iniquities" (v10). This verse gives the purpose of the whole vision. Ezekiel is not handed building plans to file away. He is to show the exiles the house God intends, so that the gap between that holy order and their own past would break their hearts. The same shame that comes from receiving undeserved grace appears in 16:61–63 and 36:31–32. Shame here is not despair. It is the right response of people who have been forgiven much.
  • "And if they are ashamed… make known to them the design… and write it down in their sight" (v11). There is a condition: the fuller detail comes to those who respond with repentance. Moses received the tabernacle pattern on the mountain (Exod 25:9, 40). Ezekiel, a priest who never served in the temple, now receives a pattern too, and he writes it down. That is why chapters 40–48 exist.
  • "This is the law of the temple: the whole territory on the top of the mountain all around shall be most holy" (v12). The vision began on "a very high mountain" (40:2). "Most holy," literally "holy of holies," was once used only of the inner room. Now it covers the whole hilltop. The frame "this is the law of the temple" opens and closes the verse, marking it as the summary: the one law of God's house is holiness.
  • Readings differ on chapters 40–48. Many dispensational readers, including Guzik, expect this temple to be built in a future thousand-year reign of Christ, with its sacrifices serving as memorials. Most Reformed and many other interpreters, including Henry, read it as a picture of God's restored people and, finally, of the church and the new creation, where John sees no temple "for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" (Rev 21:22). Some modern scholars, and the Tyndale notes, stress that the vision's first aim was to shape the exiles' hearts, not to start a building project. All agree on what v12 says: God's presence requires holiness.
  • Commentators: Guzik observes, as the Tyndale notes do, that God fills his house readily and leaves it only reluctantly. He passes on Feinberg's remark that the second temple has no record of the glory's return, and Wiersbe's point that people who spend time in holy places should be holy people. Henry, who reads the temple as "the gospel-church," sees God's glory filling it "as it had filled the tabernacle which Moses set up," and sums up v12: "Holiness becomes God's house for ever." His shorter commentary adds that when Christ crucified is shown to us by the Spirit, it makes us "ashamed for our sins." JFB ties "the way of the east" to the glory's exit in 11:22–23 and says v12 extends the superlative once used only of the Holy of Holies to "the entire building." JFB reads the "carcasses" of v9 as dead idols, which had held lordship like kings, a reading neither the Tyndale notes nor most modern scholars follow.

The Altar (vv13–27)

  • "The cubit being a cubit and a handbreadth" (v13). This is the long cubit, the royal measure used throughout the vision (40:5), about 20.5 inches (52 cm). A standard cubit was about 17.5 inches.
  • Built in stages (vv13–17). The altar rises in steps: a base set in a channel with a rim, a lower ledge two cubits high, a larger ledge four cubits high, and the hearth four cubits high with a horn at each corner. The total height was about eleven cubits, roughly 19 feet. The top hearth was 12 cubits square, about 21 feet each way. The Tyndale notes point out that the tabernacle's bronze altar was only five cubits square (Exod 27:1). This altar is nearly three times as long and wide.
  • "Its base" (v13). The Hebrew is literally "the bosom of the earth," and the top hearth (v15) is harel, a word found only in this verse, which can be read "mountain of God." In vv15–16 the text also uses the form ariel, which Isaiah applies to Jerusalem itself (Isa 29:1–2). Many scholars note that a stepped altar running from "the bosom of the earth" to "the mountain of God" looks like a small version of the Babylonian ziggurat, the temple tower, whose names used similar language. If so, the shape Ezekiel saw in Babylon is claimed for the true God. The altar is the place where earth reaches up and God comes down.
  • "Four horns" (v15). Horn-shaped corners were common on Israelite altars. Blood was put on them for atonement (Lev 4:7), and someone fleeing for his life could grab them for refuge (1 Kings 1:50; 2:28).
  • "The steps of the altar shall face east" (v17). The law of Moses forbade steps up to the altar, for modesty's sake (Exod 20:26), so a priest climbing these steps would face west, toward the sanctuary. The Tyndale notes suggest that putting the steps on the east also meant the priest turned his back on the rising sun, the reverse of the sun worshipers Ezekiel saw in 8:16, who stood "with their backs to the temple… worshiping the sun toward the east." By Ezekiel's day priests wore linen undergarments (Exod 28:42–43), which answered the old concern.
  • "These are the ordinances for the altar: On the day when it is erected" (v18). A new altar had to be consecrated before use, as Moses consecrated the tabernacle altar for seven days (Exod 29:36–37; Lev 8:11, 15, 33).
  • "The Levitical priests of the family of Zadok" (v19). Zadok was the priest who stayed loyal to David and anointed Solomon when another priest, Abiathar, backed Adonijah (1 Kings 1:7–8, 32–40; 2:26–27, 35). In Ezekiel only Zadok's line serves at the altar, because they stayed faithful when other Levites led Israel astray (44:10–16; 48:11).
  • "Put it on the four horns… the four corners of the ledge and upon the rim" (v20). The blood marks the altar's outer points, claiming the whole of it. "Purify the altar and make atonement for it." "Purify" is chata', the root of "sin offering," literally "de-sin." It sounds strange to make atonement for an object. The idea is that anything human hands build, in a world full of sin, must be cleansed before a holy God will receive offerings on it (Lev 16:18–19).
  • "Burned in the appointed place… outside the sacred area" (v21). The sin offering's body was burned outside the camp under Moses (Lev 4:11–12, 21). The book of Hebrews draws on that detail: "So Jesus also suffered outside the gate in order to sanctify the people through his own blood" (Heb 13:11–12).
  • A goat on the second day, then seven days (vv22–26). The order is cleansing first (the sin offering), then dedication (the burnt offering, wholly given to God). The priests "sprinkle salt on them" (v24). The law required salt with offerings (Lev 2:13), and "a covenant of salt" meant a permanent, unbreakable covenant (Num 18:19; 2 Chr 13:5). Salt preserves. "Consecrate it" (v26) is literally "fill its hand," the same idiom used for ordaining a priest (Exod 29:9), applied here to the altar.
  • "From the eighth day onward… your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, and I will accept you" (v27). The eighth day marks a new start, as when Aaron's priesthood began on the eighth day and fire came from the LORD (Lev 9:1, 23–24). "Peace offerings" were the shared meals of fellowship with God. And the chapter ends where Ezekiel 20:40–41 pointed: "there I will accept them… as a pleasing aroma I will accept you." The God whose glory filled the house now welcomes his people's worship.
  • Why sacrifices after Christ? This is the hardest question in the vision. Ezekiel wrote six centuries before the cross, in the only worship language his hearers knew. Those who expect a literal future temple, like Guzik, see the sacrifices as memorials looking back to Christ, much as the Lord's Supper does. Others read them as the prophet's way of describing perfect, accepted worship, fulfilled in Christ, who offered "a single sacrifice for sins" for all time (Heb 10:12–14, 18). Both readings agree that no animal blood adds anything to the work of Christ.
  • Commentators: Henry reads the altar christologically, "for Christ is our altar," and on the salt (v24) writes, "Grace is the salt with which all our religious performances must be seasoned." His shorter commentary adds that our best services are accepted only when sprinkled with Christ's blood. Guzik also connects the altar to the cross. He notes the east-facing steps break with Exodus 20:26, which he takes as a sign the old law is in some sense set aside, and he follows Feinberg and Wiersbe that these future sacrifices would be memorials, not a second atonement. JFB is unusually frank: the sacrifices in the text sound "not mere commemorative, but propitiatory," yet because Hebrews 10 makes Christ's offering final, they can only be retrospective. JFB explains harel as "mount of God," a high place of God rather than of idols, links the burning outside the sanctuary to Hebrews 13:11, and points v27 to Romans 12:1 and 1 Peter 2:5, the living and spiritual sacrifices of believers.

Pointing to Christ

The glory that left in judgment comes back, never to leave, and the house is made holy around an altar cleansed by blood. John chose Ezekiel's word when he wrote that "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory" (John 1:14), and Jesus called his own body the temple (John 2:21). He is the sin offering burned "outside the gate" (Heb 13:12), and through him God says to people who once defiled his name, "I will accept you." The vision's call to be ashamed of sin is not meant to crush. It is the shame of people who see how holy the house is and find they have been welcomed into it.

Reflection

  • The Glory Returns From the East (vv.2-5) — After departing through the east gate in an earlier vision, the glory of the LORD comes back the same way and fills the temple completely. If you have walked through a season when God's presence felt distant, what would it look like to watch for His return rather than assume He is gone for good?
  • An Eternal Throne, Not a Passing Visit (v.7) — God declares this is the place of His throne where He will dwell among His people forever, no longer coming and going. Do you relate to God as a permanent King reigning in your life, or as an occasional guest you welcome only when convenient?
  • Holiness and Presence Held Together (v.9) — God ties His continued dwelling to His people putting away what defiled Him, showing that nearness to Him and holiness are not separate pursuits. Where might you be hoping for more of God's presence while holding onto something He's asked you to let go of?
  • Consecration Before Service (vv.18-27) — Before the altar could be used, it required seven full days of atonement and purification — nothing was rushed. What in your life might need a season of preparation before God, rather than jumping straight into serving Him?
