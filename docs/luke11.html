1One day in a place where Jesus had just finished praying, one of His disciples requested, “Lord, teach us to pray, just as John taught his disciples.”

2So Jesus told them, “When you pray, say: ‘Father, hallowed be Your name. Your kingdom come.

3Give us each day our daily bread.

4And forgive us our sins, for we also forgive everyone who sins against us. And lead us not into temptation.’”

5Then Jesus said to them, “Suppose one of you goes to his friend at midnight and says, ‘Friend, lend me three loaves of bread,

6because a friend of mine has come to me on a journey, and I have nothing to set before him.’

7And suppose the one inside answers, ‘Do not bother me. My door is already shut, and my children and I are in bed. I cannot get up to give you anything.’

8I tell you, even though he will not get up to provide for him because of his friendship, yet because of the man’s persistence, he will get up and give him as much as he needs.

9So I tell you: Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened to you.

10For everyone who asks receives; he who seeks finds; and to him who knocks, the door will be opened.

11What father among you, if his son asks for a fish, will give him a snake instead?

12Or if he asks for an egg, will give him a scorpion?

13So if you who are evil know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!”

14One day Jesus was driving out a demon that was mute. And when the demon was gone, the man who had been mute spoke. The crowds were amazed,

15but some of them said, “It is by Beelzebul, the prince of the demons, that He drives out demons.”

16And others tested Him by demanding a sign from heaven.

17Knowing their thoughts, Jesus said to them, “Every kingdom divided against itself will be laid waste, and a house divided against a house will fall.

18If Satan is divided against himself, how can his kingdom stand? After all, you say that I drive out demons by Beelzebul.

19And if I drive out demons by Beelzebul, by whom do your sons drive them out? So then, they will be your judges.

20But if I drive out demons by the finger of God, then the kingdom of God has come upon you.

21When a strong man, fully armed, guards his house, his possessions are secure.

22But when someone stronger attacks and overpowers him, he takes away the armor in which the man trusted, and then he divides up his plunder.

23He who is not with Me is against Me, and he who does not gather with Me scatters.

24When an unclean spirit comes out of a man, it passes through arid places seeking rest and does not find it. Then it says, ‘I will return to the house I left.’

25On its return, it finds the house swept clean and put in order.

26Then it goes and brings seven other spirits more wicked than itself, and they go in and dwell there. And the final plight of that man is worse than the first.”

27As Jesus was saying these things, a woman in the crowd raised her voice and said, “Blessed is the womb that bore You, and blessed are the breasts that nursed You!”

28But He replied, “Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and obey it.”

29As the crowds were increasing, Jesus said, “This is a wicked generation. It demands a sign, but none will be given it except the sign of Jonah.

30For as Jonah was a sign to the Ninevites, so the Son of Man will be a sign to this generation.

31The Queen of the South will rise at the judgment with the men of this generation and condemn them; for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and now One greater than Solomon is here.

32The men of Nineveh will stand at the judgment with this generation and condemn it; for they repented at the preaching of Jonah, and now One greater than Jonah is here.

33No one lights a lamp and puts it in a cellar or under a basket. Instead, he sets it on a stand, so those who enter can see the light.

34Your eye is the lamp of your body. When your eyes are good, your whole body also is full of light. But when they are bad, your body is full of darkness.

35Be careful, then, that the light within you is not darkness.

36So if your whole body is full of light, with no part of it in darkness, you will be radiant, as though a lamp were shining on you.”

37As Jesus was speaking, a Pharisee invited Him to dine with him; so He went in and reclined at the table.

38But the Pharisee was surprised to see that Jesus did not first wash before the meal.

39Then the Lord said, “Now you Pharisees clean the outside of the cup and dish, but inside you are full of greed and wickedness.

40You fools! Did not the One who made the outside make the inside as well?

41But give as alms the things that are within you, and behold, everything will be clean for you.

42Woe to you Pharisees! For you pay tithes of mint, rue, and every herb, but you disregard justice and the love of God. You should have practiced the latter without neglecting the former.

43Woe to you Pharisees! For you love the chief seats in the synagogues and the greetings in the marketplaces.

44Woe to you! For you are like unmarked graves, which men walk over without even noticing.”

45One of the experts in the law told Him, “Teacher, when You say these things, You insult us as well.”

46“Woe to you as well, experts in the law!” He replied. “For you weigh men down with heavy burdens, but you yourselves will not lift a finger to lighten their load.

47Woe to you! For you build tombs for the prophets, but it was your fathers who killed them.

48So you are witnesses consenting to the deeds of your fathers: They killed the prophets, and you build their tombs.

49Because of this, the wisdom of God said, ‘I will send them prophets and apostles; some of them they will kill and others they will persecute.’

50As a result, this generation will be charged with the blood of all the prophets that has been shed since the foundation of the world,

51from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah, who was killed between the altar and the sanctuary. Yes, I tell you, all of it will be charged to this generation.

52Woe to you experts in the law! For you have taken away the key to knowledge. You yourselves have not entered, and you have hindered those who were entering.”

53As Jesus went on from there, the scribes and Pharisees began to oppose Him bitterly and to ply Him with questions about many things,

54waiting to catch Him in something He might say.

1And it came to pass, that, as he was praying in a certain place, when he ceased, one of his disciples said unto him, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples.

2And he said unto them, When ye pray, say, Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done, as in heaven, so in earth.

3Give us day by day our daily bread.

4And forgive us our sins; for we also forgive every one that is indebted to us. And lead us not into temptation; but deliver us from evil.

5And he said unto them, Which of you shall have a friend, and shall go unto him at midnight, and say unto him, Friend, lend me three loaves;

6For a friend of mine in his journey is come to me, and I have nothing to set before him?

7And he from within shall answer and say, Trouble me not: the door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give thee.

8I say unto you, Though he will not rise and give him, because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth.

9And I say unto you, Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you.

10For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened.

11If a son shall ask bread of any of you that is a father, will he give him a stone? or if he ask a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent?

12Or if he shall ask an egg, will he offer him a scorpion?

13If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?

14And he was casting out a devil, and it was dumb. And it came to pass, when the devil was gone out, the dumb spake; and the people wondered.

15But some of them said, He casteth out devils through Beelzebub the chief of the devils.

16And others, tempting him, sought of him a sign from heaven.

17But he, knowing their thoughts, said unto them, Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation; and a house divided against a house falleth.

18If Satan also be divided against himself, how shall his kingdom stand? because ye say that I cast out devils through Beelzebub.

19And if I by Beelzebub cast out devils, by whom do your sons cast them out? therefore shall they be your judges.

20But if I with the finger of God cast out devils, no doubt the kingdom of God is come upon you.

21When a strong man armed keepeth his palace, his goods are in peace:

22But when a stronger than he shall come upon him, and overcome him, he taketh from him all his armour wherein he trusted, and divideth his spoils.

23He that is not with me is against me: and he that gathereth not with me scattereth.

24When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest; and finding none, he saith, I will return unto my house whence I came out.

25And when he cometh, he findeth it swept and garnished.

26Then goeth he, and taketh to him seven other spirits more wicked than himself; and they enter in, and dwell there: and the last state of that man is worse than the first.

27And it came to pass, as he spake these things, a certain woman of the company lifted up her voice, and said unto him, Blessed is the womb that bare thee, and the paps which thou hast sucked.

28But he said, Yea rather, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it.

29And when the people were gathered thick together, he began to say, This is an evil generation: they seek a sign; and there shall no sign be given it, but the sign of Jonas the prophet.

30For as Jonas was a sign unto the Ninevites, so shall also the Son of man be to this generation.

31The queen of the south shall rise up in the judgment with the men of this generation, and condemn them: for she came from the utmost parts of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here.

32The men of Nineve shall rise up in the judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: for they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.

33No man, when he hath lighted a candle, putteth it in a secret place, neither under a bushel, but on a candlestick, that they which come in may see the light.

34The light of the body is the eye: therefore when thine eye is single, thy whole body also is full of light; but when thine eye is evil, thy body also is full of darkness.

35Take heed therefore that the light which is in thee be not darkness.

36If thy whole body therefore be full of light, having no part dark, the whole shall be full of light, as when the bright shining of a candle doth give thee light.

37And as he spake, a certain Pharisee besought him to dine with him: and he went in, and sat down to meat.

38And when the Pharisee saw it, he marvelled that he had not first washed before dinner.

39And the Lord said unto him, Now do ye Pharisees make clean the outside of the cup and the platter; but your inward part is full of ravening and wickedness.

40Ye fools, did not he that made that which is without make that which is within also?

41But rather give alms of such things as ye have; and, behold, all things are clean unto you.

42But woe unto you, Pharisees! for ye tithe mint and rue and all manner of herbs, and pass over judgment and the love of God: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.

43Woe unto you, Pharisees! for ye love the uppermost seats in the synagogues, and greetings in the markets.

44Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye are as graves which appear not, and the men that walk over them are not aware of them.

45Then answered one of the lawyers, and said unto him, Master, thus saying thou reproachest us also.

46And he said, Woe unto you also, ye lawyers! for ye lade men with burdens grievous to be borne, and ye yourselves touch not the burdens with one of your fingers.

47Woe unto you! for ye build the sepulchres of the prophets, and your fathers killed them.

48Truly ye bear witness that ye allow the deeds of your fathers: for they indeed killed them, and ye build their sepulchres.

49Therefore also said the wisdom of God, I will send them prophets and apostles, and some of them they shall slay and persecute:

50That the blood of all the prophets, which was shed from the foundation of the world, may be required of this generation;

51From the blood of Abel unto the blood of Zacharias, which perished between the altar and the temple: verily I say unto you, It shall be required of this generation.

52Woe unto you, lawyers! for ye have taken away the key of knowledge: ye entered not in yourselves, and them that were entering in ye hindered.

53And as he said these things unto them, the scribes and the Pharisees began to urge him vehemently, and to provoke him to speak of many things:

54Laying wait for him, and seeking to catch something out of his mouth, that they might accuse him.

Summary

Jesus teaches the disciples the Lord's Prayer, then tells the parable of a persistent friend at midnight to show that God delights to answer bold, persistent prayer and gives the Holy Spirit to those who ask (vv. 1-13). After He casts a mute demon out of a man, critics claim He works by the devil's power; Jesus counters that a divided kingdom cannot stand, insists He casts out demons by God's power, and warns that a swept but empty house invites worse spirits back in (vv. 14-26). When the crowd demands a sign, He points to Jonah and declares that someone greater than Jonah and Solomon is here, then calls people to keep their spiritual sight clear (vv. 27-36). Dining with a Pharisee, He denounces the Pharisees and lawyers as hypocrites who polish the outside while neglecting justice and the love of God, provoking their fury (vv. 37-54).

Authorship & Background

Author: Luke (see Chapter 1 notes for full authorship details).
Historical Context: Chapter 11 contains Jesus' teaching on prayer (vv.1-13), His response to the accusation of casting out demons by Beelzebub (vv.14-26), and His pronouncement of woes against the Pharisees and lawyers (vv.37-54).
Classification: Gospel — Narrative and Discourse
Key Themes: a disciple's request prompted simply by watching Jesus pray, Lord, teach us to pray, a shorter version of the same model prayer Matthew records, a persistent friend parable arguing not that God is reluctant but that shameless boldness in prayer is rewarded, three imperatives and three promises ending, in Luke's version, not on good things but specifically on the Holy Spirit, an accusation of casting out demons by Satan's own power demolished by pointing out a divided kingdom cannot stand, two Gentile examples, Nineveh and the queen of the south, offered as proof that less evidence produced more response, and a dinner invitation that turns into six woes, three on Pharisees and three on lawyers, the harshest passage in the whole Gospel
The Lord's Prayer (vv.1-4): Luke's version is shorter than Matthew's (Matthew 6:9-13). A disciple asked "Lord, teach us to pray" — prompted by watching Jesus pray. The prayer Jesus gives is a model — not a formula to recite but a pattern to follow: address God as Father, honor His name, seek His kingdom, ask for daily provision, seek forgiveness, and ask for protection from temptation.
The Persistent Friend (vv.5-8): A parable about prayer — not that God is reluctant like the sleeping friend, but that persistence in prayer is appropriate and rewarded. The Greek word "anaideia" (v.8) means shameless persistence or boldness. God invites bold, persistent asking.
Ask, and It Shall Be Given (vv.9-13): Three imperatives with three promises, ask and it shall be given you, seek and ye shall find, knock and it shall be opened. Then the argument from ordinary fatherhood, and Luke's version of the examples is bread and a stone, a fish and a serpent, an egg and a scorpion. The conclusion concedes the premise about human character in passing, if ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your heavenly Father give. And Luke's ending differs from Matthew's in what is promised: not good things, but the Holy Spirit.
The Beelzebub Controversy (vv.14-26): Jesus' enemies accused Him of casting out demons by Satan's power. Jesus demolishes this logic: a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand. He then introduces the "stronger man" — Himself — who overcomes the "strong man" (Satan) and plunders his house.
The Sign of Jonah (vv.27-36): A woman in the crowd calls out a blessing on his mother, blessed is the womb that bare thee, and the reply redirects it rather than refusing it, yea rather, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it. Then the demand for a sign is answered with two: Jonah to the Ninevites, and the queen of the south who came from the uttermost parts of the earth to hear Solomon. Both examples are Gentiles who responded to less evidence. The section ends on the single eye and the candle, take heed therefore that the light which is in thee be not darkness, which is about the capacity to see rather than the availability of light.
Woe Unto You, Pharisees (vv.37-54): A dinner invitation turns into the harshest passage in the Gospel. The complaint is that he did not wash first, and the answer moves from cups to people, ye make clean the outside, but your inward part is full of ravening. Then three woes on the Pharisees and three on the lawyers. Tithing mint and rue while passing over judgment and the love of God. Loving the uppermost seats. Being as graves which appear not, so that men walk over them unaware. Laying burdens on others and not touching them. Building the tombs of the prophets their fathers killed. And the last, which is the one that closes the passage, ye have taken away the key of knowledge, ye entered not in yourselves, and them that were entering in ye hindered. Luke records what it produced, they began to urge him vehemently, laying wait for him.

Setting: About AD 29, somewhere on the long journey to Jerusalem that began in 9:51. Chapter 10 ended with Mary at Jesus' feet, listening, the "one thing" necessary. Chapter 11 opens with Jesus praying and his disciples asking him to teach them how. The first part of the chapter is about prayer, a Father who gives good gifts and the Holy Spirit. Then the mood darkens. Some accuse Jesus of driving out demons by Satan's power, others demand a sign, and at a Pharisee's dinner Jesus pronounces six woes on Pharisees and lawyers. The chapter ends with his opponents "lying in wait for him."

The Lord's Prayer (vv1–4)

  • "Jesus was praying in a certain place" (v1). Luke keeps showing Jesus at prayer (3:21; 5:16; 6:12; 9:18, 28). The disciples ask after watching him. Seeing how Jesus prayed made them want to pray.
  • "As John taught his disciples" (v1). Jewish teachers and movements often had their own characteristic prayers, a mark of the group's identity. The disciples want a prayer that belongs to Jesus' followers.
  • Luke's shorter form. Luke's version is shorter than Matthew's (Matt 6:9–13), which Jesus gave in the Sermon on the Mount. Jesus probably taught it more than once, in slightly different words, as a pattern and not only a formula. Some later manuscripts of Luke add lines from Matthew ("Our Father in heaven," "your will be done," "deliver us from evil"), and the KJV includes them. The earliest manuscripts have the shorter form.
  • "Father" (v2). Jesus almost certainly said the Aramaic Abba, the word a child used for a father, warm but respectful (Mark 14:36). Jews did call God Father (Isa 63:16), but addressing him so directly in prayer was unusual. Paul says the Spirit puts this same word on believers' lips (Rom 8:15; Gal 4:6). Jesus gives his disciples a share in his own way of speaking to God.
  • "Hallowed be your name" (v2). "Hallowed" means treated as holy. God's "name" is his revealed character. The first request is not for anything the one praying needs. It is that God be honored as he truly is (Ezek 36:23).
  • "Your kingdom come" (v2). A prayer for God's rule to arrive in full. It echoes the Jewish Kaddish prayer, which asked that God "establish his kingdom in your lifetime."
  • "Give us each day our daily bread" (v3). The rare Greek word epiousios, translated "daily," appears almost nowhere outside this prayer. It may mean "needed for today" or "for the coming day." Either way it is a prayer of dependence, like Israel gathering manna one day at a time (Exod 16:4).
  • "Forgive us our sins, for we ourselves forgive everyone who is indebted to us" (v4). Luke writes "sins" in the first line and "indebted" in the second. Aramaic used the same word for both, and sin was pictured as a debt owed to God. Forgiving others is not the price of God's forgiveness. It is the mark of someone who has received it (7:47; Matt 18:21–35).
  • "Lead us not into temptation" (v4). God tempts no one to sin (James 1:13). The word peirasmos means testing or trial. The request asks God to keep his people from trials that would overwhelm them, and to keep them from falling. Jesus tells the disciples to pray it in Gethsemane (22:40, 46).
  • Commentators: Henry notes that although Christ was ready to teach, he waited to be asked: "though Christ is apt to teach, yet he will for this be enquired of." JFB reads "day by day" as extending the request to the needs of each day as it comes. Guzik stresses that the prayer's first concerns are God's name and kingdom, that daily bread means present provision rather than a stockpile, and that the differences from Matthew show it is a pattern, not a ritual to recite mechanically.

Ask, and It Will Be Given (vv5–13)

  • "Which of you who has a friend will go to him at midnight" (v5). Travelers often moved in the cool of evening. Village hospitality was a matter of honor, not just for the host but for the whole village. To have nothing to set before a guest was shameful. Bread was baked daily, so by midnight a household might have none left.
  • "The door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed" (v7). A peasant family often slept together on a mat in a single room. Getting up meant unbarring the door and waking everyone. Jesus' hearers would laugh: no neighbor would actually refuse.
  • "Because of his impudence he will rise" (v8). The word is anaideia, "shamelessness." Many readers take it as the persistence of the man knocking. Others think it describes the sleeper, who gets up to avoid the shame of having been a bad neighbor. Either way, the story argues from the lesser to the greater. If a grumpy neighbor will get up, how much more will God, who is not grumpy and not asleep (Ps 121:4).
  • "Ask… seek… knock" (v9). The Greek verbs are present tense: keep asking, keep seeking, keep knocking. Prayer is meant to be ongoing, not a one-time attempt.
  • "A serpent… a scorpion" (vv11–12). Some fish from the lake were snake-like, and a curled-up pale scorpion might look like an egg. No father would hand his son something deadly in place of food.
  • "If you then, who are evil… how much more will the heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit" (v13). Jesus assumes human sinfulness even in good fathers. Where Matthew 7:11 says "good things," Luke says "the Holy Spirit," the best gift of all. Luke highlights the Spirit throughout his two volumes, and in Acts the Spirit comes to those praying (Acts 1:14; 2:1–4; 4:31).
  • Commentators: JFB notes midnight was "a most unseasonable hour of call," chosen for that reason, and reads anaideia as a shameless persistence that will not take no. It calls the Spirit the "Gift of gifts." Guzik argues that persistence works more on the one praying than on a reluctant God, and stresses that God's willingness to give the Spirit is never in doubt. The Tyndale notes give the alternative reading that the sleeper acts to avoid shame. Henry calls prayer "one of the great laws of natural religion."

Jesus and Beelzebul (vv14–23)

  • "A demon that was mute" (v14). Matthew 12:22 says the man was also blind. Luke keeps the miracle short and turns to the reactions.
  • "He casts out demons by Beelzebul, the prince of demons" (v15). No one denies the miracle. They argue about its source. "Beelzebul" probably meant "Baal the prince" or "lord of the house," from a Canaanite god. In 2 Kings 1:2 the god of Ekron is Baal-zebub, "lord of flies," likely a Jewish mocking twist of the name. By Jesus' day it was a name for Satan.
  • "Others, to test him, kept seeking from him a sign from heaven" (v16). This is the second challenge, and Jesus answers it in vv29–32.
  • "Every kingdom divided against itself is laid waste" (v17). Jesus answers with common sense. Satan does not tear down his own work. Civil wars destroy kingdoms, as Israel's own history showed.
  • "By whom do your sons cast them out?" (v19). "Your sons" means their own followers. Jewish exorcists existed (Acts 19:13). If the critics accept their exorcisms as from God, they condemn themselves by calling Jesus' exorcisms satanic.
  • "By the finger of God" (v20). Pharaoh's magicians, unable to copy the third plague, said: "This is the finger of God" (Exod 8:19). Matthew 12:28 says "by the Spirit of God." Luke's phrase recalls the exodus. A new deliverance is under way.
  • "Then the kingdom of God has come upon you" (v20). The verb means it has already arrived. The exorcisms show God's rule breaking in now.
  • "When a strong man, fully armed, guards his own palace" (vv21–22). Satan is the strong man, people held in bondage are his goods, and Jesus is the "stronger" one who overpowers him, strips his armor and divides the spoil. Isaiah asked, "Can the prey be taken from the mighty?" and God answered, "Even the captives of the mighty shall be taken" (Isa 49:24–25). Isaiah 53:12 says the servant "shall divide the spoil with the strong."
  • "Whoever is not with me is against me" (v23). Compare 9:50, "the one who is not against you is for you." There Jesus warned against suspicion of others working in his name. Here he warns that no one can be neutral toward him. "Scatters" pictures a shepherd's flock or a harvest gathered or lost.
  • Commentators: JFB distinguishes "the finger of God," God's power, from "the Spirit of God" in Matthew, the personal agent. It identifies the strong man as Satan and "his palace" as man, and on v23 says "neutrality in religion there is none." Henry: "Obstinate infidelity will never be at a loss for something to say in its own excuse." Guzik reads the strong man as Christ's victory over Satan and stresses that there is no neutral ground.

Return of an Unclean Spirit (vv24–26)

  • "Passes through waterless places seeking rest" (v24). The desert was pictured as the haunt of evil spirits (Isa 13:21; 34:14). The spirit is restless outside a host.
  • "Finds the house swept and put in order" (v25). Matthew adds "empty" (Matt 12:44). The house is clean but no one lives there. Reform without a new occupant leaves a person open.
  • "Seven other spirits more evil than itself" (v26). Seven suggests completeness. Getting rid of evil is not enough. The heart must be filled by God's rule, with Jesus as master of the house. In context this warns the crowds who have seen demons driven out but remain neutral toward Jesus.
  • Commentators: Henry: "Hypocrisy is the high road to apostasy." JFB treats this as the reverse of the strong man picture: there the stronger man takes the house, here the old tenant returns. Guzik draws the point that a cleansed heart left empty invites worse evil, so it must be filled with Christ.

True Blessedness (vv27–28)

  • "Blessed is the womb that bore you" (v27). Only Luke records this. It was a common way of honoring a great man, by praising his mother. Elizabeth said something similar to Mary (1:42), and Mary herself sang that "all generations will call me blessed" (1:48).
  • "Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and keep it!" (v28). "Rather" can mean "yes, but more than that." Jesus does not deny his mother's blessedness. He points to what made Mary blessed in the first place, that she heard and believed (1:38, 45), and he opens that blessing to everyone. This echoes 8:21: "My mother and my brothers are those who hear the word of God and do it."
  • Commentators: JFB contrasts Jesus' answer with later devotion that gives Mary a special place in heaven. Protestants commonly read it that way, while Catholic interpreters note that Mary is the model hearer and keeper of the word, so the verse honors her in the right way. Henry quotes the verse as its own lesson. Guzik says Jesus honors hearing and keeping God's word above physical kinship, without belittling his mother.

The Sign of Jonah (vv29–32)

  • "This generation is an evil generation. It seeks for a sign" (v29). They have seen healings and exorcisms. Demanding more is not openness but unbelief (1 Cor 1:22).
  • "The sign of Jonah" (v30). Jonah "became a sign to the people of Nineveh." He came out of the fish, as if back from death, and preached repentance. Matthew 12:40 spells out the three days and nights in the fish as a picture of Jesus' burial and resurrection. Luke stresses the preaching. Both meanings fit: Jesus' resurrection will be the sign, and his message of repentance is the call.
  • "The queen of the South" (v31). She is the queen of Sheba, probably in southwest Arabia, modern Yemen. She traveled about 1,200 miles to hear Solomon's wisdom (1 Kings 10:1–10). "The ends of the earth" was how the far south seemed to Israel.
  • "Something greater than Solomon is here… something greater than Jonah is here" (vv31–32). "Something" is neuter in Greek, possibly pointing to the kingdom present in Jesus. Solomon was the wisest king, and Jonah the prophet who turned a pagan city. Jesus is greater than both. On judgment day, a Gentile queen and Gentile Ninevites will testify against Israel's own people, who had far more and refused.
  • Commentators: Guzik reads the sign of Jonah as a picture of the resurrection and stresses the phrase greater than. The Tyndale notes give both readings, the resurrection and Jonah's preaching. JFB refers only to Matthew.

The Light in You (vv33–36)

  • "No one after lighting a lamp puts it in a cellar" (v33). The lamp is not hidden. God's light in Jesus is shining openly. The problem is not lack of light.
  • "Your eye is the lamp of your body" (v34). In ancient thought the eye let light into the body, like a window. "Healthy" (haplous) means single, sound or sincere. "Bad" (ponēros) means diseased or evil. Hebrew idiom used a "bad eye" for stinginess or envy (Prov 28:22). A heart focused on God takes in light. A divided or envious heart stays dark however bright the lamp.
  • "Be careful lest the light in you be darkness" (v35). The sharpest warning in the section. The crowds think they see. The religious leaders think they are full of light. They may be blind.
  • Commentators: JFB says v36 shows "the clarity of the inward eye," a soul fully lit. Guzik argues that inner blindness, not lack of evidence, keeps people from seeing Jesus. Henry likewise makes a sincere, clear-sighted heart the condition of receiving the light.

Woes to the Pharisees and Lawyers (vv37–54)

  • "A Pharisee asked him to dine with him" (v37). This is Luke's second Pharisee dinner (7:36; 14:1). The word for dinner here means a midday meal.
  • "He did not first wash before dinner" (v38). This was not hygiene but ritual. Pharisaic tradition required pouring water over the hands before eating, to remove ceremonial impurity (Mark 7:3–4). The law of Moses required it only of priests in certain situations.
  • "You cleanse the outside of the cup… but inside you are full of greed and wickedness" (v39). The rules for cleaning vessels were detailed. Jesus turns the picture on them: they are the cup, clean outside and filthy inside.
  • "Did not he who made the outside make the inside also?" (v40). God made the whole person and cares about the heart (1 Sam 16:7).
  • "Give as alms those things that are within" (v41). A hard phrase. It probably means: give from the heart, give of what is inside the cup, generously, to the poor. Inner generosity cleanses in a way that washing cannot.
  • "You tithe mint and rue and every herb" (v42). The law required a tenth of grain, wine, oil and produce (Deut 14:22–23; Lev 27:30). The Pharisees tithed even tiny garden herbs. Rue was a bitter herb, and later rabbis said it did not need tithing, so these men went beyond even their own rules. Matthew lists "justice and mercy and faithfulness." Luke has "justice and the love of God," the two halves of the great commandment again (10:27). "Without neglecting the others" shows that Jesus is not against tithing.
  • "The best seat in the synagogues and greetings in the marketplaces" (v43). The front seats faced the congregation. Public greetings with honorific titles were expected for scholars.
  • "Like unmarked graves" (v44). Touching a grave made a person unclean for seven days (Num 19:16). Before Passover, Jews whitewashed tombs so pilgrims would not touch them by accident (Matthew 23:27 draws on this). An unmarked grave defiled people who never knew it. The Pharisees thought they made others holy. Jesus says they spread defilement without anyone noticing.
  • "Teacher, in saying these things you insult us also" (v45). The "lawyers" were the scholars who interpreted the law, many of them Pharisees. They expected Jesus to apologize. He turns to them instead.
  • "You load people with burdens hard to bear" (v46). The oral law multiplied rules for daily life. Jesus says these scholars laid heavy demands on others and would not lift a finger to help carry them. Contrast Jesus' "my burden is light" (Matt 11:30).
  • "You build the tombs of the prophets whom your fathers killed" (vv47–48). Monuments to prophets were being built in Jesus' day. Honoring dead prophets while rejecting the living ones shows they are like their fathers, who killed the prophets while they were alive.
  • "The Wisdom of God said" (v49). The quotation is not found in the Old Testament. It may be God's wisdom speaking through the pattern of Scripture as a whole, or Jesus speaking as God's Wisdom himself. In Matthew 23:34 Jesus says "I send you prophets." "Apostles" points ahead to the church in Acts.
  • "From the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah" (v51). Abel is the first murder in the Bible (Gen 4:8). Zechariah son of Jehoiada was stoned "in the court of the house of the LORD," saying "May the LORD see and avenge!" (2 Chr 24:20–22). Chronicles is the last book in the Hebrew Bible, so these are the first and last murders in its order, A to Z. "Between the altar and the sanctuary" matches the temple courtyard.
  • "It will be required of this generation" (v51). Jesus' generation would reject the greatest Prophet of all. Many see a fulfillment in the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70 (19:41–44; 21:20–24).
  • "You have taken away the key of knowledge" (v52). The key that opens the knowledge of God is Scripture rightly understood, pointing to Christ (24:27). The lawyers had locked it up with tradition, and stood in the doorway blocking others.
  • "Lying in wait for him, to catch him in something he might say" (v54). The picture is of hunters setting a trap. From here, opposition grows, and the road leads to the cross.
  • Commentators: JFB says the woes rest on the leaders' "heartless rigor" and inconsistency, notes that Luke has "the love of God" where Matthew has "mercy, and faith," and on v49 asks, "Could this be other than the Lord of Israel in the flesh?" It takes Zechariah as the one in 2 Chronicles 24, whose dying words were a call for the Lord to require his blood. Henry: "It is gross hypocrisy to condemn that in those who reprove us which yet we allow in those that flatter us." Guzik explains the handwashing ritual, notes that the word hypocrite meant an actor, and calls taking away the key of knowledge the worst offense, because it shut others out.

Pointing to Christ

Luke 11 shows the Son who prays and invites his disciples into his own way of speaking to God, "Father." He promises a Father who gives the Holy Spirit to those who ask. He is the stronger man who breaks into Satan's house and sets the captives free by the finger of God. He is greater than Solomon and greater than Jonah, and the sign he gives is his own death and resurrection. He is also the true light, and his woes expose religion that cleans the outside and leaves the heart dark. The key of knowledge that the lawyers took away is found in him, and the burden they would not lift, he carried to the cross.

Reflection

  • Learning to Pray by Watching (v.1) — The disciples didn't ask Jesus to teach them to preach or heal, but to pray, after watching Him do it. Would your own prayer life prompt someone else to want to learn from it?
  • Ask, Seek, Knock (v.9) — Jesus describes prayer with escalating intensity, not a single casual request. Are you asking casually about your current needs, or seeking and knocking with real persistence?
  • Asking for God Himself (v.13) — Jesus points to the Holy Spirit as the best gift the Father gives to those who ask. Do your prayers mostly ask God for things, or do they ask for more of God?
  • Swept Clean but Left Empty (vv.24-26) — Jesus warns that moral cleanup without being filled by God's Spirit leaves a person vulnerable to something worse. Are you trying to reform your behavior without inviting God to actually fill the space?
  • Hearing and Keeping (v.28) — Jesus blesses not merely hearing God's word but keeping it. What specific thing has God recently said to you that you still haven't acted on?
  • Clean Outside, Dirty Inside (v.39) — Jesus condemns the Pharisees for polishing their external religion while neglecting the state of their hearts. How clean is the inside of your cup — your private thoughts and motives — compared to what others see?
