1Now Jacob looked up and saw Esau coming toward him with four hundred men. So he divided the children among Leah, Rachel, and the two maidservants.

2He put the maidservants and their children in front, Leah and her children next, and Rachel and Joseph at the rear.

3But Jacob himself went on ahead and bowed to the ground seven times as he approached his brother.

4Esau, however, ran to him and embraced him, threw his arms around his neck, and kissed him. And they both wept.

5When Esau looked up and saw the women and children, he asked, “Who are these with you?” Jacob answered, “These are the children God has graciously given your servant.”

6Then the maidservants and their children approached and bowed down.

7Leah and her children also approached and bowed down, and then Joseph and Rachel approached and bowed down.

8“What do you mean by sending this whole company to meet me?” asked Esau. “To find favor in your sight, my lord,” Jacob answered.

9“I already have plenty, my brother,” Esau replied. “Keep what belongs to you.”

10But Jacob insisted, “No, please! If I have found favor in your sight, then receive this gift from my hand. For indeed, I have seen your face, and it is like seeing the face of God, since you have received me favorably.

11Please accept my gift that was brought to you, because God has been gracious to me and I have all I need.” So Jacob pressed him until he accepted.

12Then Esau said, “Let us be on our way, and I will go ahead of you.”

13But Jacob replied, “My lord knows that the children are frail, and I must care for sheep and cattle that are nursing their young. If they are driven hard for even a day, all the animals will die.

14Please let my lord go ahead of his servant. I will continue on slowly, at a comfortable pace for the livestock and children, until I come to my lord at Seir.”

15“Let me leave some of my people with you,” Esau said. But Jacob replied, “Why do that? Let me find favor in the sight of my lord.”

16So that day Esau started on his way back to Seir,

17but Jacob went on to Succoth, where he built a house for himself and shelters for his livestock; that is why the place was called Succoth.

18After Jacob had come from Paddan-aram, he arrived safely at the city of Shechem in the land of Canaan, and he camped just outside the city.

19And the plot of ground where he pitched his tent, he purchased from the sons of Hamor, Shechem’s father, for a hundred pieces of silver.

20There he set up an altar and called it El-Elohe-Israel.

Summary

As Esau approaches with four hundred men, Jacob arranges his family with the maidservants and their children first, then Leah, and Rachel and Joseph last, and bows to the ground seven times as he draws near his brother (vv. 1-3). Esau runs to meet him, embraces and kisses him, and both men weep, putting their old conflict behind them (v. 4). After introducing his family, Jacob presses Esau to accept his gifts of livestock, and Esau eventually agrees (vv. 5-11). Esau offers to accompany or leave men with Jacob, but Jacob politely declines, and the two part ways, Esau returning to Seir while Jacob travels on (vv. 12-16). Jacob eventually settles near the city of Shechem in Canaan, purchases land there, and builds an altar which he names El-Elohe-Israel (vv. 17-20).

Authorship & Background

Author: Moses (see Chapter 1 notes for full authorship details).
Historical Context: Chapter 33 records the long-feared reunion of Jacob and Esau — and it goes far better than expected. After 20 years of separation, Esau runs to embrace his brother. The reconciliation is genuine and gracious. Jacob, now Israel (transformed by the Peniel encounter), approaches with humility. The chapter ends with Jacob settling near Shechem and building an altar — "El-elohe-Israel" (God, the God of Israel).
The Reunion (vv.1-4): Jacob sees Esau with 400 men (v.1). He arranges his family — handmaids first, Leah next, Rachel and Joseph last (vv.1-2). Jacob goes ahead, bowing seven times (v.3). "Esau ran to meet him, and embraced him, and fell on his neck, and kissed him: and they wept" (v.4). Grace where judgment was expected.
The Introduction (vv.5-7): Esau meets Jacob's family. Jacob: "The children which God hath graciously given thy servant" (v.5). Each group bows before Esau.
The Gift Accepted (vv.8-11): Esau: "I have enough" (v.9). Jacob insists: "I have seen thy face, as though I had seen the face of God" (v.10). "God hath dealt graciously with me, and... I have enough" (v.11). Both brothers say "I have enough" — contentment on both sides.
The Parting (vv.12-17): Esau offers to travel together (v.12). Jacob declines — needs to go slowly for children and flocks (vv.13-14). They part peacefully. Jacob goes to Succoth, then Shechem (vv.17-18).
Jacob at Shechem (vv.18-20): Jacob buys land near Shechem (v.19). Builds an altar: "El-elohe-Israel" — "God, the God of Israel" (v.20). For the first time, Jacob claims God as his God — not just the God of Abraham and Isaac.

Map & Geography

  • Succoth (v.17) — meaning "booths," east of the Jordan. Jacob builds a house and shelters for livestock here — his first stop in the land.
  • Shechem (v.18) — in the central hill country of Canaan, between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim. Jacob buys a parcel of land and builds an altar called "El-elohe-Israel" (God, the God of Israel).
  • Jacob's journey: Peniel → Succoth → Shechem traces a path from the Transjordan across the Jordan and into the heart of Canaan.

Reflection

  • Grace Beyond Expectation (v.4) — After years of dreading this reunion, Jacob is met by a running embrace instead of the vengeance he feared. Where in your life are you bracing for judgment that God might actually meet with grace?
  • Seeing God in Another's Grace (v.10) — Jacob tells Esau that seeing his face felt like seeing the face of God. Has someone's unexpected kindness or forgiveness ever revealed God's grace to you in a way you didn't anticipate?
  • The Contentment of Enough (v.11) — Both brothers independently say they already have enough, refusing to grasp for more from each other. Can you honestly say you have enough, or are you still chasing more than God has already provided?
  • Pacing for the Vulnerable (vv.13-14) — Jacob turns down Esau's offer to travel together, choosing a slower pace so his children and weaker animals aren't driven too hard. Are you willing to slow your own pace for the sake of those who are more fragile than you?
  • A Faith Made Personal (v.20) — At the altar he builds, Jacob names God as his own God, not merely the God his father and grandfather worshiped. Is your faith something you've made personally your own, or is it still borrowed from someone else?
