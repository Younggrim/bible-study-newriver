9Break forth in joy, sing together, O ruins of Jerusalem, for the LORD has comforted His people; He has redeemed Jerusalem.

11Depart, depart, go out from there! Touch no unclean thing; come out from it, purify yourselves, you who carry the vessels of the LORD.

12For you will not leave in a hurry nor flee in haste, for the LORD goes before you, and the God of Israel is your rear guard.

15so He will sprinkle many nations. Kings will shut their mouths because of Him. For they will see what they have not been told, and they will understand what they have not heard.

Summary

Zion is called to wake up, put on strength and beautiful garments, and shake off the dust and chains of captivity, because the unclean will no longer enter her gates (vv. 1-2). Isaiah describes messengers running across the mountains with good news of peace and salvation, declaring that God reigns, and pictures the watchmen of Jerusalem breaking into song together as they see the LORD returning to Zion with their own eyes (vv. 7-8). God calls his people to leave captivity behind, carrying the vessels of the LORD but without haste, since he goes ahead of them and behind them (vv. 11-12). The chapter ends by introducing the Servant, announcing that he will act wisely and be highly exalted, though many will be appalled at him first because his appearance will be marred beyond recognition (vv. 13-15).

Authorship & Background

Author: Isaiah son of Amoz (see Chapter 1 notes for full details).
Classification: Prophetic Salvation Oracle + Beginning of Fourth Servant Song (52:13-15)
Key Themes: Zion's awakening, liberation from captivity, beautiful feet bringing good news, God reigns, departure from Babylon, the Servant exalted yet marred beyond recognition
Historical Context: The chapter calls Zion to wake up — captivity is over, freedom has come. Isaiah 52:1-12 celebrates liberation (from Babylon historically, from sin ultimately). Verse 7 — "beautiful feet" — is quoted by Paul in Romans 10:15 as the gospel proclamation. Then 52:13-15 begins the fourth Servant Song (continuing through 53:12). The tone shifts dramatically: from celebration to horror. The Servant will be "exalted and extolled" (v.13) yet His appearance is "marred more than any man" (v.14). Kings will shut their mouths in astonishment. This is the prelude to Isaiah 53's atonement portrait.
Structure:
  • Awake, Zion! Put on Strength (vv.1-2)
  • Sold for Nothing, Redeemed Without Money (vv.3-6)
  • Beautiful Feet: Good News of Peace (vv.7-8)
  • Break Forth Together: God Has Redeemed Jerusalem (vv.9-10)
  • Depart from Babylon: Be Clean (vv.11-12)
  • Fourth Servant Song Begins: Exalted Yet Marred (vv.13-15)

Map & Geography

  • Jerusalem (v.1, v.2, v.9): Capital of Judah; the holy city where the Temple stood.
  • Zion (v.1, v.2, v.7): The hill on which Jerusalem/the Temple stood; often used poetically for God's dwelling.
  • Egypt (v.4): Land to the southwest; place of Israel's bondage and exodus.

Reflection

  • Wake Up and Put On Strength (v.1) — Zion is commanded to awake, put on strength, and dress in beautiful garments now that captivity is over. Are you still living like a captive in some area where God has already set you free?
  • Redeemed Without Money (v.3) — Israel was sold for nothing and will be redeemed without money — God's rescue was never about a payment He owed. Do you view your salvation as something you earned, or as a gift completely outside your ability to purchase?
  • Beautiful Feet, Good News (v.7) — The messenger who brings good news of peace and salvation is called beautiful — not for eloquence, but for showing up. Who around you needs you to be the one who actually goes and brings the good news today?
  • Singing Together at the Redemption (v.9) — The waste places of Jerusalem are told to break into singing because the Lord has comforted His people and redeemed the city for everyone to see. Do you celebrate God's redemptive work openly, or keep your gratitude private?
  • Depart and Be Clean (v.11) — Those leaving captivity are told to depart, touch nothing unclean, and go out clean, carrying the vessels of the Lord. When God opens a door out of an old way of living, do you leave completely, or try to carry pieces of it with you?
  • Exalted Yet Marred Beyond Recognition (v.14) — The Servant who will be highly exalted is first described as so disfigured that His appearance no longer looks human. Does knowing what the Servant's exaltation cost Him change how lightly or seriously you take the gospel?
