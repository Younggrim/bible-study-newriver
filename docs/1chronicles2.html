1These were the sons of Israel: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun,

2Dan, Joseph, Benjamin, Naphtali, Gad, and Asher.

3The sons of Judah: Er, Onan, and Shelah. These three were born to him by Bath-shua the Canaanite. Er, Judah’s firstborn, was wicked in the sight of the LORD. So the LORD put him to death.

4Tamar, Judah’s daughter-in-law, bore to him Perez and Zerah. Judah had five sons in all.

5The sons of Perez: Hezron and Hamul.

6The sons of Zerah: Zimri, Ethan, Heman, Calcol, and Dara—five in all.

7The son of Carmi: Achar, who brought trouble upon Israel by violating the ban on devoted things.

8The son of Ethan: Azariah.

9The sons who were born to Hezron: Jerahmeel, Ram, and Caleb.

10Ram was the father of Amminadab, and Amminadab was the father of Nahshon, a leader of the descendants of Judah.

11Nahshon was the father of Salmon, and Salmon was the father of Boaz.

12Boaz was the father of Obed, and Obed was the father of Jesse.

13Jesse was the father of Eliab his firstborn; Abinadab was born second, Shimea third,

14Nethanel fourth, Raddai fifth,

15Ozem sixth, and David seventh.

16Their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail. And the three sons of Zeruiah were Abishai, Joab, and Asahel.

17Abigail was the mother of Amasa, whose father was Jether the Ishmaelite.

18Caleb son of Hezron had children by his wife Azubah and by Jerioth. These were the sons of Azubah: Jesher, Shobab, and Ardon.

19When Azubah died, Caleb married Ephrath, who bore to him Hur.

20Hur was the father of Uri, and Uri was the father of Bezalel.

21Later, Hezron slept with the daughter of Machir the father of Gilead. He had married her when he was sixty years old, and she bore to him Segub.

22Segub was the father of Jair, who had twenty-three cities in the land of Gilead.

23But Geshur and Aram captured Havvoth-jair, along with Kenath and its sixty surrounding villages. All these were descendants of Machir the father of Gilead.

24After Hezron died in Caleb-ephrathah, his wife Abijah bore to him Ashhur the father of Tekoa.

25The sons of Jerahmeel the firstborn of Hezron: Ram his firstborn, Bunah, Oren, Ozem, and Ahijah.

26Jerahmeel had another wife named Atarah, who was the mother of Onam.

27The sons of Ram the firstborn of Jerahmeel: Maaz, Jamin, and Eker.

28The sons of Onam: Shammai and Jada. The sons of Shammai: Nadab and Abishur.

29Abishur’s wife was named Abihail, and she bore to him Ahban and Molid.

30The sons of Nadab: Seled and Appaim. Seled died without children.

31The son of Appaim: Ishi. The son of Ishi: Sheshan. The son of Sheshan: Ahlai.

32The sons of Jada the brother of Shammai: Jether and Jonathan. Jether died without children.

33The sons of Jonathan: Peleth and Zaza. These were the descendants of Jerahmeel.

34Sheshan had no sons, but only daughters. He also had an Egyptian servant named Jarha.

35Sheshan gave his daughter in marriage to his servant Jarha, and she bore to him Attai.

36Attai was the father of Nathan, Nathan was the father of Zabad,

37Zabad was the father of Ephlal, Ephlal was the father of Obed,

38Obed was the father of Jehu, Jehu was the father of Azariah,

39Azariah was the father of Helez, Helez was the father of Elasah,

40Elasah was the father of Sismai, Sismai was the father of Shallum,

41Shallum was the father of Jekamiah, and Jekamiah was the father of Elishama.

42The sons of Caleb the brother of Jerahmeel: Mesha his firstborn, who was the father of Ziph, and Mareshah his second son, who was the father of Hebron.

43The sons of Hebron: Korah, Tappuah, Rekem, and Shema.

44Shema was the father of Raham the father of Jorkeam, and Rekem was the father of Shammai.

45The son of Shammai was Maon, and Maon was the father of Beth-zur.

46Caleb’s concubine Ephah was the mother of Haran, Moza, and Gazez. Haran was the father of Gazez.

47The sons of Jahdai: Regem, Jotham, Geshan, Pelet, Ephah, and Shaaph.

48Caleb’s concubine Maacah was the mother of Sheber and Tirhanah.

49She was also the mother of Shaaph father of Madmannah, and of Sheva father of Machbenah and Gibea. Caleb’s daughter was Acsah.

50These were the descendants of Caleb. The sons of Hur the firstborn of Ephrathah: Shobal the father of Kiriath-jearim,

51Salma the father of Bethlehem, and Hareph the father of Beth-gader.

52These were the descendants of Shobal the father of Kiriath-jearim: Haroeh, half the Manahathites,

53and the clans of Kiriath-jearim—the Ithrites, Puthites, Shumathites, and Mishraites. From these descended the Zorathites and Eshtaolites.

54The descendants of Salma: Bethlehem, the Netophathites, Atroth-beth-joab, half the Manahathites, the Zorites,

55and the clans of the scribes who lived at Jabez—the Tirathites, Shimeathites, and Sucathites. These are the Kenites who came from Hammath, the father of the house of Rechab.

14Nethaneel the fourth, Raddai the fifth,

15Ozem the sixth, David the seventh:

16Whose sisters were Zeruiah, and Abigail. And the sons of Zeruiah; Abishai, and Joab, and Asahel, three.

17And Abigail bare Amasa: and the father of Amasa was Jether the Ishmeelite.

18And Caleb the son of Hezron begat children of Azubah his wife, and of Jerioth: her sons are these; Jesher, and Shobab, and Ardon.

19And when Azubah was dead, Caleb took unto him Ephrath, which bare him Hur.

20And Hur begat Uri, and Uri begat Bezaleel.

21And afterward Hezron went in to the daughter of Machir the father of Gilead, whom he married when he was threescore years old; and she bare him Segub.

22And Segub begat Jair, who had three and twenty cities in the land of Gilead.

23And he took Geshur, and Aram, with the towns of Jair, from them, with Kenath, and the towns thereof, even threescore cities. All these belonged to the sons of Machir the father of Gilead.

24And after that Hezron was dead in Caleb-ephratah, then Abiah Hezron’s wife bare him Ashur the father of Tekoa.

25And the sons of Jerahmeel the firstborn of Hezron were, Ram the firstborn, and Bunah, and Oren, and Ozem, and Ahijah.

26Jerahmeel had also another wife, whose name was Atarah; she was the mother of Onam.

27And the sons of Ram the firstborn of Jerahmeel were, Maaz, and Jamin, and Eker.

28And the sons of Onam were, Shammai, and Jada. And the sons of Shammai; Nadab, and Abishur.

29And the name of the wife of Abishur was Abihail, and she bare him Ahban, and Molid.

30And the sons of Nadab; Seled, and Appaim: but Seled died without children.

31And the sons of Appaim; Ishi. And the sons of Ishi; Sheshan. And the children of Sheshan; Ahlai.

32And the sons of Jada the brother of Shammai; Jether and Jonathan: and Jether died without children.

33And the sons of Jonathan; Peleth, and Zaza. These were the sons of Jerahmeel.

34Now Sheshan had no sons, but daughters. And Sheshan had a servant, an Egyptian, whose name was Jarha.

35And Sheshan gave his daughter to Jarha his servant to wife; and she bare him Attai.

36And Attai begat Nathan, and Nathan begat Zabad,

37And Zabad begat Ephlal, and Ephlal begat Obed,

38And Obed begat Jehu, and Jehu begat Azariah,

39And Azariah begat Helez, and Helez begat Eleasah,

40And Eleasah begat Sisamai, and Sisamai begat Shallum,

41And Shallum begat Jekamiah, and Jekamiah begat Elishama.

42Now the sons of Caleb the brother of Jerahmeel were, Mesha his firstborn, which was the father of Ziph; and the sons of Mareshah the father of Hebron.

43And the sons of Hebron; Korah, and Tappuah, and Rekem, and Shema.

44And Shema begat Raham, the father of Jorkoam: and Rekem begat Shammai.

45And the son of Shammai was Maon: and Maon was the father of Beth-zur.

46And Ephah, Caleb’s concubine, bare Haran, and Moza, and Gazez: and Haran begat Gazez.

47And the sons of Jahdai; Regem, and Jotham, and Gesham, and Pelet, and Ephah, and Shaaph.

48Maachah, Caleb’s concubine, bare Sheber, and Tirhanah.

49She bare also Shaaph the father of Madmannah, Sheva the father of Machbenah, and the father of Gibea: and the daughter of Caleb was Achsa.

50These were the sons of Caleb the son of Hur, the firstborn of Ephratah; Shobal the father of Kirjath-jearim,

51Salma the father of Bethlehem, Hareph the father of Beth-gader.

52And Shobal the father of Kirjath-jearim had sons; Haroeh, and half of the Manahethites.

53And the families of Kirjath-jearim; the Ithrites, and the Puhites, and the Shumathites, and the Mishraites; of them came the Zareathites, and the Eshtaulites.

54The sons of Salma; Bethlehem, and the Netophathites, Ataroth, the house of Joab, and half of the Manahethites, the Zorites.

55And the families of the scribes which dwelt at Jabez; the Tirathites, the Shimeathites, and Suchathites. These are the Kenites that came of Hemath, the father of the house of Rechab.

Summary

The chapter begins by naming the twelve sons of Israel (vv. 1-2), then focuses on Judah's line: Er's wickedness and death, and Tamar's sons Perez and Zerah (vv. 3-4). Perez's descendants are traced through Hezron and Ram down to Jesse and his sons, ending with David as the seventh and youngest, alongside his sisters Zeruiah and Abigail (vv. 5-17). The genealogy then follows Caleb and Hezron's other branches, including Machir of Gilead (vv. 18-24) and the line of Jerahmeel (vv. 25-41), and closes with further descendants of Caleb linked to towns such as Kiriath-jearim and Bethlehem, along with the Kenite clan of Rechab (vv. 42-55). The chapter's real interest is establishing David's ancestry within Judah's tribe.

Authorship & Background

Author: Traditionally attributed to Ezra the scribe. Originally one book with 2 Chronicles. Written post-exile (approximately 450-400 BC) for the returned remnant. The Chronicler retells Israel's history from a priestly/worship perspective, emphasizing the Davidic covenant, Temple worship, and God's faithfulness.
Historical Context: Chapter 2 begins the genealogy of Judah — and this is no accident. The Chronicler skips over Reuben (the firstborn), Simeon, and Levi to begin with Judah because Judah is the royal tribe, the tribe of David, and the tribe from which Messiah would come (Genesis 49:10). This entire section (chapters 2-4) gives Judah the most extensive treatment of any tribe, reflecting the Chronicler's theological priority: the Davidic line is central to God's purposes.
Classification: Historical Narrative — Genealogy
Key Themes: Eleven tribes named in one sentence and set aside for six chapters so Judah can be taken up immediately, Er's wickedness and Tamar's deception recorded without being smoothed over in the first eight verses, nine verses carrying the entire line the whole book depends on, from Ram to Jesse to David, David's own military commanders identified as his nephews through a sister the text bothers to name, and a closing list that turns names into towns rather than persons
The Sons of Israel, and the Sons of Judah (vv.1-8): The twelve sons are listed in one sentence and then eleven of them are set aside for six chapters. Judah is taken up immediately, and the Chronicler does not tidy the record: Er, Onan and Shelah are named with the note that Er was evil in the sight of the LORD, and Perez and Zerah are recorded as born to Tamar his daughter-in-law. Then Achan appears, called Achar here, the troubler of Israel, who transgressed in the thing accursed. Two of the first eight verses of Judah's line record disgrace.
From Ram to David (vv.9-17): The line the whole book depends on, and it is nine verses long: Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salma, Boaz, Obed, Jesse. Then the sons of Jesse, and the Chronicler gives seven where 1 Samuel implies eight, with David seventh. His sisters Zeruiah and Abigail are named, which matters because Joab, Abishai and Asahel are then identified as her sons rather than as David's officers. The men who run the army for the next twenty chapters are the king's nephews.
The Sons of Caleb, and Hezron's Line (vv.18-24): The register turns aside from the royal line to follow Caleb, and the details it keeps are domestic: wives named, a wife who died, a second marriage at sixty years old. Hur and Uri and Bezalel are in this list, and Bezalel is the craftsman who built the tabernacle in Exodus. The Chronicler mentions no craft and no tabernacle. He is establishing that the man belonged to Judah.
The Sons of Jerahmeel (vv.25-41): Seventeen verses on a branch of Judah that produces nobody famous. The interest is territorial rather than biographical, because these are the families who held land in the Negev around Jerahmeel and Jerahmeelite territory that 1 Samuel places on Judah's southern edge. Two details survive the list: Sheshan had no sons but daughters, and gave one to an Egyptian servant, which the register records without comment.
Caleb's Families and the Towns (vv.42-55): The last section of the chapter turns names into places. The sons of Caleb are given as fathers of towns, the father of Ziph, the father of Hebron, the father of Bethlehem, which is how a genealogy records settlement rather than parentage. Kiriath-jearim, Bethlehem and Netophah are all placed here. The closing verse names the families of the scribes which dwelt at Jabez, and adds that they were Kenites, an outside people written into Judah's record.

  • Chapters 1-9 (genealogies) contain tribal allotments but minimal narrative geography.

⚑ Report an issue or ideaⓘ How this site is made

Setting: Chronicles was written after the exile, around 450–400 BC, for Jews rebuilding life in a small Persian province around Jerusalem. Chapter 1 traced the human race from Adam to Esau and set the side branches aside. Now the Chronicler turns to Israel itself. He lists the twelve sons of Jacob and then goes straight to Judah, ahead of the firstborn Reuben, because Judah is the tribe of David and the tribe most of his readers belonged to. The genealogy of Judah runs from 2:3 to 4:23, longer than any other tribe's. This chapter follows Judah's family down to David and then fans out into the clans and towns of Judah. It is a list of names, but it also gives its readers roots. Many of the town names here (Bethlehem, Hebron, Tekoa, Kiriath-jearim, Netophah) were places the returned exiles were living in again.

A Genealogy of David (vv1–55)

  • "These are the sons of Israel" (vv1–2). The twelve sons, roughly in birth order with Leah's sons first, much as in Genesis 35:23–26. All twelve are named. Even though the northern tribes had been scattered by Assyria three centuries earlier, the Chronicler keeps all Israel in view. His book will keep insisting that God's people means all twelve tribes, not just Judah.
  • "Bath-shua the Canaanite" (v3). The name means "daughter of Shua" (Gen 38:2). Judah married a Canaanite, against the pattern of Abraham and Isaac (Gen 24:3; 28:1). "Er, Judah's firstborn, was evil in the sight of the LORD, and he put him to death." The Chronicler does not skip the hard parts. This is the first death the book records, and it is a judgment.
  • "His daughter-in-law Tamar also bore him Perez and Zerah" (v4). The whole messy story of Genesis 38 sits behind this line. Tamar was Er's widow, and when Judah failed to give her his son Shelah, she disguised herself and conceived by Judah himself. Judah admitted, "She is more righteous than I" (Gen 38:26). From that union came Perez, ancestor of David. Matthew names Tamar in the genealogy of Jesus (Matt 1:3), the first of four women whose stories were irregular.
  • The sons of Zerah (vv6–8). Ethan, Heman, Calcol and Dara appear as famous wise men in 1 Kings 4:31, where Solomon was wiser than they ("Darda" there). Psalms 88 and 89 carry the names of Heman and Ethan "the Ezrahite," which probably means "descendant of Zerah." The Levite musicians named Heman and Ethan in 1 Chronicles 6 and 15 may be different men. Scholars disagree on how the names fit together.
  • "Achan, the troubler of Israel" (v7). The Hebrew text spells his name "Achar," which means "trouble." In Joshua he is Achan, who stole from the plunder of Jericho that was "devoted" to God, and Israel lost at Ai because of it. He and his family were stoned in the Valley of Achor, "Trouble Valley" (Josh 7:24–26). The Chronicler's spelling turns his name into his record. "Who broke faith" is maal, to act unfaithfully toward God. It is one of the key words of Chronicles. It explains why Saul died (10:13) and why Judah went into exile (9:1). Here it appears at the very start of Judah's history, a warning set inside the honor roll.
  • "Jerahmeel, Ram, and Chelubai" (v9). The three sons of Hezron give the outline for the rest of the chapter. Ram's line leads to David (vv10–17). Chelubai is another form of Caleb (vv18–24, 42–55). Jerahmeel's line comes in vv25–41.
  • The line from Ram to Jesse (vv10–12). This is the same line that ends the book of Ruth (Ruth 4:18–22). Nahshon, "prince of the sons of Judah" (v10), led Judah in the wilderness (Num 1:7; 2:3), and his sister married Aaron (Exod 6:23). Salmon married Rahab of Jericho (Matt 1:5). Boaz married Ruth the Moabite, and their son Obed was David's grandfather. Two foreign women, a Canaanite and a Moabite, stand in the background of these five names.
  • "David the seventh" (v15). 1 Samuel 16:10–11 and 17:12 say Jesse had eight sons. Chronicles lists seven, with David last. The usual explanations are that one son died young without children (JFB's view), or that the Chronicler wants David in the seventh place, the place of completeness. A brother named Elihu appears in 27:18, though that may be another name for Eliab. Samuel names only the first three brothers, so Chronicles is the only source for Nethanel, Raddai and Ozem.
  • "Their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail" (vv16–17). Only here do we learn that Joab, Abishai and Asahel were David's nephews through his sister. That explains much of 1 and 2 Samuel: these rough, loyal, dangerous men were family. Amasa, Absalom's general whom Joab murdered (2 Sam 20:10), was also David's nephew, through Abigail. 2 Samuel 17:25 calls Abigail "daughter of Nahash," which suggests she and Zeruiah were half-sisters of David, perhaps through their mother. "Jether the Ishmaelite" is "Ithra the Israelite" in the Hebrew of 2 Samuel 17:25. "Ishmaelite" is the more surprising reading and probably the original one. A foreigner married into David's family.
  • Caleb son of Hezron (vv18–20). This Caleb lived generations before the famous spy Caleb son of Jephunneh (Num 13:6), and he is a different man. His grandson's grandson was Bezalel, the craftsman God filled with his Spirit to build the tabernacle (Exod 31:1–5). The Chronicler cares about worship, so the builder of the first sanctuary gets a mention. Solomon, the builder of the temple, will be his counterpart.
  • Jair's twenty-three cities (vv21–23). Hezron married a woman of Manasseh, and their descendant Jair won towns in Gilead, east of the Jordan, called Havvoth-jair, "the tent villages of Jair" (Num 32:41; Deut 3:14). So a family of Judah was counted with Manasseh through its mother's side. Tribe lines were not as rigid as they look. "Geshur and Aram took from them" refers to a later loss of these towns to the Aramean kingdoms north and east of Gilead. When it happened is not said.
  • "Caleb went in to Ephrathah, the wife of Hezron his father" (v24). The Hebrew of this verse is hard and reads literally "in Caleb-ephrathah." The ESV follows the Greek text, which makes Ephrathah Hezron's widow, whom Caleb then married. "Ashhur, the father of Tekoa" means the founder or leading man of the town of Tekoa, later the home of the prophet Amos (Amos 1:1). Throughout these lists "father of" a place usually means its founder or chief, as JFB notes.
  • The line of Jerahmeel (vv25–33). The Jerahmeelites lived in the Negeb, the dry south of Judah. David, while living among the Philistines, claimed to raid the "Negeb of the Jerahmeelites" and later sent them gifts from his plunder (1 Sam 27:10; 30:29). The list is a careful record of a clan that might otherwise be forgotten. "Seled died childless" and "Jether died childless" (vv30, 32) are small lines of sadness. In a culture where a man lived on through his children, these were family lines that ended.
  • Sheshan and Jarha (vv34–41). "Sheshan had no sons, only daughters." (v31 names a son, Ahlai, which may be a daughter's name, or a son who died young. The text does not explain.) So Sheshan gave a daughter in marriage to Jarha, his Egyptian slave. A slave from the land of Israel's bondage became the ancestor of a thirteen-generation line in Judah. The law allowed a family without sons to keep its inheritance through daughters (Num 27:1–11), and this marriage did the same. JFB thinks Jarha was first freed and adopted into Judah.
  • Caleb's descendants and towns (vv42–50a). Many names here are towns in the hill country of Judah, south of Bethlehem: Ziph (where David hid from Saul, 1 Sam 23:14), Mareshah, Hebron (David's first capital), Tappuah, Maon (home of Nabal, 1 Sam 25:2), Beth-zur, Madmannah. The list is family tree and map at once. It tells who first settled each town.
  • "The daughter of Caleb was Achsah" (v49). Achsah was the daughter of the later Caleb, son of Jephunneh, who gave her to Othniel, Israel's first judge, and gave her springs of water at her request (Josh 15:16–19; Judg 1:12–15). Her name seems to have been added here because both Calebs belong to the same clan area. How exactly the two Calebs connect is not clear, and commentators admit it.
  • The sons of Hur (vv50b–55). Hur's family founded Kiriath-jearim, where the ark of the covenant stayed for twenty years before David brought it to Jerusalem (1 Sam 7:1–2; 1 Chr 13:5–6), and Bethlehem (v51), David's hometown and the birthplace of Jesus (Mic 5:2). The Zorathites and the Eshtaolites (v53) came from Zorah and Eshtaol, Samson's home ground (Judg 13:2, 25). The Netophathites (v54) produced two of David's mighty men (2 Sam 23:28–29).
  • "The clans also of the scribes who lived at Jabez… the Kenites" (v55). The Kenites were a non-Israelite clan, related to Moses' father-in-law, who came into the land with Judah (Judg 1:16). Jael, who killed Sisera, was the wife of a Kenite (Judg 4:17). "The house of Rechab" became famous in Jeremiah's day for refusing wine and keeping a nomadic life out of loyalty to their ancestor's command (Jer 35). Jeremiah held them up as an example of faithfulness for Judah. Like Jarha the Egyptian and Jether the Ishmaelite, these outsiders are recorded as part of Judah.
  • Commentators: Guzik observes that the line that leads to David runs through people with dark stories, Er, Judah and Tamar, and Achan, so that God's purposes clearly move forward through flawed people, not because of them. He notes that "unfaithfulness" is the Chronicler's favorite explanation for disaster, and that Zeruiah and Abigail's place in the family is known only from this list. JFB reads "Achar" as deliberate: "Achar means 'troubler.'" On David as seventh of eight sons, JFB infers that one "had died at an early age, without leaving issue." JFB thinks Jarha was adopted "into the tribe of Judah," and calls the English of the Caleb verses "confused," suggesting a copying error in v50. Henry calls the chapter "the register of the children of Israel," a people meant "to dwell alone, and not be reckoned among the nations," and adds that now "all are welcome to his salvation who come to him." The Tyndale notes agree that Caleb son of Hezron is a different man from Caleb son of Jephunneh.

Pointing to Christ

This chapter is the backbone of the family tree Matthew opens his Gospel with: Judah, Perez and Zerah by Tamar, Hezron, Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse and David (Matt 1:3–6). The same list that keeps the record of Achan the troubler, a Canaanite wife, an Egyptian slave and an Ishmaelite in-law also leads straight to Bethlehem. The line of David's ancestors was full of sin and of outsiders, and God brought the Son of David through it anyway. He came for exactly such people.

Commentary

  • Enduring Word (David Guzik): enduringword.com Guzik highlights that the Chronicler's emphasis on Judah reflects the post-exilic reality: the returned community was primarily composed of Judah and Benjamin. The detailed treatment of Judah's clans served to organize the resettlement and establish legitimate land claims. He also notes the theological significance of David appearing as the seventh son.
  • Jamieson-Fausset-Brown (1871): blueletterbible.org A critical and explanatory commentary on the whole Bible, more concise than Matthew Henry and closer to the Hebrew and Greek, with attention to historical and geographical detail. Public domain.

Reflection

  • Grace Woven Through Scandal (v.4) — The messianic line passes directly through Judah's sin with Tamar and the wickedness of his sons. Do you believe God can still write a redemptive story through the shameful chapters of your own past?
  • One Man's Sin, A Whole Nation's Trouble (v.7) — Achan is remembered here for a single act of unfaithfulness that brought judgment on all Israel. What "private" compromise in your life might actually be affecting the people around you more than you realize?
  • God's Plan Carried by Unknown Generations (vv.10-15) — Ten largely obscure generations stand between Perez and David, each one quietly preserving the line God would use. Are you willing to be faithful in an unremarkable season without knowing what it's building toward?
  • Grace Makes Room for Outsiders (vv.34-35) — Sheshan gives his daughter in marriage to Jarha, his Egyptian servant, folding an outsider into Judah's family line. Where might God be asking you to widen your circle instead of guarding it?
